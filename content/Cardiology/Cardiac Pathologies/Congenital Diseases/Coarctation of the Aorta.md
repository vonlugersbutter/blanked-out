# Definition
Congenital heart defect. Segment of the aorta is to narrow, so less oxygenated blood getting out, left ventricle pumps harder. 
# Etiology
- Reduced antegrade blood into the left side of the heart, improper growth of the aorta
- Could also be that the ectopic muscular ductus arteriosus extends to aorta, constricts when it's supposed to 
- LV higher afterload
- Head and upper extremities are okay because coarctation usually happens after that point 
- Less flow to lower extremities
- LV hypertrophy, dilatation of collateral blood vessels from intercostal arteries to help give lower blood flow
	- Larger vessels can erode ribs

# Investigations
## Physical exam
- Femoral pulses weak and delayed
- Location of coarctation can be determined by where there's high blood pressure 
- Mid-systolic ejection murmur through coarctation 
- Could have continuous murmurs over chest 
## CXR
- Notching of the ribs 
- Indented aorta
## EKG
- LV hypertrophy
## Echo
- Check pressure gradient 
# Clinical features
- Hypertension
- Heart failure
- Differential cyanosis of upper and lower halves if ductus fails to constrict 
- Claudication 
- Upper extremity hypertension later in life 
# Treatment 
- Prostaglandin infusion to keep the duct open, to maintain some kind of blood flow to the descending aorta 
- Balloon dilation
- Stent
