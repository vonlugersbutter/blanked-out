# Adrenal cortex
## Cortex zones
- Zona glomerulosa = outer layer producing mineralocorticoids (aldosterone)
- Zona fasciculata = middle layer producing glucocorticoids (cortisol)
- Zona reticularis = inner layer producing androgens
- Remember this using **salt, sugar, sex**! And GFR
- Key enzymes = includes desmolase, 17-alpha hydroxylase, 3-beta hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase, 21-alpha hydroxylase, and 11-beta hydroxylase
- Ketoconazole = an antifungal that acts as a potent inhibitor of 17,20 lyase and other enzymes, blocking cortisol and androgen synthesis
## Basic steroidogenesis
- Cholesterol is the primary precursor, mostly derived from circulating LDL
- StAR protein = steroidogenic acute regulatory protein = facilitates the rate-limiting step of transporting cholesterol into the inner mitochondrial membrane
- Conversion involves mitochondrial (Type I) and microsomal (Type II) cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes, such as CYP11A1 (side-chain cleavage) and CYP21A2 (21-hydroxylase)
- P450scc (CYP11A gene) = located in the mitochondria, it is responsible for the initial step of cholesterol side-chain cleavage
- P450c11 (CYP11B1 gene) = mediates 11β-hydroxylation in the fasciculata and reticularis zones, converting 11-deoxycortisol to cortisol
- P450aldo (CYP11B2 gene) = aka aldosterone synthase, it operates in the zona glomerulosa to produce aldosterone
- P450c17 (CYP17 gene) = found in the ER, it mediates both 17α-hydroxylase and 17,20-lyase activity, crucial for cortisol and androgen production
## Mineralocorticoids (aldosterone)
- Regulates mineral balance via the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, not really as controlled by ACTH
- Increases sodium and water resorption while promoting potassium and hydrogen ion excretion in the kidney's collecting duct (principal and intercalated cells)
- See [Normal Renal Anatomy and Physiology](Nephrology/Normal%20Renal%20Anatomy%20and%20Physiology.md)
## Glucocorticoids (cortisol)
- Synthesis = controlled by the pituitary-adrenal axis (hypothalamus → CRH; anterior pituitary → ACTH; adrenal → cortisol)
	- ACTH acts on adrenal gland through cAMP/PKA pathway 
- Circadian rhythm = serum levels are highest in the early morning (~6 am) and lowest shortly after sleep onset
- Over 90% is bound to cortisol binding globulin (CBG) in the plasma
- Maintains blood pressure by increasing vascular sensitivity to catecholamines, suppresses the immune system (inactivates NF-KB, reduces t and b cell levels), increases serum glucose through gluconeogenesis, and promotes lipolysis and fat deposition
- Cortisol is primarily metabolized in the liver and kidney into tetrahydro-metabolites (THF, THE) for urinary excretion
- Glucocorticoids are diabetogenic; they stimulate gluconeogenesis and glycogen deposition in the liver while inhibiting glucose uptake in peripheral tissues
- Promote visceral obesity, protein catabolism (muscle wasting), and skin thinning
- Inhibit osteoblasts, leading to osteoporosis and, in some cases, osteonecrosis of the femoral head
- Influence mood and cognition; excess can cause psychosis or depression
- All of these symptoms can be observed in [Cushing's Syndrome](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Cushing's%20Syndrome.md)

## Androgens
- Primarily dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) and androstenedione
- They contribute significantly to androgen production in females (~50%) but only a small amount in males
- [Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Diabetes/Congenital%20Adrenal%20Hyperplasia.md)
- Production is stimulated by ACTH, like cortisol

# Adrenal medulla
- Occupies 10% of the adrenal gland volume and is composed of chromaffin cells (pheochromocytes)
- Sympathogonia serve as the common progenitor cells for both the sympathetic nervous system and the adrenal medulla
	- From sympathogonia, the lineage splits into two primary paths =
		- Neuroblast line, which progresses through the ganglioneuroblast stage to become mature sympathetic ganglion cells; tumours associated with this line include neuroblastomas and ganglioneuromas
		- Pheochromoblast line, which matures into pheochromocytes (chromaffin cells); these cells are the origin of [Pheochromocytoma](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Pheochromocytoma.md)
- Catecholamines are synthesized from tyrosine → rate-limiting step is the conversion of tyrosine to DOPA by tyrosine hydroxylase
	- DOPA decarboxylase then converts DOPA into dopamine
	- Dopamine-β-hydroxylase (DBH) converts dopamine into norepinephrine within secretory vesicles
	- Phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase (PNMT) converts norepinephrine into epinephrine; this enzyme is primarily found in the adrenal medulla

- Epinephrine = synthesized and stored in the adrenal medulla; its production is induced by cortisol
- Norepinephrine = stored in the medulla and peripheral sympathetic nerves
- Dopamine = precursor to norepinephrine, acting primarily as a neurotransmitter
- Catecholamines are stored in neurosecretory granules alongside ATP, chromogranins (specifically chromogranin A), and peptides
- Secretion is triggered by the entry of calcium into the nerve terminal or adrenal medullary cell, facilitating the exocytosis of vesicle contents
### Receptor action
- Catecholamines act through GPCRs
	- $\alpha_1$ receptors = vasoconstriction and increase blood pressure
		- Phospholipase C, IP3, DAG, ↑ Ca++
	- $\alpha_2$ receptors = presynaptic nerve endings; they inhibit norepinephrine release when activated
		- cAMP ↑ 
	- $\beta_1$ receptors = mediate cardiac effects (increased heart rate and contractility) and renin secretion → you have one heart
		- cAMP ↑ → PKA through G<sub>s</sub> protein
	- $\beta_2$ receptors = mediate bronchial and vascular smooth muscle relaxation → you have two lungs
		- cAMP ↑ → PKA through G<sub>s</sub> protein
	- Alpha goes with vaso**c**onstriction, beta goes with vaso**d**ilation
### Reuptake and metabolism
- Norepinephrine transporter (NET) is responsible for the reuptake of norepinephrine from the synaptic cleft back into the nerve terminal
- This is inhibited by substances like cocaine and tricyclic antidepressants, leading to increased synaptic catecholamine levels
- Two primary enzymes degrade catecholamines = monoamine oxidase (MAO) and catecholamine-o-methyltransferase (COMT)
- Norepinephrine is metabolized into normetanephrine, and epinephrine into metanephrine
- The final major metabolic byproduct excreted in the urine is 3-methoxy-4-hydroxymandelic acid, commonly known as vanillylmandelic acid (VMA)
- Intermediate metabolites include dihydroxyphenylglycol (DHPG) and MHPG

## Paragangliomas
- Catecholamine-secreting paragangliomas arise from extra-adrenal sympathetic ganglia
# Note on adrenal tumors
- Incidentalomas = adrenal masses found on imaging for other reasons; 10% prevalence with age
	- Evaluation must check for hormonal function and malignancy (size > 4 cm is a risk)
- Carcinomas = rare and highly aggressive; often secretes multiple steroids
	- Treatment is surgery plus mitotane
	- Mitotane = steroidogenesis inhibitor and cytostatic antineoplastic medication which is used in the treatment of adrenocortical carcinoma and [Cushing's Syndrome](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Cushing's%20Syndrome.md)
		- Acts as an inhibitor of cholesterol side-chain cleavage enzyme (P450scc, CYP11A1), and also of 11β-hydroxylase (CYP11B1), 18-hydroxylase (aldosterone synthase, CYP11B2), and 3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (3β-HSD) to a lesser extent
