# Definition
Narrowed mitral valve. 
## Classifications
![](00%20media/{B664D596-6B8F-460D-A6B1-9C5D6C607D82}.png)
# Etiology
Usually caused by prior [Rheumatic Fever](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Rheumatic%20Fever.md). Could also be calcification of mitral annulus, infective endocarditis, or congenital stenosis. **LA pressure is going to increase** due to barrier in getting the blood out, so we would see left atrial enlargement. LA dilatation stretches fibers, could result in [Atrial Fibrillation (AF)](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Arrhythmias/Atrial%20Fibrillation%20(AF).md). This is going to shorten diastole, reduce ventricular filling time, further increase LA pressure in a positive feedback loop. 

The high LA pressure, in turn, would increase pulmonary and right heart pressures. That might cause dyspnea and symptoms of heart failure as fluid backs up in the pulmonary vasculature. Could also see hemoptysis because of ruptured bronchial vein. There is going to be passive pulmonary hypertension, because there is just fluid backing up there. But there is also going to be reactive pulmonary hypertension, involving medial hypertrophy and intimal fibrosis of the pulmonary arterioles. This is to protect the arteries against too much volume and pressure, but then puts a lot of pressure on the right heart, could lead to right heart failure. 

High LA volume and stagnation, with atrial fibrillation, makes it more likely for a thrombus to develop, which can then go to the brain to cause stroke or other organs. MS patients with [Atrial Fibrillation (AF)](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Arrhythmias/Atrial%20Fibrillation%20(AF).md) need to be on chronic anticoagulants. 

Valvular inflammation, which is going to lead to fibrous thickening and calcification of the valves, fusion of commissures (leaflet borders), thickening and shortening of chordae tendineae. There will be an abnormal pressure gradient between LA and LV, which will lead to abnormal heart sounds. An opening snap, and a decrescendo murmur.

## Summary
Mitral stenosis → ↓ LV EDV → ↓ SV → right heart failure → pulmonary edema → pulmonary hypertension → backward heart failure, RV hypertrophy

# Investigations
## Physical exam
- Mitral facies = a distinctive facial appearance characterized by plethoric (rosy) cheeks, often with bluish patches, resulting from chronic mitral stenosis
	- Widespread peripheral vasoconstriction and localized vasodilation in the cheeks and lips
	- Low cardiac output state with chronic hypoxemia and elevated pulmonary hypertension
	- Malar flush is seen, because of vasodilation (vascular stasis) in the malar area
## Auscultation
- Right ventricular heave 
- Loud S1 initially → high pressure gradient between LA and LV keeps whatever does move in the valve wide open
	- As it calcifies or progressively thickens, it becomes less mobile and this goes away
- There is also an opening snap (OS) after S2, sudden tensing of chordae tendineae and stenotic leaflets from when it opens → the more severe the LA pressure, the earlier the valve is forced to open in diastole 
- Followed by diastolic rumble, low-frequency decrescendo → turbulent flow across the stenotic valve 
	- Duration is proportional to severity 
	- Gets louder at the end due to presystolic accentuation, because now the LA is really pushing on it
## EKG
- Left atrial enlargement, RV hypertrophy, might have A fib
- Could also be normal 
## CXR
- Left atrial enlargement, pulmonary vascular redistribution, interstitial edema, Kerley B lines
## Echocardiogram
- Thickened mitral leaflet, abnormal fusion, restricted separation 
- Left atrial enlargement, detection of intra-atrial thrombi 
- Quantify degree of stenosis
	- 4 to 6 cm2 is normal
	- Severe is less than 1.5
	- Very severe is less than 1 
- If echo shows findings less severe than history and examination → exercise test or cardiac catheterization
## Laboratory testing
- BNP or NT-proBNP = ↑ 
- CBC = might be underlying [Infective Endocarditis](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Valvular%20Diseases/Infective%20Endocarditis.md), ↑ WBC
- BMP = renal impairment
- Congestive hepatopathy
- CRP → ongoing inflammation in [Rheumatic Fever](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Rheumatic%20Fever.md) 
# Clinical features
- Dyspnea, increasingly happening at rest
- Reduced exercise capacity
- Pulmonary congestion → orthopnea (when lying down, there is redistribution of blood volume from the lower extremities and splanchnic beds to the lungs) and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (a sudden, nighttime episode of severe shortness of breath that awakens you from sleep, often 1-2 hours after falling asleep → caused by fluid buildup in the lungs)
- Compression of recurrent laryngeal nerve by enlarged pulmonary artery or LA → hoarseness (Ortner syndrome)
- Dysphagia 
- Palpitations 
- Embolic issues as the thromboembolisms develop 
- Because the posterior mediastinal portion of the esophagus lies just behind the left atrium, it is at risk of being compressed and/or displaced by an enlarged left atrium. Left atrial enlargement due to increased left atrial pressure can develop in patients with chronic mitral stenosis, although this occurs very rarely
