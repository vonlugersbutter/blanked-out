# Definition
- Plasma sodium below 135 mEq/L 
- Very common electrolyte disorder 
- Water retention leading to an excess of water in relation to solute 
# Etiology
- Circulation volume depletion
	- GI losses
	- Renal losses
	- CHF
		- CO ↓ → AII, NE, ADH ↑ → ↑ water retention, renal vasoconstriction, ↓ renal blood flow 
		- Increased thirst 
	- Hepatic cirrhosis
- SIADH
	- Any neuropsychiatric disorder without narcotic use
	- Drugs
	- Tumors
- Advanced renal failure
- Hormones
	- Hypothyroidism
	- Cortisol deficiency
	- Pregnancy
- Primary polydipsia
- Reset osmostat 
	- Similar to normal, except that the threshold for ADH release is reduced

# Investigations
- Reduction in plasma osmolality 
- Pseudohyponatremia =  laboratory artifact where a blood test incorrectly shows a low sodium level, even though the body's overall tonicity (osmolality) is normal. It happens because instruments that use the indirect ion-selective electrode method assume the blood sample is 93% water, but in patients with extreme hyperlipidemia (high fats) or hyperproteinemia (high proteins), this isn't true
- Urine osmolality
- Urine sodium concentration 
- Patient with hyponatremia due to volume depletion → urine sodium concentration will remain low, urine osmolality increased because of ADH 
- Patient with hyponatremia due to corrected volume + SIADH → urine sodium concentration and osmolality high
- - **Urine Na⁺ (UNa):**
    - **Low** (<20 mEq/L) → kidney conserving Na⁺ → suggests **ongoing hypovolemia**.
    - **High** (>30–40 mEq/L) → kidney is not avidly retaining Na⁺ → suggests **euvolemia** (or SIADH, or recent saline given).
- **Urine osmolality (Uosm):**
    - **High** (>300–450 mOsm/kg) → ADH is _on_ → concentrated urine.
    - **Low** (<100–200 mOsm/kg) → ADH appropriately _off_ → dilute urine, free water excretion.

# Treatment 
- Water restriction in an edematous patient with CHF, hepatic cirrhosis, renal disease
	- Should not be given sodium unless they have symptomatic hyponatremia
	- Loop diuretics 
	- Vasopressin antagonists (aquaretics), but not used in clinical care
- GI losses or bleeding
	- Isotonic saline or oral salt 
- Water restriction in those with primary polydipsia and SIADH 
	- Isotonic saline should be avoided in SIADH 
		- This will lead to a reduction in plasma sodium
