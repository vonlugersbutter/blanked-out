# Placental-fetal interface
The placenta serves as a selective barrier and a metabolic hub that coordinates communication between mother and fetus
- Permeability = hormones larger than 0.7 to 1.2 kDa generally cannot cross the placenta, meaning maternal hormones play a very limited role in the fetal environment
- Neutralization = protect the fetus, the placenta contains enzymes that inactivate maternal hormones
	- 11βHSD2 (11-beta hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2) converts active cortisol to inactive cortisone, and 17βHSD converts active estradiol to inactive estrone
- Placental production = produces vast amounts of estrogens and progesterone, though it lacks the enzyme CYP17 and must rely on substrates (C19 androgens like DHEA) provided by the fetal and maternal adrenal glands
# HPA axis
The fetal pituitary is well-developed by the end of the first trimester
- Anterior and intermediate lobes originate from oral ectoderm (Rathke’s pouch), while the posterior lobe originates from the ventral diencephalon → see [Pituitary Gland](14%20Endocrinology/Pituitary%20Gland.md)
- Pituitary hormone secretion begins at approximately 8 to 10 weeks of gestation
- All five specialized anterior pituitary cell types (lactotropes, somatotropes, etc) are present by 16 weeks
- Essential genes for pituitary development include HESX1, PROP1, and POU1F1 (Pit-1); mutations in these genes can lead to combined pituitary hormone deficiencies (CPHD)
- Intermediate lobe = unlike in adults, the intermediate lobe is prominent in the fetus and secretes α-MSH and β-endorphin, which may play roles in adrenal activation and fetal growth
# Fetal adrenal glands
The fetal [Adrenal Glands](14%20Endocrinology/Adrenal%20Glands.md) is massive relative to adult size, forming 0.4% of body weight at term
- Fetal zone (FZ) = unique inner zone accounts for 80% to 90% of the gland's mass and is primarily dedicated to producing DHEA and DHEAS
	- Precursors for placental estrogen production
- Steroidogenesis paradox = during most of gestation, the adrenal lacks the enzyme HSD3B2, which prevents it from making its own cortisol or aldosterone, instead funneling precursors into androgens
- Transient peak of cortisol occurs around 8 to 9 weeks, but the major cortisol surge happens near term, triggered by increased adrenal production and decreased placental inactivation
# Fetal thyroid 
The [[14 Endocrinology/Thyroid]] is one of the first endocrine organs to develop
- Hormone synthesis begins at 11 weeks of gestation
- Maturation of the full hypothalamo-pituitary-thyroid control system only develops in the third trimester
- Early in pregnancy, the fetus is highly dependent on maternal T4 for brain development
- Fetal environment is generally a state of "low T3" to maintain anabolism 
	- Achieved by high levels of Type 3 deiodinase (D3), which converts T4 into inactive reverse T3 (rT3)
# Fetal pancreas
Largely an anabolic process fueled by a continuous supply of maternal glucose, see [Endocrine Pancreas](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pancreas.md)
- Development = α and β cells are identifiable by 6 to 7 weeks
- Pancreas contains high insulin levels by 12 weeks, its secretion is blunted in utero
- Fetal glucose homeostasis is mostly independent of insulin and glucagon, relying instead on the transplacental gradient
- After birth, blood glucose levels fall to a nadir before counterregulatory hormones like glucagon and epinephrine stabilize them

## Gonadal differentiation
- Chromosomal sex is determined at fertilization 
- Gonadal ridges form about 7 weeks, derived from mesenchyme (mostly mesoderm)
- Germ cells derived from epiblast
- Invade gonadal ridges
- Failure to reach ridges: gonads do not develop
- Male/female gonads initially identical = “indifferent gonad”
- Starts with undifferentiated bipotential gonads, ends with testis or ovary 
- Both start out with paramesonephric ducts (or Müllerian ducts) and mesonephric ducts (or Wolffian ducts) on surface of mesonephric kidneys 
- Mullerian duct = fallopian tubes, uterus, superior portion (upper 2/3rds) of the vagina
- Wolffian duct = epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle → all driven by androgens 
- Thickened mesothelium, medial to mesonephros, proliferates to form the urogenital ridge on each side 
- Mesenchymal cells are going to migrate to the perineum to form swellings on either side of the cloaca → fuse along the midline to form the genital tubercle, just above the urogenital ostium, and will later make the clitoris or penis 
	- Cloaca will form urogenital sinus and anal canal
	- Cloaca is a turn of the gut tube that comes to the surface
- Also see [Reproductive Hormones](14%20Endocrinology/Reproductive%20Hormones.md) for the adult perspective on all these hormones 
## Male differentiation
- SRY Gene = in the presence of the SRY gene on the Y chromosome, male gonadal differentiation starts at 7 weeks
	- Aka testis-determining factor (TDF)
	- Also causes SOX 9 ↑ → leads to Sertoli cell differentiation 
- Gonadal cords condense, form rete testis 
- Sertoli cells start producing anti-Mullerian hormone → causes the regression of female internal structures
	- Small remnant of Mullerian structures is appendix testis
- Leydig cells produce testosterone starting at week 10, stimulating the Wolffian ducts to become male internal structures
- Vascularization requires a remodeling mechanism, whereas ovaries just use angiogenesis → pre-existing mesonephric vessels dissociate and migrate to form the coleomic vessel 
- Start to descend at only 10 weeks, aided by gubernaculum (fold of the peritoneum)
- Completed descent at 25th to 35th week
- Internal male genitalia (seminal vesicles, epididymis, vas deferens) require testosterone
- Cloaca will form bladder, prostate and prostatic urethra, and penile urethra
- External male genitalia (penis, scrotum, prostate) require DHT
- Pathologies
	- Cryptorchidism = undescended testes, which can lead to low sperm counts (due to temperature effects on Sertoli cells) and an increased risk of germ cell tumors
	- Varicocele = dilatation of the pampiniform plexus, more common on the left side due to the "nutcracker effect" where the left renal vein is compressed
		- Presents as a "bag of worms" in the scrotum and can cause infertility
## Female differentiation
- In the absence of AMH and high testosterone, the Müllerian duct system matures into female structures
	- Three phases: specification, invagination, elongation 
	- Wolffian remnant in females is called Gartner's duct, may form cyst
- FOXL2 (forkhead transcription factor 2), WNT4 (wingless-type MMTV integration site family member 4), RSPO1 (R-spondin 1), and the beta-catenin pathway
- Absence of active SRY gene in a chromosomal male won't result in ovaries, but variable dysgenesis
- Gonadal cords → network of canals called rete ovarii, eventually degenerate, leaving irregular clusters in medullary ovary 
- Cortical cords start to arise, grow into mesenchyme, house the primordial germ cells → keep increasing through mitosis and differentiate into oogonia
	- Oogonia do not form post-birth
	- 10th week, some are entering the meiotic phase, and then it goes from center to periphery 
- Become enveloped by follicular or granulosa cells, and 7 months in, all of them are in their meiotic prophase I arrested phase 
	- These are going to be stimulated at puberty to form primary follicles
- Small sample of those primary oocytes will mature, complete first meiotic division to form secondary oocyte 
	- Meiosis in males just begins at puberty 
- No coleomic vessel here, and the ovary does require germ cells to fully grow
- Cloaca will form bladder, inferior vagina, and connects with Mullerian ducts
- Uterine anomalies if the Mullerian ducts do not fuse
	- Lateral fusion defects most common, can cause [Infertility](13%20Reproduction/Reproductive%20Pathologies/Infertility.md)
	- Septate uterus = septum making two endometrial cavities, defect in resorption of septum between Mullerian ducts, requires surgery
	- Bicornuate = fundus is indented 
	- Unicornuate = only connects to one ovary 
	- Uterine didelphys = Mullerian ducts did not fuse
# Fetal growth factor
- GH and T4 are critical for postnatal growth, but play a limited role in the womb
- Insulin-like growth factors (IGF1 and IGF2) are the primary drivers of fetal growth
	- IGF2 is paternally expressed (imprinted) and is vital for placental nutrient supply and early growth
- Nutrition = fetal IGF production is regulated mainly by the transplacental supply of nutritional substrates
# Post birth
- Cortisol surge = late-gestation cortisol surge is critical for lung surfactant synthesis, gut enzyme maturation, and increasing T4 to T3 conversion
- Parturition triggers a massive release of NE and Epi, which mobilize pulmonary fluid, stimulate brown fat thermogenesis, ↑ blood pressure
- Fetus must move from a high-calcium environment (maintained by placental transport and PTHrP) to a low-calcium environment regulated by its own PTH and Vitamin D
# Barker hypothesis
- Intrauterine environment has significant impact on lifelong health
- Hormonal perturbations during critical windows (like excessive glucocorticoid exposure) → ↑ susceptibility to adult diseases 
- Intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR) and an increased risk of adult hypertension, insulin resistance, and cardiovascular disease
- Epigenetics = mediated by changes in gene expression (eg, DNA methylation) that can sometimes be transmitted across generations → see DOHADs
