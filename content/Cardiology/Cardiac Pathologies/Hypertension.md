Risk factor for CAD, stroke, heart failure.

Essential hypertension makes up 90% of cases, cause unknown. As opposed to secondary hypertension.

![](00%20media/{095C3399-733A-4E20-B028-AC09A923F59A}.png)
## Normal blood pressure control
Recall that 
$$
BP = CO \times TPR
$$
$$
CO = HR \times SV
$$
- Things that contribute to blood pressure control
	- Heart
	- Blood vessel tone
	- Kidneys
	- Hormones
- Pressure ↑ → urine volume, bp back to normal
	- This is pressure natriuresis, blunted in hypertensive patients
- Carotid sinus → glossopharyngeal IX
- Aortic sinus → vagus X
- Pressure ↑ → ↑ PNS → ↓ SNS, ↓ CO (HR ↓, reduced force)
## Essential hypertension
- Genetic component from angiotensinogen, alpha adducin (cytoskeletal protein, renal Na+ absorption)
- Insulin resistance
	- Insulin ↑ → ↑ SNS, smooth muscle hypertrophy
- Obesity
	- Angiotensinogen from adipocytes
	- Blood volume ↑ from body mass ↑ 
	- Blood viscosity ↑ , adipocytes release profibrinogen
- EH caused by multiple defects → genetic or acquired contribute to the disease process

## Secondary hypertension
- Age = before 20 or older than 50
- Severity = dramatic, quick rise
- Onset = abrupt
- Associated symptoms, like renal artery bruit for renal artery stenosis
- Family history is usually more visible in EH 
- Lab tests
	- Urinalysis to check for renal abnormalities
	- Glucose
	- Serum K+ (really low in renovascular hypertension or aldosteronism)
	- Serum cholesterols
	- EKG for LV hypertrophy
- Exogenous causes
	- Oral contraceptives → estrogen → ↑ angiotensinogen from liver
	- Glucocorticoids
	- EPO
	- NSAIDs (Na+ retention)
	- Ethanol and cocaine (both SNS ↑)
- Renal causes
	- Renal parenchymal disease
		- Nephrons don't excrete Na+ and water, so blood volume higher
	- Renal artery stenosis
		- Emboli, vasculitis, compression, atherosclerosis, fibromuscular dysplasia (proliferation, generally in young women)
		- Blood flow to the kidney ↓, ↑ renin, ↑ blood pressure 
		- Abdominal bruit or unexplained hypokalemia → renal excretion of K+ because ↑ aldosterone 
		- Use ACE inhibitors to treat
- Mechanical causes
	- Coarctation of aorta → congenital 
		- Blood flow to the kidneys ↓ → ↑ RAAS activity
		- High pressure proximal to coarctation → ↑ medial hyperplasia and atherosclerosis → baroreceptor response gets stunted 
		- Inadequate blood flow to legs/left arm, claudication, midsystolic murmur because aortic segment is stenosed → audible between scapulae 
- Endocrine causes 
	- Pheochromocytoma = catecholamine-secreting tumor, usually of adrenal medulla
		- Use some kind of alpha and beta blocker, or remove tumor 
	- Adrenocortical hormone excess
		- Primary aldosteronism = adrenal adenoma or bilateral hyperplasia of adrenal glands 
		- Secondary aldosteronism = rare renin-secreting tumors 
		- Women taking oral contraceptives
		- Excess glucocorticoids → ↑ blood volume, RAAS 
		- Cushing syndrome = too much glucocorticoids → hypertension 
	- Thyroid hormone
		- Inducing Na+/K+ ATPases in the heart and vessels
		- Increasing blood volume
		- Stimulating tissue metabolism and O2 demand 
## Consequences of hypertension
![](00%20media/{A57926DA-7B8C-41FB-ADE6-86DAEB349640}.png)
- Flushing, sweating, blurred vision
- Headache, nose bleeds, dizziness 
- Organ damage
	- Workload of heart, arterial damage, atherosclerosis
- Heart
	- LV hypertrophy and diastolic dysfunction
	- Systolic dysfunction
	- Coronary artery disease
- Cardiovascular system
	- Cerebrovascular accidents 
		- Hemorrhagic CVAs → rupture of microaneurysms in cerebral parenchymal vessels induced by long-standing hypertension
		- Atherothrombotic (also called thromboembolic) CVAs arise when portions of atherosclerotic plaque within the carotids or major cerebral arteries 
- Aorta and peripheral vasculature
	- Coronary artery atherosclerosis
	- Abdominal aortic aneurysm, rupture 
	- Aortic dissection! 
- Kidney
	- Hypertension-induced nephrosclerosis 
	- Perpetuation of elevated BP → a positive feedback cycle
- Retina → hypertensive retinopathy 
	- Burst small vessels, local infarction, nicks, indents
## Hypertensive crisis
- Medical emergency, severe elevation
- Acute hemodynamic insult or acute renal disease
- Hypertensive encephalopathy 
- Accelerated-malignant hypertension → acute damage to retinal vessels 
# Treatment
- Nonpharmacologic
	- Weight reduction
	- Exercise
	- Diet 
		- Salt restriction
		- Potassium increase
		- No alcohol
		- Get Ca++ and Mg++ in
	- Smoking cessation
	- Treat sleep apnea
	- Relaxation therapy
- Pharmacologic treatment
