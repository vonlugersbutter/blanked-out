# Etiology
## Colon polyps
- Hyperplastic polyps = these are benign, the most common type, and usually found in the rectosigmoid colon; they classically have a "saw tooth" pattern
- Adenomatous polyps = these are dysplastic with malignant potential
	- Shapes = sessile (broad base) or pedunculated (on a stalk)
	- Histology = tubular (most common) or villous (less common, often sessile, and high risk for cancer)
- Symptoms = polyps are almost always asymptomatic, but large ones may cause occult bleeding
	- Villous adenomas can lead to secretory diarrhea and hypokalemia
- High-risk indicators = villous histology, high-grade dysplasia, lesions >1 cm, and high numbers of polyps
- Juvenile polyps = benign hamartomas in children, usually in the rectum, causing painless bleeding
	- Juvenile polyposis syndrome increases cancer risk
- Peutz-Jeghers syndrome = an autosomal dominant disorder with multiple hamartomas and pigmented spots on the lips/mucosa; it carries risks for gastric, small intestinal, and colon cancer
## Genetics
- Chromosomal instability pathway (adenoma-carcinoma sequence) = this is the sequence of genetic events leading to cancer over 10–40 years, common in left-sided tumors
	1. APC mutation = loss of this tumor suppressor increases beta-catenin and polyp risk
	2. K-RAS mutation = a proto-oncogene mutation leading to adenoma formation
	3. P53 loss = leads to tumor cell growth
- Familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP) = germline mutation of the APC gene (chromosome 5q) that 100% progresses to colon cancer; treatment is colectomy
	- Variants include Gardner’s syndrome (polyposis plus osteomas, skin cysts, and CHRPE in the retina) and Turcot syndrome (polyposis plus brain tumors like medulloblastomas)
- Microsatellite instability = arises from mutations in DNA mismatch repair enzymes; more common in right-sided tumors
- HNPCC (hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer, which includes and once meant the same thing as Lynch syndrome) is an inherited form with an 80% lifetime risk of colon cancer and increased risk of endometrial cancer
- Other genetic factors = COX-2 expression is increased in colon cancer, providing a rationale for aspirin therapy the dcc gene is frequently mutated in advanced cancers

# Investigations
- Epidemiology = colon cancer is the 3rd most common and 3rd most deadly cancer, occurring mostly after age 50
- Site-specific symptoms 
	- Right-sided = iron-deficiency anemia, weight loss, and "exophytic" tumors
	- Left-sided = LLQ pain, blood-streaked stool, change in stool caliber, and circumferential lesions
- Metastasis = the liver is the most common site
- Screening = recommended at age 50 via colonoscopy (every 10 years) or fecal occult blood testing
- *Strep bovis* = this colonic bacteria is strongly associated with colon cancer; if *S. bovis* [Infective Endocarditis](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Valvular%20Diseases/Infective%20Endocarditis.md) is found, a colonoscopy must be performed
- CEA (carcinoembryonic antigen) = a tumor marker used to monitor disease burden and relapse, but poor for screening
