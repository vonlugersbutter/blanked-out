Ductus arteriosus remains open. Aortic blood flows into the pulmonary circuit. Both ventricles get overworked. 
# Definition
# Etiology
![200](00%20media/{F85163E8-04A9-482E-A1B2-DD0CC96003B6}.png)
- Risk factors
	- Maternal rubella infection
	- Prematurity
	- Birth at high altitude 
- Blood flows from aorta to pulmonary trunk after birth
- Volume load on pulmonary circulation, LA, LV 
- LV dilatation, left heart failure; right side stays normal unless there's pulmonary vascular disease → [Eisenmenger Syndrome](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Congenital%20Diseases/Eisenmenger%20Syndrome.md)

# Investigations
## Physical exam
- Continuous machine-like murmur, best at left subclavian 
	- Pressure gradient between aorta and pulmonary artery 
- Pulmonary vascular disease → murmur will get quieter 
- Clubbing, cyanosis may develop 
## CXR
- Enlarged silhouette, pulmonary markings
- Calcification of the ductus in adults
## Echo
- Visualize defect 
# Clinical features
- Asymptomatic
- Those with large shunts
	- Tachycardia
	- Poor feeding
	- Slow growth
	- Recurrent infections 
- Moderate lesions (all occur in adult life)
	- Fatigue
	- Dyspnea
	- Palpitations 
- Atrial fibrillation due to LA dilatation
- Turbulent flow across the defect → endarteritis, similar to [Infective Endocarditis](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Valvular%20Diseases/Infective%20Endocarditis.md)
# Treatment 
- Risk of endarteritis → have to close this even if asymptomatic 
- Prostaglandin inhibitors
- Surgical division or ligation
