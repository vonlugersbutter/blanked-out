# Definition

# Etiology
- Most pancreatic cancers are adenocarcinomas, more commonly found at the head of the pancreas
- Age >50, smoking, diabetes, and long-standing [Chronic Pancreatitis](10%20Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Chronic%20Pancreatitis.md) (>20 years)
- Also associated with BRCA2 mutations, particularly in Ashkenazi Jews
# Epidemiology

# Clinical features
- Often presents as "painless jaundice" because the slow-growing tumor gradually obstructs bile flow without causing acute inflammation
- Physical signs include Courvoisier’s sign (an enlarged, non-tender gallbladder with jaundice) and Trousseau’s syndrome (migratory superficial thrombophlebitis)
# Investigations
- CA-19-9 is the primary tumor marker used to follow treatment the most frequently mutated gene is K-RAS (seen in 90% of cases), while the tumor suppressor gene SMAD4 is inactivated in 60% of cases
# Prognosis
- Prognosis is very poor, with a 5-year survival rate of only 10% if nodes are positive
# Treatment 
- Chemotherapy, radiation, and the Whipple procedure (pancreatoduodenectomy)
