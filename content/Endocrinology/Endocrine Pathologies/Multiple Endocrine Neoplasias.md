# Definition
- MEN syndromes are rare, autosomal dominant genetic disorders where germline mutations lead to tumors in multiple endocrine glands
# Etiology
## MEN 1
- An inherited disorder involving tumors of the pituitary, parathyroid, and pancreas (including insulinomas and glucagonomas)
- Aka Wermer syndrome
- Primary hyperparathyroidism here happens because hyperplasia of all four parathyroid glands, or more if there are more 
	- Metachronous enlargement → selective resection will result in sustained clinical remission 
- Enteropancreatic or pancreaticoduodenal neuroendocrine tumors (NETs) = occur in 30%–70% of patients
	- Gastrinomas = most frequent functional enteropancreatic tumor (40%–60%); often small, multicentric, and ectopically located in the duodenal submucosa
		- [Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Zollinger-Ellison%20Syndrome.md)
		- They are often malignant, but for some unknown reason, are less aggressive than sporadic tumors 
	- Insulinomas = Constitute ~20% of islet cell tumors; notably, metastases are present in up to 50% of MEN1 cases compared to 10% in sporadic cases
	- Could also be VIPoma or glucagonoma 
	- Non-functional Tumors = potentially the most common; surgery is generally deferred for lesions < 2 cm
		- Apparently these are also the leading cause of death 
- Pituitary adenomas (most commonly prolactinomas)
	- Followed by GH-secreting and non-functional adenomas
	- May be more aggressive, larger (macroadenomas), and less responsive to medical therapy than sporadic counterparts
	- Pituitary adenomas here could also be secreting ACTH
- Burin variant = higher frequency of prolactinomas, carcinoids, and hyperparathyroidism, with low frequency of gastrinomas → not associated with a specific MEN1 mutation 

- Inheritance = Autosomal dominant with a prevalence of 2 to 20 per 100,000; approximately 10% of cases are "sporadic" (de novo germline mutations)
- Generally caused by a mutation in the men1 gene on chromosome 11q13, which codes for the tumor suppressor protein menin
	- Allelic loss, leading to large DNA deletions 
	- A lot of these mutations are just not detected by current technologies 
- Follows the Knudson "two-hit" model where an inherited defective allele is silent until a second somatic mutation (often a large deletion) leads to a null genotype and loss of heterozygosity (LOH)
- Menin generally acts as a scaffold protein for protein-protein interactions 
- Menin interacts with the trithorax family histone methyltransferase complex (specifically MLL proteins) to regulate the expression of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors p27Kip1 and p18Ink4c; loss of menin leads to down-regulation of these inhibitors and deregulated cell growth
- Loss of menin leads to downregulation of p27Kip1 and p18Ink4c → cell transitions uncontrollably into S phase → hyperplasia and adenomas occur 
- The specific tissues affected by MEN1 are dependent on the menin-mediated pathway for cell cycle regulation, while other tissues might have redundant pathways (like p21 or p16) that compensate for the loss of menin

## MEN 2
- MEN 2A and 2B = both involve medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) and pheochromocytoma
- Caused by gain-of-function germline mutations in the ret proto-oncogene on chromosome 10
- Inheritance = autosomal dominant with 1 to 10 per 100,000 prevalence
- Main determinant of clinical course is the MTC
	- Cancer of the parafollicular cells, can also occur sporadically 
	- Preceded by C-cell hyperplasia which shows up as a thyroid lesion
	- Diagnosis is via calcitonin levels and stimulated pentagastrin/calcium tests
- Caused by activating mutations in the RET proto-oncogene (chromosome 10), which encodes a transmembrane tyrosine kinase receptor for glial cell line–derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF)
- Unlike MEN1, these are gain-of-function mutations
	- Extracellular cysteine-rich domain mutations (MEN 2A) cause constitutive dimerization; intracellular kinase domain mutations (MEN 2B) alter substrate specificity to activate mitogenic pathways
- Pheochromocytoma is often bilateral, adrenal-based, and rarely malignant; must be ruled out before any neck surgery
- Cutaneous lichen amyloidosis (CLA) = pruritic skin lesion often associated with MEN 2A (codon 634) that may precede MCT
	- Some kind of cutaneous innervation issue 
- Variants can alco include MEN2 the CLA and Hirschsprung's disease, again an innervation issue
### MEN 2A
- MEN 2A = characterized by medullary thyroid carcinoma (MCT) (100%), pheochromocytoma (40%), and PHPT (25%)
- Aka Sipple syndrome
### MEN 2B (aka MEN3)
- MEN 2B = Characterized by MCT (100%), [Pheochromocytoma](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Pheochromocytoma.md) (50%), and distinct physical findings = marfanoid habitus, mucosal neuromas (lips/tongue), and intestinal ganglioneuromatosis 
- Unlike MEN 2A, it usually does not involve the parathyroid glands
- MCT is significantly more aggressive, presenting earlier in childhood
- 75% of cases are due to de novo mutations (primarily Met918Thr)
### Familial MCT
- MCT is the sole manifestation in the kindred
## MEN4
- A phenotypic mimic of MEN1 caused by germline mutations in CDKN1B (encoding p27Kip1)
## MEN5
- Putative syndrome associated with germline MAX mutations (14q23.3), characterized primarily by pheochromocytoma, but also pituitary and parathyroid tumors
## Other similar syndromes
- Hyperparathyroidism–Jaw Tumor (HPT-JT) syndrome = mutations in CDC73 (1q31.2), encoding parafibromin
	- PHPT with a remarkably high risk (15%–30%) of parathyroid carcinoma; associated with ossifying fibromas of the maxilla/mandible and renal/uterine tumors.
- Carney complex = autosomal dominant (PRKAR1A mutation on 17q22-24); includes cardiac/cutaneous myxomas, lentiginosis, and PPNAD 
	- PPNAD is primary pigmented nodular adrenal disease → basically causes ACTH-independent Cushing
	- GH-secreting pituitary tumors 
- McCune-Albright syndrome = somatic activating mutation in GNAS (Gs-alpha); leads to mosaicism with fibrous dysplasia, café au lait spots, and ligand-independent endocrine hyperfunction (eg, precocious puberty despite GH excess)
- Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) = VHL gene (3p25); Type 2 variant is associated with pheochromocytomas and pancreatic islet tumors
	- VHL is a tumor suppressor gene that regulates the hypoxia-inducible factor pathway 
- Neurofibromatosis Type 1 (NF1) = NF1 gene (17q112); endocrine features include pheochromocytoma and duodenal somatostatinomas
	- Will also see pituitary dysfunction due to optic gliomas 
- Cowden syndrome = mutations in PTEN (10q23.31), a negative regulator of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway
	- High risk of follicular thyroid cancer, breast cancer, and macrocephaly

# Clinical features
## MEN1
- Family history of other tumors 
- Primary hyperparathyroidism
- Enteropancreatic tumors (gastrinomas, insulinomas)
	- Gastrinoma would lead to [Peptic Ulcer Disease](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Peptic%20Ulcer%20Disease.md), diarrhea (due to acidity inactivating pancreatic enzymes) → heartburn and abdominal pain 
- Pituitary adenomas
	- In the case of a prolactinoma, the prolactin levels will be very high → much higher than you would expect from some kind of stalk effect (like there's another tumor there that's pressing on the stalk and preventing secretion of dopamine, so prolactin release is not inhibited)
	- Bitemporal hemianopsia, compression of the nasal retinal fibers 
	- High prolactin suppresses GnRH secretion from the hypothalamus, leading to hypogonadotropic hypogonadism → low FSH, LH, estradiol in women 
- Carcinoid tumors
- Adrenal adenomas 
# Investigations
## MEN1
- Diagnostic criteria could be any of the following
	- 2 or more primary MEN1 associated tumors
	- First degree of relative clinically diagnosed with MEN1, if they present with a single MEN1 tumor
	- Germline MEN1 mutation identified
- Tumors are often small, and CT and MRI only useful for larger lesions → important for showing hepatic metastasis, though 
- Use endoscopic and intraoperative US instead, radiolabeled octreotide scanning 
	- Still, a lot of gastrinomas are not found 
### Gastrinoma
- Check fasting gastrin, and then also do a secretin test for [Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Zollinger-Ellison%20Syndrome.md) 
	- Secretin stimulates gastrinomas but inhibits normal G cells
	- Note that PPI use, which would be happening because of the [Peptic Ulcer Disease](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Peptic%20Ulcer%20Disease.md) that presents because of elevated gastrin → could also ↑ gastrin levels → check the levels after withdrawl from PPIs 
		- Same thing with histamine receptor blockers 
- Check in the gastrinoma triangle
	- Gastrinoma triangle (or Passaro’s triangle) is an anatomical region in the abdomen where 70%–90% of primary gastrinomas (neuroendocrine tumors secreting gastrin) arise
	- Defined by the confluence of the cystic/common bile ducts (superior), the 2nd/3rd duodenum junction (inferior), and the pancreatic neck/body junction (medial)
- Normally duodenal ulcers present with weight gain, but here, these gastrinomas cause weight loss because they do allow for secretin release

# Treatment 
## MEN1
- Treatment can be hard
	- Lots of tumors, so surgical cure difficult
	- Occult metastases more likely
	- Larger tumors, more clinically aggressive 
- Hyperparathyroidism = Subtotal parathyroidectomy or total parathyroidectomy with forearm autograft is standard; persistence (12%) and recurrence (44%) are higher than in sporadic disease due to supernumerary glands and continued mitogenic drive
	- Will also remove thymus gland prophylactically, to avoid any intrathymic parathyroid glands getting tumors, and to prevent thymic [Carcinoid Tumors](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Carcinoid%20Tumors.md)
- Gastrinomas = mainstay is PPI therapy; surgical cure is difficult due to multicentricity, though resection of tumors > 3 cm is advised due to malignancy risk
	- Usually low-grade malignant behavior, long periods of symptom-free survival 
	- Could use chemotherapy or radiation for palliative care and advanced disease → especially in the case of hepatic metastases 
	- Calcium → ↑ acid → corrected the hyperparathyroidism may cause ↓ in gastrin, and things might normalize after parathyroidectomy 
		- G cells have CaSR → they will sense Ca++ → directly stimulate to ↑ gastrin and ↑ acid secreted from parietal cells 
### Surveillance
- Annual screening with ionized calcium, PTH, and gastrin (starting age 8–20); insulinoma screening (fasting glucose/insulin) starting age 5; and periodic MRI of the sella and abdomen
- All index cases and first-degree relatives 
- This whole analysis should not be used to do some kind of therapy → like don't do a pancreatic exploration just because, due to morbidity risk

## MEN2
- Conducting thyroidectomy is guided by following recommendations, primary to prevent medullary thyroid carcinoma 
	- Highest Risk (Level D) = RET Met918T (MEN 2B); thyroidectomy recommended in the first year of life (ideally < 6 months)
	- High Risk (Level C) = RET Cys634; thyroidectomy before age 5
	- Intermediate/Low Risk (Levels A & B) = Various codons (eg, 609, 611, 804); surgery may be delayed beyond age 5 if basal calcitonin and ultrasounds remain normal
	- Multi-kinase inhibitors (vandetanib, cabozantinib) and selective RET inhibitors (selpercatinib, pralsetinib) are first-line therapies
- For anyone that has a pheochromocytoma
	- Often bilateral (50%); adrenal-sparing surgery is increasingly advocated to avoid lifelong adrenal insufficiency
