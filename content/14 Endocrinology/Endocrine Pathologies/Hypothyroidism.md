# Definition
Insufficient production of thyroid hormones, usually caused by permanent loss or destruction of the thyroid gland (primary hypothyroidism). 
# Etiology
- Hashimoto’s thyroiditis = most common non-dietary cause of hypothyroidism, characterized by an autoimmune lymphocytic infiltration and the presence of anti-TPO antibodies, thyroglobulin antibodies may also be found 
	- HLA-DR3, DR5 are implicated 
	- There is a transient hyperthyroidism because all the TPO and thyroglobulin release
	- Massive lymphocytic infiltrate (germinal centers form)
	- Hurthle cells = enlarged eosinophilic follicular cells 
	- Usually in women, enlarged, non tender thyroid gland
	- Increased risk of Non Hodgkin B cell lymphoma, because lymphatic infiltration
	- Could also have lymphocytic thyroiditis, aka painless thyroiditis, which is a variant of this 
		- Lymphocytic infiltration of gland, transient hyperthyroidism 
		- Followed by hypothyroidism
		- All of it usually self limiting 
- Goitrogens = substances that inhibit thyroid hormone production
	- Usually iodine
	- Lithium
- Central (secondary/tertiary) hypothyroidism = due to insufficient stimulation of a normal gland by the hypothalamus or pituitary gland, resulting in TSH deficiency
- Transient hypothyroidism = often observed as a phase of subacute thyroiditis or following childbirth (postpartum thyroiditis)
- Iatrogenic hypothyroidism = could occur from thyroid surgery, radioiodine therapy, neck radiation for unrelated cancers 
- Subacute thyroiditis, de Quervain's, granulomatous thyroiditis 
	- Young females, granulomatous infiltration of thyroid 
	- Hyperthyroid → euthyroid → hypothyroid 
	- Anti-inflammatories needed, usually mild symptoms, resolves by itself 
- Riedel's thyroiditis
	- Fibroblast activation, proliferation
	- Fibrous tissue deposition, rock hard
	- Goes into parathyroid glands, laryngeal nerves, trachea compression 
	- IgG4 plasma cells are involved 
- Myxedema
	- Non-pitting edema of the skin from hypothyroidism
	- Hyaluronic acid deposit in the dermis, draw out water → facial and periorbital swelling → could also be pretibial myxedema (peau d'orange in Grave's disease )
	- Myxedema coma can result
- Congenital hypothyroidism = identified in about 1 =3000 newborns via screening programs; often caused by thyroid agenesis or dysplasia
- Consumptive hypothyroidism = rare form caused by accelerated inactivation of thyroid hormone by type 3 iodothyronine deiodinase (D3), often expressed in large hemangiomas
- Serum creatine kinase ↑ → weakness, cramps, myalgia → hypothyroid myopathy 
- Hypothyroidism → ↑ ADH (SIADH) → ↓ Na+ → confusion 
# Epidemiology
- Autoimmune causes more common in women 
# Clinical features
- Skin and appendages = accumulation of hyaluronic acid leads to myxedema (thickened, puffy skin) Skin is often pale, cool, and dry; hair becomes brittle and may fall out (including the lateral aspects of the eyebrows, known as the Queen Anne sign)
- Cardiovascular = reduced cardiac output, bradycardia (slow heart rate), and increased peripheral vascular resistance 
	- Severe cases may show an enlarged cardiac silhouette due to pericardial effusion
	- Consistently associated with elevations in LDL cholesterol and triglycerides
		- Hyperlipidemia due to ↓ LDL receptor density → T3 upregulates this 
- Slowing of all intellectual functions, memory defects, lethargy, and somnolence 
	- Physical signs include "hung-up" tendon reflexes (delayed relaxation phase) and potential carpal tunnel syndrome due to glycosaminoglycan deposits
	- Hung up reflexes are called Woltman's sign 
- Cold intolerance
- Weight gain, no appetite, constipation
- Respiratory = increased prevalence of obstructive sleep apnea and depression of the hypoxic ventilatory drive
- Essential for bone maturation; deficiency in early life leads to dwarfism and epiphyseal dysgenesis (a stippled appearance of bone ossification centers)
# Investigations
- Primary hypothyroidism = characterized by a high serum TSH and low free T4
- Central hypothyroidism = characterized by a low free T4 with an inappropriately normal or low TSH
- Presence of antithyroid peroxidase (TPO) antibodies generally points to Hashimoto disease as the underlying cause
- Risk of progression from subclinical to overt hypothyroidism is most closely related to the magnitude of TSH elevation and the presence of TPO antibodies
# Prognosis
- Myxedema coma = the ultimate stage of severe, long-standing hypothyroidism, characterized by hypothermia, bradycardia, and coma
	- Medical emergency requiring rapid repletion with intravenous levothyroxine and supplemental glucocorticoids to prevent adrenal crisis ([Adrenal Insufficiency](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Adrenal%20Insufficiency.md) often coexists with these autoimmune conditions)
- Thyroiditis = a group of inflammatory conditions painful subacute thyroiditis (postviral) features extreme thyroid tenderness and a suppressed RAIU, while infectious thyroiditis is a rare bacterial or fungal infection of the gland
# Treatment 
- Levothyroxine (Synthroid): synthetic T4 (preferred)
- Liothyronine (Cytomel): synthetic T3
- T3 absorbed from intestines rapidly, can cause mild [Hyperthyroidism](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Hyperthyroidism.md) symptoms
- Titrate dose until TSH is normal
- Levothyroxine (t4) monotherapy is the current standard of care the typical daily dose is approximately 16 to 18 μg/kg of ideal body weight
- For primary hypothyroidism, goal is to normalize the serum TSH 
- For central hypothyroidism, goal is to restore ft4 to the upper half of the reference range
- Labs are usually checked in 6 weeks → half-life of $T_4$ is about 7 days
	- It takes roughly 5 half-lives (5–6 weeks) to reach a new steady-state concentration where the TSH can be accurately interpreted
	- This is usually standard I think for most drugs
