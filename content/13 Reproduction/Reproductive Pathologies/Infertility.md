# Definition
Inability to conceive after 1 year of unprotected intercourse with reasonable frequency.
- Primary infertility = no prior pregnancies
- Secondary infertility = occurs after at least one prior conception
- Fecundability = the ability to conceive the monthly probability of conceiving is typically 20 to 25 percent
- Over 85% of couples attempting conception will be pregnant within one year
- For women older than 35 years, evaluation is recommended after only 6 months due to lower pregnancy outcomes and higher aneuploidy rates
# Etiology
- Successful pregnancy requires a complex sequence = ovulation, ovum pick-up by the fallopian tube, fertilization, transport to the uterus, and implantation
- Common causes in broad categories 
	- Male factor (sperm production) = 25%
	- Ovulatory dysfunction = 27%
	- Tubal/uterine factors = 22%
	- Other factors = 9%
	- Unexplained = 17%
- Thyroid hormone ↓ → less sex hormone binding globulin, for [Hypothyroidism](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Hypothyroidism.md)
- [Hemochromatosis](10%20Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Hemochromatosis.md) = HFE protein defective, usually responsible for secretion of hepcidin → deficiency here means too much Fe → iron deposition in [Pituitary Gland](14%20Endocrinology/Pituitary%20Gland.md) → no GnRH, LH, FSH
## Lifestyle factors
- Body mass index (BMI) >25 or <17 is linked to GnRH and gonadotropin secretion abnormalities → [Obesity](10%20Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Obesity.md) can lower fecundity through poor oocyte quality and altered endometrial function 
	- 10-15% reduction in body mass can lead to 20 to 40% rises in successful pregnancies 
- Smoking = negatively impacts fertility; awareness of this risk is relatively low compared to heart/respiratory diseases
- Alcohol = heavy intake lowers fertility in women and is associated with decreased sperm counts in men
- Caffeine = moderation is recommended, though data on its negative impact is conflicting
- Illicit drugs = strongly discouraged while marijuana may not significantly lengthen "time to pregnancy," it is linked to poor neonatal outcomes like premature rupture of membranes and fetal demise
- Folic acid = daily multivitamin with at least 400 µg of folic acid is encouraged to prevent neural tube defects
- [Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome](13%20Reproduction/Reproductive%20Pathologies/Polycystic%20Ovarian%20Syndrome.md)

# Investigations
## History
Both partners are urged to attend the initial consultation for education on the conception process
- Fertile window = the chance of conception increases from 5 days preceding ovulation through the day of ovulation
- Female history
	- Gynecologic = menstrual patterns, contraceptive use, duration of infertility, history of PID, STDs, endometriosis, and prior pregnancy complications (eg, miscarriage, preterm delivery)
	- Medical/surgical = symptoms of thyroid disease, hyperprolactinemia, or androgen excess (acne, hirsutism) prior chemotherapy or pelvic irradiation is also relevant
- Male history
	- Focuses on prior chemotherapy/radiation, medical conditions like diabetes (linked to erectile dysfunction), and exercise habits (eg, heavy bicycling or long-distance running)
## Female fertility
### Confirming ovulation
- Cyclic menses (25–35 days) is a strong predictor of regular ovulation
- Ovulation predictor kits = measure urinary luteinizing hormone (LH) concentration ovulation typically occurs the day following the LH peak
- Serum progesterone = adequate levels are needed for endometrial preparation; low levels may suggest a luteal phase defect
- Sonography = serial ovarian sonograms can track antral follicle development and collapse
### Ovarian reserve 
- FSH and estradiol = "cycle day 3" FSH level >10 mIU/ml may indicate a significant loss of ovarian reserve
- Anti-Mullerian hormone (AMH) = expressed by granulosa cells of small follicles; used as a predictor of the follicle pool
- Can also measure inhibin B 
### Anatomy evaluation
- Hysterosalpingography (HSG) = used to evaluate tubal patency and the uterine cavity (eg, looking for "filling defects" caused by polyps or leiomyomas)
- Saline-infusion sonography (SIS) = more sensitive than HSG for identifying endometrial polyps or submucous leiomyomas
- Laparoscopy = often indicated if endometriosis or pelvic adhesive disease (PAD) is suspected
## Male fertility
### Semen analysis
- Reference limits
	- Volume = >15 ml
	- Count = >15 million/ml
	- Total motility = >40%
	- Morphology = >4%
- Terminology
	- Aspermia = no semen 
	- Oligospermia = low sperm concentration
	- Azoospermia = complete absence of sperm in semen
	- Asthenospermia = decreased sperm motility
	- Teratospermia = increased morphologically abnormal sperm
	- Necrozoospermia = dead sperms
	- Leukocytospermia = high WBC in semen  
## Genetic analysis
- Female = carriers screening for cystic fibrosis, spinal muscular atrophy, and thalassemias should be offered karyotype testing and fragile X screening are considered for women with primary ovarian insufficiency (POI)   
- Male = karyotype testing is recommended for men with severe oligospermia (<5 million/ml), as genetic abnormalities are a relatively common cause of abnormal semen characteristics
## Approach
- Confirming female ovulation and tubal patency while performing a semen analysis for the male because the incidence of couples having two abnormalities is high, both partners should ideally complete their evaluations even if one clear defect is found
