# Sonography (Ultrasound)
## Grayscale Imaging Techniques
- Transabdominal = Generally the first component of an examination, using a 3- to 5-MHz transducer It offers a global view of pelvic organs A full bladder is preferred as it acts as an "acoustic window" and displaces the bowel
- Transvaginal (TVS) = Required for detailed assessment of the endometrial cavity when transabdominal imaging is limited
- Transrectal and Transperineal = Less common; used primarily for pelvic floor imaging
- Harmonic Imaging = A newer technique using multiple frequencies simultaneously to improve resolution and reduce artifacts from superficial structures like adipose tissue
- Saline-Infusion Sonography (SIS) = Also called sonohysterography It involves distending the endometrial cavity with sterile saline to provide detailed anatomical views It is often used to investigate suspected endometrial masses, infertility, or when the endometrial echo is poorly imaged
## Three-Dimensional (3-D) Sonography
- Components = Volume acquisition (using automated mechanized probes), processing, and display
- Display Modes = The most common is multiplanar reconstruction, displaying sagittal, axial, and coronal planes simultaneously Rendered images provide a "sum" of all coronal planar images
- Clinical Use = Invaluable for assessing the uterine cavity, complex ovarian masses, fertility reserve, uterine anomalies, and interstitial pregnancies It is increasingly used to map leiomyomas and evaluate focal endometrial thickenings like polyps or cancer
# Computed Tomography (CT)
CT uses multiple thin x-ray beams to create 2-D axial "slices"
- Helical (Spiral) CT = Allows for continuous image acquisition and multiplanar reformatting, making it much faster than traditional CT
- Contrast Enhancement = Intravenous (IV) contrast is used to superiorly evaluate solid organ parenchyma and vasculature, making masses more obvious due to density differences
- Specialized CT = CT angiography (CTA) evaluates vasculature, while CT cystography can be used to identify bladder injuries by retrograde filling with contrast
# Magnetic Resonance (MR) Imaging
MRI provides superior soft-tissue contrast and multiplanar imaging without ionizing radiation
- T2-weighted = Provides detailed architecture of internal organs like the uterus and ovaries
- T1-weighted = Delineates organ boundaries and fat, optimal for visualizing lymph nodes
- Gadolinium-based contrast agents (GBCA) = Paramagnetic agents that enhance information regarding tissue vascularity Caution is required for patients with severe renal disease due to the risk of nephrogenic systemic fibrosis (NSF)
- Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) = Helps identify tumors and abscesses by characterizing proton movement in highly cellular tissues
- Indications = Used when sonography is equivocal, particularly for distorted anatomy, large masses, adenomyosis, and staging pelvic malignancies MRI is highly accurate (92%) in staging endometrial cancer
# Radiographic & Nuclear Techniques
- Hysterosalpingography (HSG) = A radiographic technique used in infertility evaluations to assess the endocervical canal, endometrial cavity, and fallopian tube patency by injecting radiopaque contrast
- Positron Emission Tomography (PET) = Often fused with CT (PET-CT), it uses tracers like FDG to highlight areas of accelerated glycolysis common in neoplastic cells, aiding in cancer staging and recurrence detection
# Interventional Radiology
- Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE) = A vascular intervention using angiography to guide the injection of embolic particles into the uterine arteries to treat leiomyomas or control severe bleeding It has also shown efficacy in treating adenomyosis
- MR-guided Focused Ultrasound (MRgFUS) = Also known as MR-HIFU, this treatment directs high-power ultrasound pulses ("sonications") into uterine myomas MRI guidance allows for precise tissue targeting and real-time thermometry to minimize thermal injury to surrounding tissues
