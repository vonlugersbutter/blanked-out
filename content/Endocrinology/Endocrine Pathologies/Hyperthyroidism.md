# Definition
Excessive production of thyroid hormones.
# Etiology
- Primary disease
	- TSH is opposite thyroid hormone
- Central disease
	- Rare disorders of the pituitary and hypothalamus, usually tumors that block TRH/TSH leading to [Hypothyroidism](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Hypothyroidism.md), but can also have TSHoma 
- Grave’s disease = the leading cause of hyperthyroidism, where antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor unique features include exophthalmos (bulging eyes) and pretibial myxedema
- Toxic multinodular goiter
- Amiodarone
- Iodine load
- Early thyroiditis 
- Gestational thyrotoxicosis = transient, physiologic hyperthyroidism in the first trimester caused by high levels of hCG, which acts as a weak TSH receptor agonist
## Pathophysiology
- Type 2 hypersensitivity reaction → lymphocyte infiltration → cytokine receptors
- B cells, activated by T-helper cells, produce thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulins (TSI/TSAb) that act as agonists to TSH receptors
## Jod-Basedow phenomenon
- Iodine-induced hyperthyroidism, regions of iodine deficiency, where introduction of iodine causes this
- Often in those with toxic adenomas → drugs administered with high iodine content like CT contrast dye or amiodarone

# Clinical features
- Metabolism speeds up, causing hyperactivity, heat intolerance, weight loss despite increased appetite, and tachycardia
- Goiter = enlarged thyroid, high TSH, can't produce T3 or T4
- Exophthalmos, periorbital edema, pretibial myxedema, 
	- T cells activate fibroblasts, which contain TSH receptors
	- Secretion of glycosaminoglycans
	- Hydrophilic substances, mostly hyaluronic acid, draws in water, leads to swelling 
- Merseburg Triad = classic hallmarks are palpitations, goiter, and exophthalmos (eye bulging)
- Thyroid bruit = audible bruit and palpable thrill are due to the massive increase in thyroid blood flow In Graves' → hypervascularization 
- Lid lag = sympathetic overactivity sign where the upper eyelid is higher than normal on downward gaze
# Investigations
- Initial screen: look at the TSH → serum TSH concentration (usually <0.1 mU/L) low and elevated free T4 and T3 tell you that it's the target organ that's the issue 
- Conduct a radioactive iodine uptake test to check for Grave's disease → usually not elevated uptake in thyroiditis or exogenous hormone intake
- A small amount of radioactive iodine (I131) can be used for uptake and visualization
	- Hot nodule is usually no cancer
	- Cold nodule has 5% chance of cancer, see [Thyroid Cancer](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Thyroid%20Cancer.md)
- Then check for TSHR antibodies, which are consistent for Grave's 
# Prognosis
## Thyroid storm
- Life-threatening massive catecholamine surge, often triggered by surgery or infection in patients with existing hyperthyroidism
- Hyperglycemia, hypercalcemia 
- Steroids to reduce T4 → T3 conversion, suppress autoimmune damage
- Note that serum hormone levels do not necessarily correlate with severity here! 
- Requires intensive care, intravenous fluids, beta-blockers, high-dose antithyroid drugs (usually PTU), and glucocorticoids to block hormone conversion
- One hour after the initial ATD dose, potassium iodide is administered to inhibit hormone release
- 4 Ps of management 
	- Propranolol
	- Propyl thyrouracil
	- Prednisone
	- Potassium iodide to stop TPO synthesis
- For evaluation, use the Burch-Wartofsky Point Scale (BWPS) for Thyrotoxicosis
	- Takes into account temperature, CNS involvement, HR, presence of [Atrial Fibrillation (AF)](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Arrhythmias/Atrial%20Fibrillation%20(AF).md), presence of precipitating event 
# Treatment 
- Radioactive ablation with a large amount of radioactive iodine 
- Thionamides
	- Propylthiouracil (PTU)
		- Inhibits TPO: ↓ T3/T4 from thyroid gland
		- Inhibits 5’-deiodinase: ↓ T4 to T3 conversion peripherally
	- Methimazole
		- Inhibits TPO
		- Teratogen, especially in first trimester 
	- Side effects: skin rash, agranulocytosis and aplastic anemia, hepatotoxicity 
- Propranolol
	- Beta blocker
	- Weak inhibitor of 5’-deiodinase, excellent drug in thyrotoxicosis
	- Blocks catecholamines and T4-T3 conversion
- If you're going to do radioactive iodine therapy to treat hyperthyroidism, you need to hold methimazole for a few days → methimazole prevents the organification of iodine, and we need the I131 to be trapped in the follicles to ablate the overactive tissue 
- When a woman with Grave’s disease gets pregnant, she still has those antibodies → don’t want the fetus to develop this
	- Propylthiouracil (PTU) is generally preferred in the first trimester to avoid rare methimazole-associated birth defects
	- Methimazole (MMI) can be used in subsequent trimesters
	- Aim for mild undertreatment; the maternal free T4 should be kept at or slightly above the upper limit of normal to prevent fetal hypothyroidism, which can impair cognitive development
