# Etiology
![](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/{620C6DA5-6B34-487C-9FFB-884A34ECB530}.png)
- Primary (Addison’s disease) = failure of the adrenal gland itself low cortisol and aldosterone with high ACTH, leading to skin hyperpigmentation (via MSH)
	- Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome = acute adrenal insufficiency caused by hemorrhage, often associated with meningococcemia
		- Rapid onset of septic shock, widespread purpura (purple skin rash), and disseminated intravascular coagulation
- Secondary = failure of pituitary ACTH release only cortisol is low; aldosterone is usually unaffected
- ADH is also affected, so we will see some forms of central forms of [Diabetes](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Diabetes/Diabetes.md)
	- Cortisol normally inhibits ADH → ↑ ADH

# Prognosis
- Adrenal crisis = an acute, life-threatening loss of function resulting in shock
	- Sudden discontinuation of corticosteroids
	- Or undiagnosed, and person experiences some kind of huge stress 
	- Can also mimic an acute abdomen 
	- 5S's → Salt (0.9% saline), Sugar (50% dextrose), Steroids (100 mg hydrocortisone IV once, then 200 mg over 24 hours), Support (normal saline to correct hypotension and electrolyte abnormalities), and Search (for the underlying disorder)
- In secondary and tertiary, if they don't get stimulated, the adrenal gland will eventually become atrophied and it will become primary
