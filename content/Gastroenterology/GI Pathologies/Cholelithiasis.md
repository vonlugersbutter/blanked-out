# Definition
- Bile in the gallbladder consists of bilirubin, bile salts, and cholesterol an upset in the balance of these components causes precipitation and stone formation
- Cholelithiasis is gallstones in the gallbladder 
- Gallstones can cause cholelithiasis, choledocholithiasis, acute [Cholecystitis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Cholecystitis.md), acute [Cholangitis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Cholangitis.md)
# Etiology
- Bile cholesterol oversaturation, stasis, impaired circulation → bowel rest, TPN, pregnancy
- Cholesterol gallstones = these are typically radiolucent (not visible on x-ray) and are most common in 40-year-old females
	- Risk factors include estrogen (which increases cholesterol synthesis), obesity, rapid weight loss, and pregnancy
	- Think 6Fs → female, fat, fair-skinned, family history, fertile, forty
- Bile salt risk factors = a loss of bile salts—which are normally 95% recycled via the terminal ileum—leads to stones this can be caused by cirrhosis (decreased synthesis), Crohn’s disease (impaired resorption in the ileum, see [Inflammatory Bowel Disease](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Inflammatory%20Bowel%20Disease.md)), cystic fibrosis, or medications like clofibrate and bile acid resins
- Pigment stones (bilirubin stones) = composed of calcium bilirubinate, these are radiopaque (visible on x-ray) and can be black or brown they result from a rise in unconjugated bilirubin, often due to extravascular hemolysis, cirrhosis, or recurrent biliary tree infections where bacteria produce glucuronidases
	- Chronic hemolytic anemias, cirrhosis, 
	- Hemolysis → unconjugated bilirubin → increased uptake and conjugation of bilirubin → prepiciptation of bilirubin and stone formation 
- Mixed stones = formation of gallbladder stones composed of a combination of ingredients, most commonly cholesterol, calcium salts (bilirubinate, carbonate, phosphate), and bile pigments
	- Infection or infestation → release of β-glucuronidase (by injured hepatocytes and bacteria) → hydrolyzes conjugated bilirubin and lecithin in the bile → increased unconjugated bilirubin and fatty acids

# Epidemiology
- More likely in middle-aged females 
# Clinical features
- Usually **asymptomatic**
- Biliary colic = episodic RUQ pain, often after fatty meals, caused by the gallbladder contracting against a stone in the outlet → for **less than 6 hours**
	- Due to CCK release 
- Nausea, vomiting, bloating
# Investigations
- Lab findings are usually normal
- Diagnostic imaging with US will show gallstones 
- MRI with MRCP (magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography)
- CT abdomen, could also have an abdominal XR
# Treatment 
- Supportive care, pain medication, low fat diet 
- Extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL)
- Elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy → indicated if it is symptomatic or there is increased risk of gallbladder cancer 
- But if there is gallbladder cancer, then open surgery is recommended 
# Prognosis
## Complications 
- Acute [Cholecystitis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Cholecystitis.md) = gallbladder inflammation caused by a stone obstructing the cystic duct features include RUQ pain, fever, and a positive murphy’s sign
- Choledocholithiasis = a stone in the common bile duct causing biliary obstruction, jaundice, and elevated alkaline phosphatase
- Ascending [Cholangitis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Cholangitis.md) = a medical emergency where a stone blocks bile flow, allowing GI bacteria to ascend the biliary tree
	- Presents as Charcot’s triad (fever, pain, jaundice) or Reynolds pentad (adding confusion and hypotension, indicating sepsis) treatment involves antibiotics and biliary drainage, often via ERCP
- Gallstone ileus = a massive stone erodes through the gallbladder wall into the small intestine, creating a fistula and causing a bowel obstruction at the ileocecal valve a key imaging finding is air in the biliary tree
	- **Rigler triad** = imaging findings of small bowel obstruction, gallstone (most commonly in iliac fossa), and pneumobilia
- Gallbladder carcinoma = a rare adenocarcinoma resulting from chronic inflammation (eg, porcelain gallbladder) or chronic *Salmonella typhi* infection (carrier state)
- Biliary [Acute Pancreatitis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Acute%20Pancreatitis.md) 
- Mirizzi syndrome = extrinsic compression of the common bile duct (or any extrahepatic bile duct) by gallstone(s) impacted in the cystic duct or the infundibulum of the gallbladder, very similar to choledocholithiasis 
## Note on choledocholithiasis
- Just doing this here because feel that another page is not needed
- Gallstones in CBD
- Usually because cholelithiasis → migrating stone
- RUQ pain lasting more than an hour, possible jaundice
- ↑ total bilirubin, ↑ GGT, ↑ alk phos, ↑ AST, ALT → all showing cholestatic pattern 
- Dilated CBD, MRCP or ERCP 
	- MRCP = magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography is a non-invasive MRI scan → detailed images of the liver, gallbladder, bile ducts, and pancreas
	- ERCP = endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography is a specialized, minimally invasive procedure combining upper endoscopy and X-ray imaging to diagnose and treat conditions of the bile and pancreatic ducts
- Supportive care, analgesics, stone retrieval, elective cholecystectomy 
- Could also happen from post-cholecystectomy choledocholithiasis if stones were missed
