# Definition
Gastro-esophageal reflux disease. 
# Etiology
- Alcoholism can increase risk because of belly pushing on structures
- Risk factors include alcohol, smoking, obesity, fatty foods, caffeine, and hiatal hernias
- GERD occurs when gastric juice from the stomach "refluxes" back into the esophagus, representing a failure of the LES due to decreased tone
- Reflux esophagitis involves inflammation of the epithelial layer, characterized by mucosa erythema, edema, and erosions
- Histologically, reflux esophagitis presents with basal zone hyperplasia, elongation of the lamina propria papilla, and the presence of eosinophils and neutrophils
- Ingestion of lye = accidental ingestion of alkali substances (sodium/potassium hydroxide) by children causes liquefactive necrosis, which rapidly injures the esophageal wall and can result in strictures

# Prognosis
- Barrett's esophagus is a serious condition where the tissue lining the lower esophagus changes due to long-term acid reflux (GERD), becoming similar to intestinal lining, a process called metaplasia, which slightly increases the risk for [Esophageal Cancer](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Esophageal%20Cancer.md)
	- Endoscopically, Barrett's appears pink/red compared to the normal white squamous mucosa patients require regular surveillance endoscopy with biopsies to screen for carcinoma
- Ulcers, fibrosis, and strictures = acid can destroy the mucosa, which is then replaced by fibrous tissue, leading to strictures and dysphagia

# Clinical features
- Pediatric GERD is often caused by an immature les and manifests as vomiting and crying
- Symptoms include heartburn (retrosternal burning after meals or when lying flat), dysphagia due to painful esophagitis, and respiratory issues like adult-onset asthma, cough, or dyspnea chronic acid exposure can also damage tooth enamel
# Treatment 
- PPIs
- Sodium alginate 
- Treatment involves weight loss and avoiding triggers like chocolate, spicy foods, carbonated beverages, and peppermint refractory cases may require a Nissen fundoplication surgery (wrapping the top of the stomach (fundus) 360 degrees around the lower esophagus)
- Medical therapies include h2 blockers (eg, famotidine, ranitidine), which block parietal cell histamine receptors, and proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) (eg, omeprazole, lansoprazole), which inhibit the H+/K+ pump
