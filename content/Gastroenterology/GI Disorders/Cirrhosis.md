# Definition
- Cirrhosis is the irreversible replacement of liver tissue with fibrosis and nodules, commonly caused by viral hepatitis (b and c), alcohol, or NAFLD
# Etiology
- Stellate cells are the major contributors to cirrhosis; when activated by liver disease, they secrete TGF-β and produce fibrous tissue
- Systemic manifestations 
	- Hyperammonemia = loss of ammonia clearance leads to asterixis (hand flapping), confusion, and coma treatment involves a low protein diet and lactulose, which traps NH4+ in the colon
	- Loss of synthesis = leads to jaundice, hypoglycemia, coagulopathy, and hypoalbuminemia
	- Elevated estrogen = because the liver cannot remove it, patients may develop gynecomastia, spider angiomata, and palmar erythema
- Hemodynamics = cirrhosis causes splanchnic vasodilation via nitric oxide, leading to decreased systemic vascular resistance (SVR), activation of RAAS and ADH, and a massive increase in total body water
- Obstructed blood flow increases pressure in the portal vein, opening venous collaterals 
	- Umbilicus = paraumbilical and epigastric veins engorge, causing caput medusa
	- Esophagus = drainage via the left gastric vein leads to esophageal varices, a source of painless bleeding
	- Stomach = short gastric veins lead to gastric varices
	- Rectum = superior rectal vein involvement leads to internal hemorrhoids
- Hypersplenism = engorgement of the splenic vein leads to a palpable spleen and low platelets
- Ascites is fluid accumulation in the peritoneal cavity due to portal hypertension and low albumin
- SAAG (serum ascites albumin gradient) = a SAAG > 11 g/dl indicates portal hypertension; a saag < 11 g/dl suggests malignancy
	- Treatment = sodium restriction, spironolactone (drug of choice), or a tips procedure, which connects the portal vein directly to the hepatic vein to bypass the liver
- Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP) = an infection of ascitic fluid (usually e coli or klebsiella) presenting with fever and abdominal pain diagnosis is confirmed by absolute PMNs (neutrophils) ≥ 250 cells/mm³
## Biliary cirrhosis
- Liver damage resulting from chronic biliary obstruction, which can be caused by gallstones, pancreatic cancer, or strictures
- Primary biliary cirrhosis (PBC) = an autoimmune disorder characterized by a T-cell attack on small interlobular bile ducts, leading to granulomatous inflammation
	- Most common in women, with fatigue and severe pruritus (itching) being the most common initial symptoms
	- Pruritus often precedes jaundice and may worsen at night
	- Diagnosis = the hallmark is the presence of anti-mitochondrial antibodies (found in ~95% of patients)
		- Labs show markedly elevated alkaline phosphatase and occasionally elevated serum lipids (cholesterol >1000), which may cause xanthomas liver
		- Biopsy is the gold standard for diagnosis but is often not required if antibodies are present  
	- Treatment = ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) is the only effective therapy; it replaces endogenous bile acids and improves liver function tests
		- Liver transplant is an option for advanced disease
- Associated disorders = PBC is frequently associated with other autoimmune disorders, most commonly Sjogren’s syndrome

# Investigations
- MELD score = uses bilirubin, creatinine, and INR to estimate 3-month mortality
- Child-Pugh classification = uses encephalopathy, ascites, bilirubin, albumin, and pt to categorize cirrhosis severity into classes a, b, and c
- Diagnosis = while biopsy is the gold standard, clinical diagnosis is common based on the presence of ascites, low platelets, and spider angiomata
