# Definition
# Etiology
- Autosomal recessive disorder of copper metabolism caused by a mutation in the atp7b gene on chromosome 13
- Atp7b normally incorporates copper into ceruloplasmin and excretes excess copper into bile
- Absence leads to copper accumulation in the liver, causing free radical damage, and eventually "spilling" copper into the plasma to deposit in the brain, cornea, kidneys, and joints
# Epidemiology
- Mean age of onset is 12 to 23 years
# Prognosis
# Investigations
- Key markers include low serum ceruloplasmin, high 24-hour urinary copper excretion, and the presence of Kayser-Fleischer rings on a slit lamp exam the primary
# Clinical features
- Liver involvement includes cirrhosis and a high risk of HCC
- SNA features involve the basal ganglia, leading to parkinsonian symptoms, tremors, dyskinesia, and behavioral changes
- Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia
- Kayser-Fleischer rings (corneal copper deposits in Descemet's membrane)
# Treatment 
- Treatment is penicillamine, which binds copper to promote urinary excretion
