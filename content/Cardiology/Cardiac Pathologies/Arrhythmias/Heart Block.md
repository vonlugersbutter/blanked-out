Arrhythmia where electrical pathways between atria and ventricles are blocked. 
## First-degree AV block
PQ interval prolonged, longer than 0.2 s. All P waves and QRS complexes preserved
- Transient reversible influence or structural defect
	- Reversible = vagal tone, AV node ischemia, beta blockers, Ca++ channel antagonists, digitalis
	- Structural = infarction, aging 
- Usually benign

## Second-degree AV block
Some of the action potentials from the SA node not conducted to the AV. Dropped beats, fewer QRS complexes.
### Type I (Wenckebach) block
![](00%20media/{5E23EA3A-0DB3-4FF2-917D-2CD007AA656D}.png)
- PR interval gradually delayed until there is a dropped beat
- Impaired conduction in AV node
- Treatment may not be necessary, but might have to administer atropine or isoproterenol 
### Type II block
![](00%20media/{9A20B44D-DB47-4946-AE27-87294389D5BF}.png)
- Sudden, intermittent loss of AV conduction without lengthening 
- PR interval will be regular! 
- High-grade AV block = when it is for two or more beats
- QRS pattern often widened 
- Extensive MI in the septum or from chronic degeneration of the His-Purkinje system 
- Usually requires pacemaker 
- Below is high-grade AV block

## Third-degree (complete) AV block
![](00%20media/{DB6661BD-428A-4A8C-8B0F-A27CED64BFE1}.png)
No SA node impulses get to the AV node. Autorhythmic fibers pace chambers separately. Irregular PR interval.
- Acute MI, chronic degeneration of conduction pathways
- AV dissociation 
- Widened QRS complex because it originates in the distal ventricular conduction system
