the parathyroid glands are four endocrine glands formed by the 3rd and 4th pharyngeal pouches, located behind the thyroid gland their primary function is to secrete parathyroid hormone (pth), which is essential for maintaining calcium and phosphate homeostasis pth is a protein hormone synthesized by the chief cells of the parathyroid gland and acts by binding to cell surface receptors in the bone and kidneys

effects of parathyroid hormone the net effects of pth include increasing plasma calcium, decreasing plasma phosphate, and increasing urinary phosphate these results are achieved through both direct action and the indirect activation of vitamin d

- kidneys = in the distal convoluted tubule (dct), pth increases calcium resorption in the proximal convoluted tubule (pct), it decreases phosphate resorption, leading to phosphaturia, and stimulates the production of active 1,25-(oh)2 vitamin d
    
- gi tract = it indirectly increases calcium and phosphate absorption through the action of activated vitamin d
    
- bone = pth stimulates both bone resorption and formation, with the dominant effect depending on dosage and timing continuous administration leads to bone resorption and increased serum calcium, while low-dose, once-daily boluses (such as the drug teriparatide) increase bone mass
    

regulation of pth secretion pth is secreted in response to low serum calcium (the fastest and major stimulus), high plasma phosphate, and low levels of active vitamin d calcium-sensing receptors (casrs) on the glands inhibit pth release when activated by calcium magnesium also plays a role = mild decreases in magnesium stimulate pth release, but very low magnesium levels inhibit pth release because some magnesium is required for normal casr function

clinical disorders of the parathyroid

- primary hyperparathyroidism = usually caused by a parathyroid adenoma, this involves inappropriate pth secretion not triggered by low calcium symptoms are traditionally described as "stones, bones, groans, and psychiatric overtones," including kidney stones, bone pain, constipation, and anxiety osteitis fibrosa cystica is the classic bone disease associated with this condition, characterized by subperiosteal bone resorption and "brown tumors"
    
- secondary and tertiary hyperparathyroidism = secondary hyperparathyroidism occurs in renal failure as a response to chronically low calcium tertiary hyperparathyroidism occurs when the parathyroid becomes autonomous after chronic renal failure, leading to very high pth and elevated calcium
    
- familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia (fhh) = a rare autosomal dominant disorder involving abnormal casrs, resulting in a higher-than-normal set point for calcium, leading to high serum calcium but low urinary calcium
    
- hypoparathyroidism = characterized by low pth and low calcium, often due to accidental surgical excision or autoimmune conditions like autoimmune polyendocrine syndrome type 1 (aps-1) digeorge syndrome (thymic aplasia) also causes hypoparathyroidism due to the failure of the 3rd and 4th pharyngeal pouches to form symptoms include tetany, chvostek's sign, and trousseau's sign
    
- pseudohypoparathyroidism = a group of disorders where kidneys and bones are unresponsive to pth, often due to impaired g-protein signaling albright's hereditary osteodystrophy (aho) is a form of this, presenting with short stature and shortened 4th and 5th metacarpals
