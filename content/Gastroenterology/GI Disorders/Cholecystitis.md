# Definition
- Acute inflammation of the gallbladder 
# Etiology
- Usually caused by an obstructing [Cholelithiasis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Cholelithiasis.md)
- Presence of gallstones → duct obstruction → distension and inflammation
- Secondary bacterial infection could be present but not necessary, usually *E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, Enterobacter, Enterococcus
- Could also be an acalculous cholecystitis 
## Acalculous cholecystitis
- Life-threatening, usually seen in those that are critically ill
- Bile stasis, reduced perfusion to the gallbladder
- Multiorgan failure, severe trauma, surgery, infection, sepsis 
- Critically ill patient with fever and RUQ tenderness
- Management: supportive, NPO, IV fluids, analgesics, IV antibiotics 
## Emphysematous cholecystitis
- Life-threatening, cholecystitis caused by air within the gallbladder from a gas-producing bacterium 
- Usually in older men with diabetes
- Vascular compromise in the gallbladder → ischemia → necrosis → proliferation of the bacteria
- Associated with gangrene and gallbladder perforation
- Use IV antibiotics, broad-spectrum, use emergency laparoscopic cholecystectomy or gallbladder drainage 
# Epidemiology
- Common in middle-aged females  
# Clinical features
- RUQ pain, especially greater than 6 hours, especially after eating, radiation to the right scapula due to phrenic nerve 
- Positive Murphy's sign → sudden pause on inspiration when palpating RUQ, see [Abdominal Exam](Clinical%20Exams/Abdominal%20Exam.md)
- Fever, malaise, nausea, vomiting
# Investigations
- Diagnostic criteria 
	- Local signs of inflammation
		- Murphy sign
		- RUQ pain, tenderness, mass
	- Systemic inflammation
		- Fever
		- CRP ↑ 
		- Leukocytosis
	- Imaging findings 
		- Wall thickening, distension, edema
- Suspected diagnosis = ≥ 1 local sign of inflammation AND ≥ 1 systemic sign of inflammation
- Definite diagnosis = ≥ 1 local sign of inflammation AND ≥ 1 sign of systemic inflammation AND any characteristic imaging finding

- Lab studies, RUQ U/S 
- Use CT, MRI, MRCP, or HIDA scan if unsure about diagnosis 
	- HIDA scan = hepatobiliary iminodiacetic acid scintigraphy 
- Assess severity by looking at involvement of AKI, any coagulopathies 
- Rule out related biliary comorbidities like looking at LFTs
	- Signs of cholestasis aren't usually present
	- Check lipase and amylase for [Acute Pancreatitis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Acute%20Pancreatitis.md)
- RUQ U/S is preferred, could conduct Murphy's sign with the probe 
- MRI and CT if there are inconclusive findings 
## Severity
- Tokyo guidelines 
- Grade I (mild)
	- No evidence of severe disease
- Grade II (moderate)
	- Symptom duration >72 hours
	- WBC count >18K
	- Palpable tender RUQ mass
	- Signs of a lot of local inflammation
- Grade III (severe)
	- Organ system dysfunction
	- Could be cardiovascular, neurologic, respiratory, renal, hepatic, or hematologic 
# Treatment 
- Empiric antibiotics and cholecystectomy
- Make sure there is no sepsis, shock, assess surgical risk 
- Laparoscopic cholecystectomy, or gallbladder drainage (percutaneous cholecystectomy)
# Prognosis
- Gangrenous cholecystitis
	- Ischemic necrosis of the gallbladder, will see floating membranes, same treatment as normal 
- Gallbladder perforation
	- Ischemic necrosis followed by break in the wall, same treatment
- Gasllbladder empyema = pus-filled
- Chronic cholecystitis = less severe, usually self-limiting, could develop porcelain gallbladder, which is a risk for [Biliary Cancer](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Biliary%20Cancer.md)
