# Definition
- MEN syndromes are rare, autosomal dominant genetic disorders where germline mutations lead to tumors in multiple endocrine glands
# Etiology
- MEN type 1 = an inherited disorder involving tumors of the pituitary, parathyroid, and pancreas (including insulinomas and glucagonomas)
	- Pituitary adenomas (most commonly prolactinomas), parathyroid adenomas (the first finding in ~90% of patients), and pancreatic-duodenal neuroendocrine tumors (most commonly gastrinomas)
	- Caused by a mutation in the men1 gene on chromosome 11, which codes for the tumor suppressor protein menin
- MEN 2a and 2b = both involve medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) and pheochromocytoma
	- Caused by gain-of-function germline mutations in the ret proto-oncogene on chromosome 10
	- MEN 2a = specifically includes parathyroid hyperplasia alongside MTC and pheochromocytoma
	- MEN 2b = characterized by MTC, pheochromocytoma, and two key physical findings
		- Mucosal neuromas (on lips/tongue) and a marfanoid body habitus
		- Unlike men 2A, it usually does not involve the parathyroid glands
