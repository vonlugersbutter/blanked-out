# Oral cavity
- Lips and cheeks = contain muscles (orbicularis oris and buccinator) that keep food between the teeth during chewing (mastication)
	- Masseter, temporalis, medial pterygoid, lateral pterygoid all from motor fibers of mandibular nerve (branch of trigeminal CN V)

![](00%20media/Pasted%20image%2020260120171604.png)

- Tongue = skeletal muscle forming the floor of the oral cavity; maneuvers food into a soft, flexible mass called a bolus for swallowing
	- The dorsum contains papillae, some housing taste buds.
	- Lingual glands secrete lingual lipase, an enzyme that begins triglyceride digestion, though it is activated later in the acidic environment of the stomach
- Teeth (dentes) = accessory digestive organs anchored by the periodontal ligament
	- The internal structure is primarily dentin (calcified connective tissue)
	- The crown is covered by enamel, the hardest substance in the body
	- Humans have two sets: 20 deciduous (primary) teeth and 32 permanent (secondary) teeth
	- Incisors cut, canines tear/shred, and molars/premolars crush and grind food
## Salivary glands
- Parasympathetic innervation promotes secretion
- Parotid glands = serous (watery) fluid
	- Salivary amylase, some kallikrein (family of serine proteases)
- Submandibular glands = mixed serous and mucus 
	- Salivary amylase, also kallikrein, lysozyme, lactoperoxidase (antimicrobial), and lactoferrin (also antimicrobial)
- Sublingual glands = thicker mucus-rich, minimal amylase
	- Lingual lipase, other stuff
- Modification = initial fluid is isotonic (produced by acinar cells) ductal cells (impermeable to water) remove Na and Cl while secreting K and HCO3- (bicarbonate), making the final saliva hypotonic
- Flow rate = at higher flow rates, there is less time for modification, so saliva becomes more like plasma (closer to isotonic) and bicarbonate concentrations increase
- Aldosterone = increases Na absorption and K secretion in salivary glands, similar to its effect on the kidneys
- Control = saliva production is increased by both sympathetic and parasympathetic systems (parasympathetic is the major system) and is activated by the smell or sight of food muscarinic antagonists (eg, atropine) cause dry mouth, while agonists (eg, pilocarpine) increase saliva
	- M1 and M3 receptors 
	- Pilocarpine, used in Sjogren's syndrome (dry eyes and dry mouth)
	- Cholinesterase poisoning, salivation → from organophosphates 
- Also contains IgA for mucosal immunity 
- Sialolithiasis = stones obstructing salivary flow, most common in submandibular glands and aggravated by eating
- Sialadenitis = inflammation often due to staph aureus or anaerobes, frequently secondary to a stone
	- Nafcillin (Staph coverage) or metronidazole or clindamycin (anaerobes)
- Mumps = an RNA virus causing bilateral parotitis
- Tumors
	- Pleomorphic adenoma = the most common salivary tumor; a painless, mobile, benign mixed tumor containing epithelial and stromal (cartilage/bone) cells
	- Warthin’s tumor = the second most common; key risk factor is smoking (8x risk) histologically characterized by fluid-filled cysts surrounded by dense lymphoid infiltrate
	- Mucoepidermoid carcinoma = the most common malignant salivary tumor; contains a mixture of squamous and mucus-secreting cells and can invade the facial nerve
# Pharynx and Deglutition
- Pharynx = muscular contractions propel the bolus to the esophagus
- Deglutition (Swallowing) = moves the bolus from the mouth to the stomach in three stages:
	- Voluntary stage = bolus is moved to the oropharynx
	- Pharyngeal Stage (involuntary) = soft palate/uvula close off the nasopharynx, the epiglottis seals the larynx, and the bolus enters the esophagus
	- Esophageal Stage (involuntary) = the bolus is pushed toward the stomach via peristalsis (coordinated contractions/relaxations of the muscularis)
- Oropharyngeal stages
	- Tongue moves the bolus backward; the soft palate elevates to close the nasopharynx
	- Upper pharyngeal muscle contracts, forcing the bolus into the pharyngeal channel
	- Distal pharynx relaxes, the UES opens, and the glottis closes the trachea to protect the airway
