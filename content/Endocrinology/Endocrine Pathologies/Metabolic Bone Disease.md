# Osteoporosis
- Osteoporosis is characterized by low bone mass and microarchitectural disruption, leading to fragility fractures
- Primary osteoporosis includes postmenopausal and age-related types, while secondary osteoporosis results from disorders like thyrotoxicosis or glucocorticoid excess
- Deprivation of estrogen increases bone turnover by releasing constraints on cytokines like IL-6, which stimulates osteoclast production It also leads to low OPG secretion, further enhancing bone resorption
# Rickets and osteomalacia
- Defective mineralization of the bone matrix
- In children (rickets), features include growth retardation, "beaded" swellings at costochondral junctions (rachitic rosary), and flaring of long bones
- Common causes include vitamin D deficiency, malnutrition, malabsorption, and renal phosphate-wasting syndromes
# Paget disease (osteitis deformans)
- Focal disorder of bone remodeling with accelerated rates of bone turnover
- Asymptomatic, but can cause pain, bone bowing (anterior/lateral femur or tibia), and fractures
- Markedly increased blood flow to pagetic bone can rarely cause high-output congestive [Heart Failure](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Heart%20Failure.md)
# Primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT)
- See [Hyperparathyroidism](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Hyperparathyroidism.md)
- Stones, bones, groans, moans, psychiatric overtones 
- Ca++ ↑, PTH ↑ 
# Renal and hepatic bone disease
- Renal osteodystrophy = chronic renal failure leads to phosphate retention and decreased 1,25(OH)₂D production, triggering secondary hyperparathyroidism and osteitis fibrosa
- Hepatic osteodystrophy = usually manifests as osteoporosis, as 25(OH)D production is only rate-limiting in severe liver failure
# Genetic and Matrix Disorders
- Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) = heritable disorder of type I collagen causing bone deformity and fractures
- Hypophosphatasia = rare defect in the tissue-nonspecific alkaline phosphatase gene, leading to high pyrophosphate levels (a mineralization inhibitor) and osteomalacia
- Fibrous dysplasia = results from activating mutations in the GNAS gene; lesions can overproduce FGF23, leading to hypophosphatemia
