# Definition
- Pregnancy outside the uterus, 98% occur in fallopian tube, most commonly ampulla 
# Etiology
## Risk factors
- Damage to fallopian tube
- Prior ectopic pregnancy
- Tubal disorders
- Tubal ligation (rarely pregnancy occurs)
- Tubal surgery (tumor)
- [Pelvic Inflammatory Disease](13%20Reproduction/Reproductive%20Pathologies/Pelvic%20Inflammatory%20Disease.md)
- Infertile women: higher incidence, use of ARTs
- Smoking
- [Contraceptives](13%20Reproduction/Contraceptives.md) failure 
	- IUD is 50% ectopic if pregnancy occurs, also risk of PID during insertion 
- Kartagener syndrome (1° ciliary dyskinesia)
## Pathogenesis
- Because the fallopian tube lacks a submucosal layer, the fertilized ovum burrows promptly into the muscularis
- Potential outcomes
	- Tubal Rupture: Spontaneous or occasionally triggered by coitus/examination, leading to life-threatening hemorrhage
	- Tubal Abortion: Passage out of the distal end of the tube, which may resolve symptoms or lead to hematosalpinx or secondary abdominal pregnancy
	- Spontaneous Failure: Death and reabsorption of the pregnancy
- Acute vs. Chronic: Acute ectopic pregnancies grow rapidly with high β-hCG levels and higher rupture risk. Chronic cases involve early trophoblast death, low/static β-hCG levels, and the formation of a complex pelvic mass.
# Epidemiology

# Clinical features
- Symptoms in 1st trimester
	- Vaginal bleeding
	- Abdominal pain (may mimic appendicitis)
	- Abnormal ↑hCG based on dates
- Classic Triad: Amenorrhea followed by pain and vaginal bleeding
- Symptoms of Rupture: Severe, sharp, stabbing, or tearing lower abdominal/pelvic pain
- Referred Pain: Diaphragmatic irritation from hemoperitoneum can cause neck or shoulder pain, especially on inspiration
- Physical Findings: Abdominal tenderness and sometimes a palpable mass. The uterus may be slightly enlarged due to hormonal stimulation
- Laboratory Indicators: A trending decline in hemoglobin or hematocrit is a valuable index of blood loss. Leukocytosis (up to 30,000/μL) occurs in about half of women with tubal rupture
## Types
### Interstitial pregnancy
- Implantation = The pregnancy implants in the proximal tubal segment located within the muscular uterine wall
- Characteristics = These often rupture later than other tubal pregnancies because the surrounding myometrium is more distensible
- Sonographic Criteria = Findings include a gestational sac located >1 cm from the endometrium, covered by less than a 5-mm muscular mantle, and the "interstitial line sign"—an echogenic line extending from the sac to the endometrial cavity
### Cesarean scar pregnancy (CSP)
- Definition = Implantation occurs within the scar of a previous cesarean section
- Risks = These are associated with high rates of uterine rupture or dehiscence (10%) and high recurrence rates in subsequent pregnancies
- Diagnosis = Suggested by an intrauterine mass in the anterior uterine isthmus with an empty cervical canal and an empty uterine cavity
### Ovarian and heterotopic pregnancies
- Ovarian Pregnancy = A rare condition diagnosed using the Spiegelberg criteria, which require an intact ipsilateral tube, a pregnancy occupying the ovary connected to the uterus by the ovarian ligament, and histologically confirmed ovarian tissue in the placenta
- Heterotopic Pregnancy = This involves a co-existing intrauterine and extrauterine pregnancy While rare naturally (1 in 30,000), the risk increases significantly with Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART)
# Investigations
## Serum hCG
- In a robust intrauterine pregnancy (IUP), $\beta$-hCG levels typically rise by at least 35 to 53 percent every 48 hours
- Ectopic Pregnancy Patterns = While a normal rise can sometimes be seen in ectopic cases, inadequately rising levels generally indicate a nonviable pregnancy but do not specify its location
## Transvaginal sonography
- Primary Findings = Sonographic diagnosis of an ectopic pregnancy requires seeing a mass separate from the ovary This may appear as an inhomogeneous adnexal mass, an empty extrauterine sac with a hyperechoic ring, or a sac containing a yolk sac and/or fetal pole
- "Ring of Fire" = Color Doppler may show a "ring of fire," representing placental blood flow around the periphery, though this can also be seen in corpus luteum cysts
- Pseudogestational Sac = A circular fluid collection located centrally within the endometrial cavity can mimic a gestational sac but is characteristic of ectopic pregnancy
## Physical findings
- Shock Index = This index, calculated as heart rate divided by systolic blood pressure, helps assess for hypovolemic shock
	- Shock index >0.85 and a systolic blood pressure <110 mmHg strongly suggest a life-threatening emergency, such as a ruptured ectopic pregnancy
- Fluid Detection = TVS can detect peritoneal fluid in the posterior cul-de-sac
- Significant hemorrhage is indicated if fluid is found in the paracolic gutters or the Morison pouch (between the liver and kidney)
## Culdocentesis
- Spinal needle is inserted through the posterior vaginal fornix into the cul-de-sac to check for fluid
- Interpretation = Clear fluid is a negative result; nonclotting blood indicates hemoperitoneum; purulent fluid suggests infection; and feculent material may indicate bowel perforation
- Largely replaced by TVS
## Endometrium
- Decidual Changes = Ectopic pregnancies can cause endometrial changes like decidua (42%), secretory endometrium (22%), or proliferative endometrium (12%) in the absence of trophoblasts
- Decidual Cast = A woman may pass a decidual cast, which is the entire sloughed endometrium that takes the shape of the uterine cavity
	- Histological evaluation for chorionic villi is necessary to differentiate this from an incomplete intrauterine abortion

# Prognosis

# Treatment 
- Methotrexate (MTX) is a common medical treatment, though its success is monitored through serial $\beta$ -hCG levels
	- Folic acid antagonist, used against rapidly proliferating trophoblasts
	- Side effects = Liver involvement (12%), stomatitis (6%), and gastroenteritis (1%). Separation pain occurs in 65–75% of patients several days after therapy
	- Patients must avoid folic acid supplements, NSAIDs, alcohol, sunlight (photosenstivity caused by impaired healing of basal cells), and sexual activity. Future pregnancy should be delayed for 3 months post-treatment
- Surgery = Laparoscopy is the preferred surgical approach unless the patient is hemodynamically unstable For unique locations like cervical or scar pregnancies, surgical excision is often required
	- Salpingectomy: Removal of the entire fallopian tube; used for both ruptured and unruptured pregnancies.
	- Salpingostomy: A linear incision is made to extract the pregnancy, leaving the tube to heal by secondary intention
	- Persistent Trophoblast: Complicates 5 to 15 percent of salpingostomy cases. If β-hCG levels remain stable or rise post-operatively, MTX is usually administered.
