# Definition
Rare clinical condition (not entirely sure why this one is singled out on our exam). Dyspnea and hypoxemia worsening in the **upright** position and getting better in the **supine** position. Basically a symptom of an underlying disease. 
# Etiology
Usually associated with intracardiac shunting (PFO, ASD, ASA, congenital cardiomyopathy). Or extracardiac shunting, like in pulmonary arteriovenous malformation (PAVM), hepatopulmonary syndrome, [ARDS](09%20Respirology/Respiratory%20Pathologies/ARDS.md). V/Q mismatches from other conditions can also contribute to this. PFO's the most common cause, essentially getting arterial to venous shunting of blood, contributing to hypoxemia. When sitting upright, there's more pressure on the right atrium, which amplifies this. Same thing can be said for the PAVM → more of them are usually found in the lung bases, so sitting upright would send more blood to those malformations.

# Investigations
Cardiac auscultation could show murmurs in patients with heart abnormalities. PFO might not show up in this, though. Abnormal breath sounds, cyanosis, clubbing may all be present.

If it's hepatic, signs like spider nevi (bright red, web-like markings on the skin caused by a central, dilated blood vessel with radiating capillaries that resembles a spider), palmar erythema (red, warm palms → associated with pregnancy and liver cirrhosis), ascites, and anasarca (generalized edema) may develop. 
# Clinical features
Hypoxemia in the upright position, improving in the reclining position.
