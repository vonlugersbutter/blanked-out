# Definition
- An autoimmune (type IV hypersensitivity) destruction of beta cells
# Etiology
- Associated with HLA-DR3 and HLA-DR4
# Epidemiology

# Clinical features
- Polyuria, polydipsia, and weight loss
- Fruity odor on breath, ↑ ketones 
- Kussmaul breathing = deep, rapid, and labored breathing pattern that acts as a compensatory mechanism for severe metabolic acidosis
# Investigations
- Glucose ↑
- Check kidney function → creatinine, microalbumin to creatinine ratio
- EKG, chest x-ray 
- Sodium needs to be corrected based on glucose level → high blood sugar (hyperglycemia) draws water out of cells into the bloodstream, diluting sodium and causing a falsely low measurement (pseudohyponatremia)
# Prognosis
- End-stage renal disease is more common in type 1
- [Hypoglycemia](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Hypoglycemia.md) = symptoms include tachycardia, sweating, and tremors (at ~54 mg/dl), progressing to confusion and headache (~50 mg/dl) and eventually seizures or coma
- Stage 1: Early Autoimmunity (Presymptomatic)
	- Definition: Two or more diabetes-related autoantibodies present
	- Blood Sugar: Normal (Normoglycemic)
	- Symptoms: None
	- Risk: Lifetime risk of developing T1DM approaches 100%
- Stage 2: Dysglycemia (Presymptomatic)
	- Definition: Two or more autoantibodies present, along with abnormal blood sugar levels (glucose intolerance/dysglycemia)
	- Blood Sugar: Rising glucose levels due to significant beta-cell loss
	- Symptoms: None (asymptomatic)
	- Note: A 2-week course of the drug teplizumab has been shown to delay the onset of clinical T1DM in this stage
- Stage 3: Clinical Diagnosis (Symptomatic)
	- Definition: Significant beta-cell destruction
	- Blood Sugar: High (hyperglycemia), usually diagnosed with an HbA1C > 6.5%
	- Classic signs appear, including polyuria (frequent urination), polydipsia (excessive thirst), weight loss, and fatigue.
## Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
- Driven by low insulin and high glucagon, catecholamines, and cortisol this leads to high levels of ketone bodies (3- $\beta$ -hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate, and acetone)
- Treatment involves IV fluids, insulin, monitor potassium levels 
- Cerebral edema → too much fluid going into the cells, because of excessive fluid correction 
- Note that hepatocytes lack the enzyme succinyl-CoA acetoacetyl-CoA transferase (thiophorase), which is necessary for the conversion of acetoacetate to acetyl-CoA (and the downstream production of ATP and GTP from ketone bodies)
	- Hepatocytes **produce** ketone bodies for use by other peripheral tissues 
	- Also if there isn't enough glucose, they will use fatty acids 
- Total body K+ is ↓↓, while serum K+ may be normal or even high
	- Because of osmotic diuresis, no insulin to cause intracellular shift, RAAS ↑, ketoanions get secreted with Na+ and K+, vomiting also loses K+
- Insulin given → K+ rushes into cells → could lead to fatal arrhythmias if no K+ given 
### Mechanism behind ketone production
- Absolute or relative insulin deficiency coupled with high counter-regulatory hormones (glucagon, epinephrine), forcing the liver to switch from glucose metabolism to excessive fat breakdown (lipolysis)
- Massive amounts of FFAs in the liver, converted to to form acetyl-CoA
# Treatment 
- Insulin hexamers = in the body, insulin naturally forms stable hexamers monomers are absorbed faster than hexamers, which influences the onset of action for different preparations
- Types of insulin
	- Rapid acting (lispro, aspart, glulisine) = modified amino acid structure prevents hexamer formation, leading to rapid onset (15 min) and short duration (2-4 hrs); typically used pre-meal
	- Regular insulin = short-acting; the only type given intravenously (iv) for DKA/HHS or hyperkalemia
	- Intermediate acting (NPH) = human insulin combined with protamine to slow absorption; duration is 12-16 hours
	- Long acting (glargine, detemir) = used for background (basal) therapy; glargine precipitates at body pH to provide a steady level for up to 24 hours, while detemir binds to albumin
- Side effects = primary side effects of insulin therapy are hypoglycemia (which can cause tremors, palpitations, or coma) and weight gain
