# Definition
- Infection of upper genital tract, including endometritis, salpingitis and peritonitis → involves endometrium and/or fallopian tubes 
# Etiology
- *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* and *C. trachomatis* are the two most common pathogens 
- Other organisms like E. coli, group B streptococci can also be isolated, difficult to determine exact cause in individual cases
- Vaginal douching, multiple sexual partners 
# Epidemiology
- Becoming less common
- Dilation and curettage or C section can ↑ risk 
- Menstruation is a risk factor for ascending infection from cervix and vagina
- OCP use and tubal sterilization ↓ risk 
# Clinical features
- Women with gonococcal PID present with lower abdominal pain, abnormal uterine bleeding, dyspareunia (pain with intercourse) and fever. Although mortality from PID is low, morbidity is extremely high
	- Important risk factor for chronic pelvic pain, [Infertility](13%20Reproduction/Reproductive%20Pathologies/Infertility.md), tubal pregnancies
- Endometritis = inflammation of the uterine lining (endometrium) → lower abdominal pain, abnormal discharge and bleeding 
- Salpingitis = dull or aching pain, cervical motion tenderness when stretching adnexal attachments 
	- Can present with fever, ↑ ESR, ↑ WBC 
- Perihepatitis and periappendicitis = RUQ pain in some women → could be mistaken for [Cholecystitis](10%20Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Cholecystitis.md) 
# Investigations
- Difficult to confirm in some cases → laparoscopy is needed to fully confirm salpingitis, but will do empiric treatment anyway 
# Prognosis
- [Infertility](13%20Reproduction/Reproductive%20Pathologies/Infertility.md), [Ectopic Pregnancy](13%20Reproduction/Reproductive%20Pathologies/Ectopic%20Pregnancy.md) 
# Treatment 
- Ceftriaxone to cover possible gonococcal infection
- Doxycycline to cover possible chlamydial infection 
- CDC STD treatment guidelines = empirical treatment for PID in sexually active young women and other women at risk for PID if: experiencing pelvic or lower abdominal pain, if no other cause for the pain can be identified, and if pelvic examination reveals one or more of the following criteria: cervical motion tenderness, uterine tenderness, or adnexal tenderness
- Chronic PID = Defined as PID symptoms persisting for more than 30 days
- Actinomyces israelii = Identified as a significant cause of chronic PID It is a gram-positive, slow-growing anaerobic bacterium

- Should show improvement in 3-5 days, if not consider laparoscopy 
- Surgery for salpingitis if there is life-threatening infection (rupture of tuboovarian abscess) or abscess drainage 
	- Posterior colpotomy, peritoneal lavage for generalized peritonitis
