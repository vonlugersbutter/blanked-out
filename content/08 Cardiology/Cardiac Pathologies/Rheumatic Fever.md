# Definition
Inflammatory condition, affects the heart, skin, connective tissues.  
# Etiology
Complication of pharyngitis from group A beta-hemolytic (a complete destruction of red blood cells) streptococci. Develops 2-3 weeks after initial throat infection. 

Autoimmune cross-reactivity between bacterial and cardiac antigens. Histology shows Aschoff bodies = fibrinoid necrosis and inflammatory cells. 

![](00%20media/{8C16D88B-C86A-49FF-AAA8-4761193010BF}.png)

Causes tachycardia, impaired contractility, pericardial friction rub, transient heart murmurs → all because of that turbulent flow on the valve. 
# Epidemiology
Largely a burden in developing countries. Children and young adults. 
# Prognosis
- Chronic rheumatoid heart diseases (RHD) characterized by permanent deformity, 10-30 years after rheumatic fever 
# Investigations
- Echocardiogram
# Clinical features
- Chills
- Fever
- Fatigue
- Migratory arthritis 
## Jones' criteria
Diagnosis requires two major criteria or one major plus two minor. 
### Major criteria
Carditis (inflammation of all three heart layers)
Migratory arthritis (mainly large joints)
Sydenham chorea (involuntary movements)
Erythema marginatum (skin rash with advancing edge and clearing center)
Subcutaneous nodules
### Minor criteria
Arthralgias
Fever
Elevated acute-phase reactants (ESR, CRP)
Prolonged PR interval on electrocardiogram
### Evidence of group A streptococcal infection
Antistreptolysin O antibodies
Positive throat culture or rapid antigen test
# Treatment
- High dose aspirin, reduce inflammation
- Penicillin to kill off streptococcal infection

Getting strep as a kid → treat with antibiotics → will protect against rheumatic fever but not glomeluar nephritis
