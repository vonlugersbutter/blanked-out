The hypothalamus is a critical region of the diencephalon located below the thalamus, forming the floor and walls of the third ventricle. It serves as a command centre, integrating environmental cues, signals from other brain regions, and visceral afferents to maintain homeostasis. it coordinates physiological responses including energy expenditure, fluid balance, body temperature, and the sleep cycle.

the hypothalamus is organized into nuclei, which are clusters of neurons with extensive projections. two specific types of neurohormonal neurons are essential for endocrine function:

- magnocellular neurons: located in the supraoptic (son) and paraventricular (pvn) nuclei, these large neurons have long axons forming the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract. this tract traverses the median eminence and terminates in the posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis), where the hormones oxytocin (ot) and arginine vasopressin (avp) are released directly into the systemic circulation.
    
- parvicellular neurons: these smaller neurons project to the median eminence, where they release hypophysiotropic hormones (releasing or inhibiting factors) into the specialized blood supply that carries them to the anterior pituitary.
    

the median eminence is the bridge-like region where the two halves of the hypothalamus rejoin at the floor of the third ventricle. it funnels into the infundibular stalk, which connects to the pituitary gland. in practical terms, the posterior pituitary is considered an extension of the hypothalamus
