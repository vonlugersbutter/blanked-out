# Composition of blood
- Liquid connective tissue, where the ECM is plasma
- Interstitial fluid is the fluid that bathes body cells → constantly renewed by blood 
- Functions: transportation, regulation, protection

We start as we always do: with lovely diagrams from Tortora. Note the Buffy coat, the various proteins, cells, and solutes found throughout.
![](00%20media/{BD694438-F9E4-480B-8E6E-DCC52119F8F2}.png)

* Note the blood plasma (55%), buffy coat of WBCs and platelets, and RBCs (45%)  
* The hematocrit is this portion of the blood volume, or the percentage of total blood volume occupied by RBCs  
	* This is usually higher in males, because testosterone leads to more EPO production  
	* Polycythemia is when there are too many RBCs, and the hematocrit is \>65%. This leads to more resistance to flow, and HTN  
* Note also the various dissolved elements throughout  
  * Hepatocytes synthesize most plasma proteins, such as albumins, globulins, and fibrinogen
# Hematopoiesis
Okay, we get down to tacks of brass (Rise of the Guardians reference; they should make another movie). How exactly is blood made, along with all of its constituents?
## Embryonic hematopoiesis
- Embryonic yolk sac → stem cells in the aorta-gonads-mesonephros → liver and spleen until 6-7 months fetal age → bone marrow then takes over
## Bone marrow
* In infancy all bone marrow is hematopoietic, but then the marrow gets replaced by fat cells    
* Hematopoiesis occurs in the bone marrow    
    * Red bone marrow \= spongy, reddish tissue inside certain bones that produces blood cells; found in flat bones like the skull and pelvis, along with the ends of long bones in adulthood    
    * Yellow bone marrow \= fatty tissue found in the cavities of long bones, primarily responsible for fat storage, energy reserves, and containing mesenchymal stem cells    
        * Forms from red bone marrow with age
### Bone marrow transplant
* Replacement of cancerous or abnormal red bone marrow with healthy red bone marrow    
* Donor marrow removed from iliac crest, and then injected into recipient’s veins    
* Used for aplastic anemia, leukemias, SCID, thalassemia, sickle cell disease, hemolytic anemia    
* Patient must be on immunosuppressants to avoid graft-versus-host disease
### Cord blood transplant
* Alternative for obtaining stem cells, removed using syringe and then frozen    
* Easily collected, abundant, less likely to cause graft-versus-host disease, fewer infections, stored indefinitely in banks
## Cell lineages
* Divide into the myeloid and lymphoid lineages, with the appropriate progenitor cells, which then form precursor cells    
* Earlier progenitors can be identified with burst-forming units rather than colony-forming units when grown in culture
* Hematopoietic stem cells are usually dormant, while progenitor cells are numerous and more proliferative
