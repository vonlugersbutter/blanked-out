# Definition
# Etiology
- Iron metabolism = iron is absorbed in the duodenum (heme iron from meat is easily absorbed; non-heme requires a Fe2+ state and vitamin c)
- The body has few mechanisms to excrete iron, primarily losing it through skin sloughing or menstruation in women
- Hereditary hemochromatosis = usually an autosomal recessive disorder involving the HFE gene (chromosome 6), most commonly the homozygous c282y mutation
	- Leads to unregulated iron absorption and gradual accumulation, meaning patients usually present in adulthood (women present later due to menstruation)
- Secondary hemochromatosis = often caused by excessive blood transfusions in patients with chronic hematologic disorders like beta thalassemia major or sickle cell anemia

# Investigations
- Laboratory findings include ferritin >1000 ng/ml and high transferrin saturation diagnosis is confirmed via genetic testing, MRI (where the liver appears black), or biopsy with Prussian blue staining
# Clinical features
- Known as "bronze diabetes," it affects the liver (cirrhosis, HCC risk), pancreas (diabetes), and skin (pigmentation from iron and melanin)
	- Excess iron to deposit in the heart muscle (myocardium), leading to severe cardiac dysfunction
- Can also cause dilated cardiomyopathy, arthropathy, and testicular atrophy
- Alcohol accelerates the associated liver disease
# Treatment 
- Treatment is primarily phlebotomy to remove iron, body's natural processes can re-make blood 
- Iron chelating agents like deferoxamine are rarely used
	- Usually only in secondary hemochromatosis, where the patient is already anemic so it's bad to remove blood components
