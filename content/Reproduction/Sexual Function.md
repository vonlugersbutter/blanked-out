- WHO defines sexual health as a "state of physical, emotional, mental and social well-being related to sexuality"
# Sexual response cycle
- Linear model = initially described as a progression from arousal to a plateau, followed by orgasm/ejaculation and resolution
	- Kaplan later added "desire" to create a three-stage model
- Circular/incentive model = suggests that positive sexual experiences reinforce desire and highlights that emotional intimacy and relationship satisfaction are key incentives, particularly for women
- Physiological basis = the cycle is a motivation/incentive-based process where phases (desire, arousal, orgasm) often overlap and occur in variable orders
# Female function and dysfunction
## Physiology and hormonal role
- Neurobiology = sexual desire and pleasure result from excitatory networks (dopamine, melanocortins) exceeding inhibitory networks (serotonin, opioids)
- Genital arousal = involves vascular engorgement of the clitoris and vaginal lubrication
- Estrogen = essential for vaginal health estrogen insufficiency (menopause) leads to vulvovaginal atrophy (VVA), causing dryness and dyspareunia (painful sex)
- Testosterone = in women, testosterone is associated with sexual self-image and orgasm
	- Levels decline slowly with age, but there is no specific "androgen deficiency" cut-off for diagnosis
## Female sexual dysfunction (FSD)
- Classification = includes HSDD, female sexual arousal dysfunction (FSAD), and female orgasmic dysfunction (FOD)
	- Hypoactive sexual desire dysfunction (HSDD) = the most common; it involves a lack of motivation for sexual activity that causes distress
		- Diagnosis = requires symptoms to be persistent for several months and associated with significant distress
## Treatment 
- Hormonal therapies
	- Systemic MHT (menopause hormone therapy) = primarily for vasomotor symptoms (hot flushes) but can improve overall well-being and sexual function
	- Local vaginal estrogen = highly effective for VVA and dyspareunia
	- Testosterone therapy = recommended specifically for postmenopausal HSDD
- Non-hormonal therapies
	- Flibanserin = an oral medication for premenopausal HSDD
	- Bremelanotide = an injectable medication for premenopausal HSDD
	- Lifestyle/psychosocial = weight management, pelvic floor therapy, and counseling are vital
# Male function and dysfunction
## Physiology of erection
- Consists of two corpora cavernosa and one corpus spongiosum
- Erection is a biochemical and hemodynamic event involving the relaxation of cavernosal smooth muscle, increased blood flow through the helicine arteries, and venous occlusion (trapping blood against the tunica albuginea)
- Biochemical regulation
	- NO = the primary mediator of relaxation; it activates guanylyl cyclase to produce cGMP, which lowers intracellular calcium
	- Calcium flux = smooth muscle tone is regulated by transmembrane and intracellular calcium flux high intracellular calcium causes contraction (flaccidity); low calcium causes relaxation (erection)
	- PDE5 = this enzyme breaks down cGMP, reversing the erection
## Dysfunction
- Male hypoactive sexual desire dysfunction (MHSDD) = characterized by a persistent lack of sexual fantasies or desire that causes clinically significant distress → often associated with testosterone deficiency, hyperprolactinemia, or certain medications like SSRIs
- Erectile dysfunction (ED) = the inability to attain or maintain an erection sufficient for satisfactory intercourse
	- Risk factors = age, diabetes, hypertension, smoking, and cardiovascular disease
	- Marker of systemic disease = ED often precedes symptoms of [Acute Coronary Syndrome](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Ischemic%20Heart%20Diseases/Acute%20Coronary%20Syndrome.md) by 2 to 3 years and major cardiac events by 3 to 5 years
- Ejaculatory disorders
	- Premature ejaculation (PE) = ejaculation occurring within roughly 1 minute of penetration it is linked to neurotransmitters like serotonin (which delays it) and dopamine (which stimulates it)
	- Delayed ejaculation = an involuntary delay or absence of ejaculation
	- Retrograde ejaculation = semen is propelled backward into the bladder
## Treatment
- First-line = PDE5 inhibitors (eg, sildenafil, tadalafil) require an intact nitric oxide response
	- Psychosexual counseling is also recommended
- Second-line = vacuum constriction devices, intraurethral alprostadil, or intracavernosal injections (ICI) of vasoactive agents
- Third-line = penile prosthesis surgery for patients unresponsive to medical therapies
- Testosterone therapy = indicated for men with hypogonadism
# Endocrine conditions and sexual health
- [Diabetes](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Diabetes/Diabetes.md) = increases risk for ed and low testosterone in men in women, it is associated with FSD due to neuropathy and vascular insufficiency
- Hyperprolactinemia = suppresses GnRH, leading to low testosterone/estrogen and reduced desire in both sexes
- Thyroid disease = hypothyroidism is linked to delayed ejaculation (men) and low desire
	- Hyperthyroidism is strongly associated with premature ejaculation
- medications = many common drugs (SSRIs, beta-blockers, 5 $\alpha$-reductase inhibitors like finasteride) can cause sexual side effects
