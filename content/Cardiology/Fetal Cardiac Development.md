We start with Tortora. 
![](00%20media/{DD06D510-24CA-4C03-8AD7-9D02F774145E}.png)
Forms from cardiogenic area in the mesoderm. Two cell clusters called angioblastic cords, which then turn into cardiogenic cords, then endocardial tubes. These then fuse at the midline, forming a single tube at day 22. 

The tube has the following layers:
- Endothelial lining, becomes endocardium
- Gelatinous connective tissue, or cardiac jelly
- Thick muscular layer from splanchnic mesoderm, becomes myocardium

Primitive heart tube then turns into the following structures, and what they eventually turn into. Listed from superior to inferior. 

| Structure           | Eventual part of organs                                                                  |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Truncus arteriosus  | Ascending aorta, pulmonary trunk                                                         |
| Bulbus cordis       | Right ventricle                                                                          |
| Primitive ventricle | Left ventricle                                                                           |
| Primitive atrium    | Anterior wall of right atrium, right auricle, anterior wall of left atrium, left auricle |
| Sinus venosus       | Posterior wall of right atrium, coronary sinus, SA node                                  |
![300](00%20media/{039E5D0C-82E1-42EC-B2EC-E8D1A6CAF6F3}.png)

Endocardial cushions appear, fuse into the atrioventricular canals between atria and ventricles → these divide into left and right. Interatrial septum moves, fuses with endocardial cushions, form an opening called foramen ovale between left and right atria. The atrioventricular canals eventually house the tricuspid and mitral valves. 
# Septation
![](00%20media/{5C3FE47A-1063-458A-A53A-C806FBB57378}.png)
- Happens around 4 to 6 weeks 
- Septum primum begins at the roof of the common atrium, goes down, and leaves a large opening called the ostium primum 
- Eventually, septum primum fuses, closing over the ostium primum
- There are small perforations on the septum primum, that become the ostium secundum, so the blood flow keeps happening
- Over the right, the septum secundum comes over, and leaves a foramen ovale
- The septum primum regresses from the top, and leaves a flap that only allows right to left flow 
- When birth occurs, pressure increases on the left side, and the foramen ovale closes, eventually forming the fossa ovalis 
- There is also an interventricular foramen, but this closes at 7th week

- During 5th week, the truncus arteriosus gets bulbar ridges that fuse at the midline, and undergo a spiral → this forms the aorticopulmonary septum 
# Valve development
- Semilunar valves start developing right before completion of aorticopulmonary segment
	- Three outgrowths of tissue from the holes, form three cusps 
- Outgrowths develop similarly for the AV valves, and then undergo apoptosis, leaving the chordae tendineae behind 
# Circulation
![](00%20media/{EFD6FAA0-DB91-4059-9851-ED8D05CBB4CF}.png)
Placenta → umbilical vein → ductus venosus (bypassing liver, go straight to IVC) → IVC 
- IVC is mixed well-oxygenated and low oxygen return from systemic veins
- Oxygen content is higher than SVC
- Fetal brain and myocardium get lots of oxygen, and poor oxygen is directed to umbilical arteries
- And IVC opening is right next to the foramen ovale (crista dividens, which is bottom of septum secundum, is really close )

IVC → RA → foramen ovale → LA 
- Mixes with small, deoxygenated blood coming in from the lungs 
- Remember that the lungs aren't doing ventilation, they're actually using oxygen 

LA → LV → aorta 
- Then, it goes into the coronary arteries, carotid and subclavian, and then to descending aorta and rest of body 

RA → RV → to the lungs through pulmonary arteries or to aorta (majority) through ductus arteriosus 
- Bypassing lungs, mostly 
- High pulmonary vasculature pressure because of lack of oxygenation allows for shunting of blood into aorta through ductus arteriosus 
# Transition from fetal to neonate
- Umbilical cord clamps or naturally constricts → ↑ SVR
- Pulmonary vascular resistance ↓ 
	- Inflation of lungs, lung tissue stretch, pulmonary artery expansion and wall thinning 
	- Vasodilation of vasculature upon oxygenation 
- So pressure on the right side is now lower, while pressure on the left side is higher 
- Less IVC inflow, constriction of the ductus venosus → foramen ovale shuts 
- Ductus arteriosus also closes
	- During fetus → ↑ PGE1 because hypoxia → smooth muscle relaxation of ductus ↑ , keeping it relaxed
	- Birth → ↓ PGE1 → ductus constricts 
- LV hypertrophies, RV recedes in wall thickness
