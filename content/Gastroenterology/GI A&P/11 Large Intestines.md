- Contains only crypts interspersed with surface epithelium; haustra (folds) and a larger diameter support its reservoir function
- Reservoir for waste and a site for fluid reclamation and salvage of dietary by-products (e.g., short-chain fatty acids) via the commensal microbiome
- Regions = cecum, colon (ascending, transverse, descending, sigmoid), rectum, and anal canal
- Muscularis features bands of thickened longitudinal muscle called teniae coli, whose tonic contractions create pouches called haustra
	- Haustral churning, peristalsis, and mass peristalsis (a strong wave that forces contents into the rectum, typically after a meal)
- Epiploic appendages = small, fat-filled pouches of visceral peritoneum hanging off the surface
- Mucosa lacks villi and circular folds but has numerous goblet cells that secrete lots of mucus
- No enzymes, chemical digestion by bacteria
	- Bacteria ferment carbohydrates (releasing flatus), break down amino acids, decompose bilirubin into stercobilin (colouring the feces brown), and synthesize some B vitamins and vitamin K
- Absorption = water (solidifying feces), ions, and vitamins
- Defecation = elimination of feces, typically initiated by a defecation reflex when mass peristalsis pushes feces into the rectum and distends the wall
	- Opens the involuntary internal anal sphincter, while the external anal sphincter is under voluntary control

## SCFAs
- Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), primarily acetate, propionate, and butyrate, are essential metabolites produced when gut microbiota ferment dietary fiber
- Strengthen the gut barrier, regulate immune function, and manage metabolic health
- Diet rich in fibers and Ω-3 fatty acids increases SCFA production by beneficial bacteria, reducing inflammation and risk of disease
- Propionate can go to the liver to aid with glucose production
