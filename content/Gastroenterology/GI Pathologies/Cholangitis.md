# Definition
Bacterial infection of the biliary tract, especially after something like [Cholelithiasis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Cholelithiasis.md).
# Etiology
- Biliary tract obstruction, bacteria comes into the bile ducts, and then ascends the tracts to make its way into the duct and even hepatic ducts 
- Could also be because of a stricture or obstruction, or [Acute Pancreatitis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Acute%20Pancreatitis.md)
- Biliary-enteric fistula could lead to contamination of bile with intestinal contents 
# Epidemiology
- Much more common in women, in patients with existing [Cholelithiasis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Cholelithiasis.md)
- Usually in middle-aged women 
# Clinical features
- Really important → Charcot's triad
	- Abdominal pain (RUQ)
	- Fever 
	- Jaundice 
- Reynolds pentad = Charcot's triad AND hypotension and mental status changes
	- This indicates more severity, as features of sepsis and septic shock may be present → more indicated with severity and mortality
# Investigations
- Making the diagnosis
	- Systemic signs of inflammation
		- Fever and or chills
		- WBC ↑, CRP ↑ 
	- Signs of cholestasis
		- Jaundice 
		- LFTs show cholestatic pattern 
	- Imaging findings 
		- Biliary dilatation 
		- Evidence of underlying etiology, like choledocholithiasis 
- Suspected = 1 sign of inflammation AND 1 sign of cholestasis or imaging finding
- Definite = at least 1 sign from each above category 
- Get CBC, CRP, LFTs, blood cultures, bile cultures during drainage
- Test severity = blood gas analysis, BMP for AKI, PT/INR for coagulopathy 
- Get serum lipase and amylase to differentiate from [Acute Pancreatitis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Acute%20Pancreatitis.md) 
- Imaging
	- RUQ U/S
		- Dilated CBD, dilated intrahepatic duct
		- Evidence of choledocholithiasis 
	- If diagnosis is inconclusive, then you move to CT or MRI (MRCP)
- Grading the severity using Tokyo guidelines 
	- Grade I (mild)
		- No evidence of severe disease
	- Grade II (moderate)
		- Temp >39
		- WBC count <4K or >12K
		- Total bilirubin >5
		- Serum albumin less than 70% of LLN 
	- Grade III (severe)
		- Organ system dysfunction
		- Could be cardiovascular, neurologic, respiratory, renal, hepatic, or hematologic 
# Treatment
- Empiric antibiotic therapy
- Urgent biliary drainage 
	- ERCP-guided treatment → send the bile for culture 
- Stabilize patient, identify and treat choledocholithiasis 
	- ERCP-guided stone extraction
	- After resolution of acute symptoms, you can do cholecystectomy
	- Put a stent if there is a stricture
	- For parasitic infection, use antihelmintics and ERCP-guided parasite extraction
# Prognosis
- Shock, sepsis, multiple organ failure! 
- Pyogenic liver abcess
- Biliary stricture
