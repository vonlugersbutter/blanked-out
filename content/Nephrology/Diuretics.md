# Extra notes
- Normally, transepithelial voltage of LoH → ↑ Ca++ reabsorption
	- Loop diuretics inhibit that, and lead to ↑ Ca++ excretion → used to treat hypercalcemia 
- Diuretics (except for the K+ sparing kind) → inhibit Na+ and H2O reabsorption upstream to the ASDN → ↑ tubular flow → ↑ K+ secretion
	- Also, more reabsorption of those → ↑ aldosterone → ↑ K+ secretion
- Diuretics get in usually through the organic anion and cation transporters, so they may need to compete with other drugs 
- Diuretic braking → administration of diuretic to person with a fixed Na+ intake will lead to short-lived natriuresis
	- Diuretic-induced inhibition of Na+ reabsorption → ↑ Na+ reabsorption elsewhere
	- NaCl ↓ → ↓ ECF → ↑ RAAS → ↑ Na+ reabsorption
