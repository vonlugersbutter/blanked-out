# Definition
- Atresia is a closed or absent opening, while stenosis is a narrowing.
- Duodenal Atresia: Failure of recanalization of the duodenum; it is associated with Down syndrome and the "double bubble" sign on imaging.
- Jejunal-Ileal-Colonic Atresia: Caused by vascular disruption leading to ischemic necrosis; it may result in "apple peel atresia".
- Pyloric Stenosis: Hypertrophy of the pylorus causing projectile, non-bilious vomiting and a palpable "olive" mass in newborns.
	- Usually male first-borns, often newborns
- Biliary atresia = idiopathic neonatal condition where biliary ducts either fail to form or degenerate early in life, leading to obstruction
	- Clinical findings include jaundice, dark urine, and pale (acholic) stools
	- Ultrasound typically shows an absent gallbladder or common bile duct
	- Treated surgically with the Kasai procedure, which uses the small intestine to create a conduit for bile drainage
