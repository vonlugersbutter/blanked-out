# Definition
Genetic disorder caused by LDL receptor deficiency, leading to severely elevated LDL and premature coronary disease, see [Acute Coronary Syndrome](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Ischemic%20Heart%20Diseases/Acute%20Coronary%20Syndrome.md).

# Clinical features
- Xanthomas = skin deposits of lipids
- Xanthelasma = yellowish plaques on the eyelids
- Tendon xanthomas = nodules on the Achilles or extensor tendons 
- Eruptive xanthomas = small, yellow-orange papules associated with severe hypertriglyceridemia
- [Atherosclerosis](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Ischemic%20Heart%20Diseases/Atherosclerosis.md) = most critical complication, leading to coronary, peripheral, and cerebrovascular disease
- Pancreatitis = life-threatening risk when TG levels exceed 1000 mg/dL, [Acute Pancreatitis](10%20Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Acute%20Pancreatitis.md)

# Treatment 
## Dietary
- Achieve and maintain a normal BMI
- Restrict total fat to <35% and saturated fat to <8% of calories
- Avoid trans-fatty acids (which raise LDL and lower HDL) and limit cholesterol to <200 mg/day
- Increase intake of omega-3 fatty acids (fish oils) to lower TGs and soluble fiber to modestly lower LDL
## Drugs
- HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins) = most effective agents for lowering LDL by inhibiting cholesterol synthesis
- PCSK9 Inhibitors = monoclonal antibodies that prevent LDL receptor degradation, used for refractory hypercholesterolemia
- Ezetimibe = inhibits intestinal cholesterol absorption; often used in combination with statins
- Fibrates = preferred for lowering TGs and raising HDL by activating PPAR-alpha
- Niacin = reduces VLDL secretion and raises HDL; it is also one of the few agents that lowers Lp(a)
- Bile acid sequestrants (resins) = bind bile acids in the gut, cholesterol excretion
## Other
- Acute lipemia = requires total elimination of oral intake and dietary fat restriction to prevent pancreatitis
- Combination therapy = using multiple drug classes (eg, statin + ezetimibe + resin) is more effective than high-dose monotherapy for severe genetic disorders
