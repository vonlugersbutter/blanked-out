# Urinalysis
# Blood tests
- Pseudo-hyperkalemia = falsely high blood plasma K+, because of traumatic lysis of RBCs while blood is being drawn 
- Blood urea nitrogen = measures the blood nitrogen that is part of urea resulting from deamination of AAs 
- BUN rise means that GFR is low 
- Plasma creatinine = metabolism of creatine phosphate in skeletal muscle, normally steady because it gets discharged in the urine
- Renal plasma clearance 
$$
clearance = \frac{U \times V}{P}
$$
- Inulin, for example, is a plant polysaccharide that is easily filtered out but not reabsorbed, and is used as a proxy for the GFR → same formula applies to creatinine 
	- Inulin can be cumbersome to handle in the clinic
	- Issues with creatinine
		- Incomplete urine collection from the patient 
		- Older people have worse creatinine excretion 
		- When GFR is normal → 15% of creatinine is through secretion; but when plasma creatinine rises 
$$
GFR = \frac{U_{in}\times V}{P_{in}}
$$
- Para-amino hippuric acid (PAH) → used to measure renal plasma flow 

Free water clearance (C<sub>H<sub>2</sub>O</sub>) = calculate amount of solute-free water generated by the kidneys
Osmolar clearance → total clearance of solutes 
$$
C_{OSM}=\frac{U_{OSM} \times \dot{V}}{P_{OSM}}
$$
