# Definition
- Inflammation of the lung parenchyma, associated with the alveoli filling up with exudate
- Commonly due to bacterial infections → *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Legionella pneumophila* 
# Etiology
- Alveoli will have a lot of leukocytes
- Suppuration might cause necrosis, leading to an abscess 
- Pneumonic region is not ventilated, it is going to cause low V/Q, possibly shunt
- Pulmonary blood flow may be reduced due to hypoxic vasoconstriction 
- Complications with ARDS → severe hypoxemia and reduced compliance
- Chest movement restricted due to pleural pain or a pleural effusion 
- CO2 retention usually does not occur
## Lobar pneumonia
- Lobar pneumonia is of a lobe; bronchopneumonia is patchy and centered around the airways
- Occurs in discrete stages, based on what cells are around

### Stages of lobar pneumonia

| Stage             | Timing (days) | Macroscopic issues                                                                             | Microscopic issues                                                                                            |
| ----------------- | ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Congestion        | 1-2           | Parenchymal partial consolidation<br>Red-purple                                                | Serous exudate, bacteria, and rare inflammatory cells                                                         |
| Red hepatization  | 3-4           | Parenchymal consolidation<br>Red-brown<br>Dry and firm<br>Liver–like consistency<br>Reversible | Exudate rich in fibrin, bacteria, erythrocytes, and inflammatory cells<br>Walls of alveoli thickened          |
| Gray hepatization | 5-7           | Uniformly gray<br>Liver-like consistency                                                       | Suppurative exudate (neutrophils and macrophages)<br>Degraded RBCs and bacteria<br>Walls of alveoli thickened |
| Resolution        | 8-28          | Gradual aeration of segment                                                                    | Enzymatic fibrinolysis<br>Macrophages remove suppurative exudate                                              |
## Aspiration pneumonia
- In patients who aspirate in a supine position, the superior segment of the right lower lobe and the posterior basal segment of the right upper lobe are the most common
## Fibrinous pleuritis
A complication of parenchymal inflammation that most commonly occurs as a result of bacterial pneumonia. Acute inflammation causes capillary vasodilation and leads to the deposition of a protein-rich, fibrinous exudate on the serosal surface of the pleura. 

- Viral pneumonias usually cause diffuse bilateral opacities rather than focal consolidation 
- Parapneumonic effusions (buildup of inflammatory fluid in the pleural space (between the lung and chest wall) → can become infected, which is empyema

# Prognosis
# Investigations
- Sputum exam and culture for identifying organism, but common organisms like *Legionella* and mycoplasma might not grow
# Clinical presentations
- Malaise, fever, cough, purulent sputum production 
- Pleuritic pain, that occurs when the pneumonia has reached the edge of lung and pleural surface (somatic innervation)
- Rapid, shallow breathing, tachycardia, occasionally cyanosis
- Typical pneumonia → classic features, usually lobar or bronchopneumonia
- Atypical pneumonia → less distinct classical features, may be unremarkable on auscultation, usually interstitial pneumonia
