# Definition
- Aka miscarriage
- Pregnancy loss before 20 weeks (after 20 weeks: stillbirth or fetal demise) → induced or spontaneous 
- Up to 70 percent of first-trimester abortions are anembryonic, meaning no identifiable embryonic elements are present = used to be called blighted ovum 
# Etiology
## Chromosomal abnormalities 
- 50% cases due to fetal chromosomal abnormalities
- Aneuploid fetuses tend to abort earlier than those with a normal chromosomal complement, with 75 percent occurring before 8 weeks, whereas euploid abortions peak at approximately 13 weeks
- Autosomal trisomy is the most frequently identified chromosomal anomaly in early miscarriages
- Monosomy X (45,X), or Turner syndrome, is the single most common specific chromosomal abnormality 
- Triploidy is frequently associated with partial hydatidiform moles and molar placental degeneration
## Euploid abortions
- Maternal [Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Diabetes/Type%202%20Diabetes%20Mellitus.md) and [Thyroid](14%20Endocrinology/Thyroid.md) disorders are clearly associated with increased loss rates 
- Systemic infections like Ebola virus and Mycoplasma genitalium are significantly linked to spontaneous abortion, while the role of Chlamydia trachomatis remains suspected but unclear
- [Obesity](10%20Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Obesity.md) and low BMI both serve as modifiable pre-pregnancy risk factors for miscarriage 
- Maternal smoking, alcohol, cocaine, maternal infection (TORCH) → see [Teratogens](13%20Reproduction/Reproductive%20Pathologies/Teratogens.md)
- Hypercoagulable states
- Lupus/antiphospholipid syndrome → woman with multiple pregnancy losses
## Pathophysiology
- Death of the embryo/fetus typically precedes expulsion and is usually accompanied by hemorrhage into the decidua basalis, followed by tissue necrosis that stimulates uterine contractions.
# Epidemiology
- More than 80 percent of spontaneous abortions occur within the first 12 weeks of pregnancy
- The risk of miscarriage increases significantly with advancing maternal age, nearly doubling for parents older than 40
- Highly sensitive assays for $\beta$ -hCG suggest that 31% of pregnancies are lost after implantation, though two-thirds of these very early losses are clinically silent
# Clinical features
- Presents as vaginal bleeding → classification based on findings
- Threatened abortion = bloody vaginal discharge through a closed internal cervical os; common, bleeding combined with uterine cramping predicts a poor prognosis
- Inevitable abortion = defined by amnionic fluid leakage through a dilated cervix, often followed by contractions or infection
- Incomplete abortion = products of conception are partially extruded through a dilated os
- Complete abortion = entire pregnancy is expelled, and the cervical os is closed upon presentation
- Missed abortion = early pregnancy loss, this involves fetal or embryonic death without expulsion of the products of conception
- Septic abortion = rare but severe complication involving infection of the uterus and products of conception, often polymicrobial but occasionally caused by dangerous clostridial species
# Investigations
- Diagnosis relies on transvaginal sonography (TVS) and quantitative serum $\beta$ -hCG levels
- Healthy intrauterine pregnancy (IUP) should show a $\beta$ -hCG increase of at least 33 to 49 percent every 48 hours
- Specific sonographic criteria for diagnosing pregnancy failure include a crown-rump length (CRL) >7 mm with no heartbeat or a mean sac diameter (MSD) >25 mm with no embryo
- Nonviability Criteria: Loss is confirmed if the mean sac diameter (MSD) is ≥25 mm without an embryo, or if the crown-rump length (CRL) is ≥7 mm without cardiac activity.
# Prognosis
## Recurrent pregnancy loss
- Loss of two or more pregnancies and affects 1-2% of fertile couples
- Causes
	- Parental chromosomal abnormalities
	- Antiphospholipid antibody syndrome (APS) = lupus 
	- Uterine abnormalities = septate uterus, Asherman syndrome, polyps 
- Balanced reciprocal translocations are the most common parental genetic abnormality
- Could also be [Hypothyroidism](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Hypothyroidism.md), [Diabetes](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Diabetes/Diabetes.md), hyperprolactinemia 
# Treatment 
## If no bleeding or signficant infection
- Expectant management = results in spontaneous resolution in more than 80 percent of women with suspected first-trimester miscarriage
- Medical management = standard regimen is 800 µg of misoprostol given vaginally, which may be repeated if necessary
	- Pretreatment with 200 mg of mifepristone can improve the efficacy of misoprostol
- Surgical Management = vacuum aspiration (electric or manual) is definitive and invasive but yields a 95- to 100-percent success rate
	- Manual vacuum aspiration (MVA) is portable and quieter, making it suitable for early gestations
- Often requires D&C to remove all tissue = dilation and curettage = used to remove tissue from inside the uterus by dilating the cervix and using a spoon-shaped instrument (curette) or suction
- For all D-negative women with first-trimester bleeding, the administration of anti-Rh0(D) immunoglobulin is recommended to prevent isoimmunization
## Induced abortion
- Surgical methods = dilation and evacuation (D & E) or vacuum aspiration
	- For gestations >12 weeks, cervical preparation with osmotic dilators (eg, Laminaria) or misoprostol is common to reduce trauma
- Most common evidence-based regimen for gestations up to 70 days is 200 mg of mifepristone followed by 800 µg of buccal misoprostol 24 to 48 hours later
