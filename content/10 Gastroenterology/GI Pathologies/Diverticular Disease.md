# Definition
- Diverticulosis = the presence of many "false diverticula" (pouches of mucosa/submucosa) usually in the sigmoid colon; caused by wall stress from straining and low-fiber diets
- Diverticulitis = inflammation of a diverticulum, presenting with LLQ pain, fever, and elevated WBC
	- Complications include abscesses, bowel obstruction, perforation, and colovesical fistulas to the bladder
# Etiology
- NSAID use, smoking, sedentary lifestyle, and diets high in fat or low in fiber
- Anatomic classification
	- True diverticulum = herniation of the entire bowel wall
	- Pseudodiverticulum (false) = protrusion of only the mucosa and submucosa through the muscularis propria
		- Most common type found in the colon
- Diverticula typically occur at points of weakness where the vasa recta penetrate the muscularis propria
- Sigmoid colon is the most common site due to high intraluminal pressures
- More likely in the colon because longitudinal muscle doesn't fully extend (teniae coli)
# Epidemiology
- Affects 50% of the us population over age 60 incidence is rising globally with the adoption of "westernized" diets
