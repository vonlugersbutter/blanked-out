Rapid but regular heart rate (160 to 200 bpm) that starts in the atria. 

## Paroxysmal SVT
- Sudden onset and termination
- Atrial rate between 140 and 250 bpm
- Narrow QRS, unless there's aberrant conduction
### AV node re-entrant tachycardia (AVNRT)
- AV node has slow and fast pathways
- Slow usually encounters refractory period of His, so only fast gets through
- Let's say an APB comes in → goes to slow pathway
	- Goes down His-Purkinje and comes up through the fast pathway, then back down through slow
	- Re-entry tachycardia
	- Requires 
		- Transient unidirectional block in fast pathway (through refractory period)
		- Slow conduction through the other pathway
- EKG: P waves not apparent because atrial depolarization happens with the QRS → retrograde, hidden, inverted
- Uncommon AVNRT
	- Anterograde conduction through slow, retrograde conduction through fast
	- Visible P waves after the QRS complex
	- Teens, young adults, well-tolerated
	- If it occurs in the elderly → it can lead to syncope, angina, and edema 
- Treatments
	- Temporary: increase parasympathetic tone through carotid sinus massage or Valsalva maneuver
	- Adenosine → ↓ AV node conduction
		- ↓ re-entry rhythm: beta blockers, Ca++ antagonists 
	- Cath ablation of the slow pathway

### Atrioventricular re-entry tachycardia
- Similar to AVNRT, but the re-entry loop limb is on an accessory pathway
- Ventricular pre-excitation syndrome, aka WPW 
	- PR interval short
	- QRS slowed (delta wave)
	- Widened QRS 
	- Predisposed to PSVTs because that accessory pathway can form a limb of a re-entry loop

![](00%20media/{DB94E5ED-09BA-4930-906E-637C34EFAE6B}.png)
- Orthodromic AVRT = impulse goes anterograde down AV, retrograde up accessory pathway
	- QRS normal
	- Only AV node, no delta waves
	- Retrograde P waves because re-entry through accessory
- Antidromic AVRT = anterograde accessory, retrograde AV node
	- QRS wide → going down accessory, not nice Purkinje thingy 
	- Might have rabbit ears 
- Arrhythmia w WPW + atrial flutter or fibrillation is super dangerous
	- Accessory pathway
	- 300 bpm ventricular rate! 
	- Cardiac arrest
- Treatment
	- Beta blockers, digitalis, Ca++ will not slow conduction through the accessory pathway → they might even increase it! 
	- Use Na+ channel blockers instead

Lown-Ganong-Levine syndrome
- Short PR, normal QRS
- Enhanced AV conduction 
- PSVT in these patients is just AV node re-entry
