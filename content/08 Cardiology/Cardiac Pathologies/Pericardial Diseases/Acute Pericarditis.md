# Definition
Inflammation of pericardial layers.
# Etiology
## Infectious 
- Acute pericarditis is usually idiopathic → studies have shown that these are usually caused by viral infection
	- Influenza, varicella, mumps 
- Tuberculous pericarditis = reactivation of tuberculosis in mediastinal lymph nodes to pericardium, or straight from within the lungs through blood; especially in immunocompromised
- Nontuberculous bacterial pericarditis = common in immunocompromised people
	- Pneumococci, staphylococci
	- Perforating trauma to chest
	- Contamination during surgery
	- Extension of infective endocarditis
	- Extension of pneumonia or subdiaphragmatic infection
	- Blood spread from remote infection 
## Non-infectious
- After MI
	- First few days after MI → inflammation spreading from myocardium, common in transmural
	- Dressler syndrome = autoimmune origin, directed at antigens released from necrotic myocardial cells 
	- Similar, after heart surgery → post pericardiotomy pericarditis 
- Uremic pericarditis = untreated chronic renal failure
- Neoplastic pericarditis = cancer spread from other places 
- Radiation-induced pericarditis = local inflammatory response, leads to fibrosis
- Associated with connective tissue disease
	- SLE, RA, progressive systemic sclerosis 
- Drug-induced → SLE-like syndrome, some antiarrhythmics, hydralazine, immune checkpoint inhibitors
## Pathogenesis
- Local vasodilation with transudation of protein-poor cell free fluid into pericardial cavity 
- Vascular permeability ↑ , lead of proteins
- Leukocyte exudation initially by neutrophils, then by mononuclear cells 
- Serous pericarditis = thin exudate, early inflammatory response, few leukocytes
- Sero-fibrinous pericarditis = commonly seen, exudate has plasma proteins, thickened and fused pericardium, dense scar that limits movement and filling
- Suppurative or purulent pericarditis = intense, usually associated with bacterial
- Hemorrhagic = often caused by malignancy or TB

# Investigations
## Physical exam
- Friction rub, movement of inflamed layers against each other 
## EKG
- Abnormal
- Diffuse ST-elevation
- PR depression, because of atrial epicardial inflammation

## Blood studies
- Acute inflammation
- WBC count, inflammatory markers like erythrocyte sedimentation rate and CRP 
	- Sedimentation rate is a common blood test that measures how quickly red blood cells settle to the bottom of a test tube in one hour. It is a non-specific indicator of inflammation in the body, as inflammation can cause red blood cells to clump together and fall faster.
## Echo
- Pericardial effusion
## Additional
- Test for TB
- Serologic tests for connective tissue diseases
- Careful search for malignancy 
# Clinical features
- Chest pain (retrosternal and left precordium), may radiate to the back)
	- Sharp, pleuritic, positional (leaning forward makes it feel better)
- Fever
- Non-exertional dyspnea, probably because it hurts to breathe 
# Treatment 
- Idiopathic is self-limited, 1-3 weeks
	- Rest
	- Pain relief by analgesic and anti-inflammatory drugs
	- Colchicine = anti-inflammatory usually used for gout
	- Oral corticosteroids only for severe or recurrent pain 
- Purulent requires more aggressive treatment
	- Catheter drainage 
- Tuberculous → prolonged anti-tuberculous therapy
