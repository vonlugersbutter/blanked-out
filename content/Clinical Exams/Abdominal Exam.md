# Physical
## Vitals
- Check for [[Orthostatic Vitals]]
## Inspection (8)
### General habitus
- Respiratory distress (such as accessory muscle use, like with the sternocleidomastoid and scalene muscles)
- Diaphoresis
- Body habitus
### Volume status
- Conduct [[Volume Status Exam]]
### General abdomen shape
- Abdomen shape
- Bulging flanks
- Obvious bulges, peristalsis
- Past surgical scars, colostomies
- Pregnancy
- Pulsations
- Movement with respiration
- Striae, superficial dilated veins, bruising 
### Facial stigmata of chronic liver disease
- Frontal balding
- Temporal muscle wasting
- Parotid enlargement
- Fetar hepaticus
- Scleral icterus
### Hand stigmata of chronic liver disease
- Terry's nails 
- Palmar erythema
- Thenar atrophy
- Hypothenar atrophy
- Asterixis 
- Dupuytren's contracture 
### Abdominal stigmata of chronic liver disease
- Spider angiomas
- Caput medusae
- Ascites
- Gynecomastia in males 
### Cullen's sign and Grey-Turner sign
- Bruising in the abdomen
- Cullen's = around the umbilicus 
- Grey-Turner's = on the flanks 
## Auscultation (5) 
- Bowel sounds with diaphragm (9 regions, stop once you hear it once)
- Abdominal bruits with bell
	- Abdominal Aorta: Slightly left and superior to umbilicus
	- R and L Renal: 5cm superior, 5cm lateral to umbilicus
	- R and L Iliac: 5cm inferior and 5cm lateral to umbilicus
	- R and L Femoral: Midway between ASIS & symphysis pubis
- Venous hum with bell
	- Epigastric and umbilical regions
- Friction rub with diaphragm
	- Over liver and spleen on lower L and R costal areas 
- Liver scratch test (in patients with obesity)
	- Place diaphragm of stethoscope at xiphisternum over the liver, just above the costal margin at the R MCL
	- Starting in RLQ, move one finger up the surface of the abdomen, scratching gently
	- Intensity of the transmitted sound is greatly enhanced once the finger is over the lower border of liver
## Percussion (6)
- General
	- Percuss in all 9 regions
	- Any tenderness?
	- All areas should be tympanic
- Liver excursion
	- Begin at R ASIS at MCL, percuss up 2 cm with each inspiratory rise 
	- Note tympanic → dull → resonant, landmark those two points
	- That's the liver span, should be around 6-12 cm 
### Tests for ascites
- Fluid wave test
	- Ask pt to hold their hands in a line at the midline
	- Tap on one side, should not feel vibrations on the other side if test negative
- Shifting dullness
	- Percuss on top, there should be an air pocket so tympanic, and then dull as you move to one side
	- Ask pt to then move to one side, and then percuss, now the side should be tympanic and the umbilical region should have more fluid 

### Splenic tests
- Castell's sign
	- Last intercostal space along the anterior axillary line, percuss throughout respiration
	- Should be tympanic
- Traube's space 
	- 6th rib superiorly, anterior axillary line laterally, left costal margin inferiorly → that triangle 
## Palpation (11)
- Light and deep palpation of all 9 regions
	- If there is pain anywhere, palpate that last
- Rebound tenderness if there is pain
	- Pain on release of fingers, not when it's still → peritonitis 
- Shake test
	- Grab the bed, and shake it to see if it causes pain → peritonitis 
- Palpation of liver edge 
	- Make an L shape with hand along RLQ along MCL 
	- Move up 2 cm with each inspiratory rise 
	- Make sure you can't feel the liver 
- Splenic palpation
	- Palpate for an enlarged spleen
	- Go from RLQ to LUQ
- Offer a DRE
### Tests for appendicitis
- McBurney's point
	- McBurney’s Point is 1/3rd the distance from the ASIS to the umbilicus on side of ASIS
	- Shouldn't be any pain
- Rovsing's sign
	- Palpate the LLQ, check if there is tenderness in the RLQ
- Psoas' sign
	- Patient in supine position, right leg relaxed and then manipulate so knee in approximately 90 degrees flexion
	- Push against thigh, ask pt to resist
	- Look for signs of pain
- Obturator sign
	- Grasp patient’s right leg below knee and ankle, hip and knee both flexed at 90, internally rotate the hip
	- Check for RLQ discomfort

- Murphy's sign for cholecystitis 
	- As patient begins deep inspiration, hook right hand under right costal margin at MCL
	- Look at patient to assess whether inspiration has been arrested
