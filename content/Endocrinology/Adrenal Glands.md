- Location = situated above the kidneys
- Vascular supply
	- Arteries = superior, middle, and inferior suprarenal arteries
	- Veins = the right adrenal vein drains directly into the inferior vena cava (ivc), while the left adrenal vein drains into the renal vein before entering the IVC
- Cortex = derived from the mesoderm; produces mineralocorticoids (aldosterone), glucocorticoids (cortisol), and androgens (testosterone)
- Medulla = derived from the neural crest; produces epinephrine and norepinephrine under the control of the sympathetic nervous system

## Cortex zones
- Zona glomerulosa = outer layer producing mineralocorticoids (aldosterone)
- Zona fasciculata = middle layer producing glucocorticoids (cortisol)
- Zona reticularis = inner layer producing androgens
- Key enzymes = includes desmolase, 17-alpha hydroxylase, 3-beta hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase, 21-alpha hydroxylase, and 11-beta hydroxylase
- Ketoconazole = an antifungal that acts as a potent inhibitor of 17,20 lyase and other enzymes, blocking cortisol and androgen synthesis
## Mineralocorticoids (aldosterone)
- Regulates mineral balance via the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system
- Increases sodium and water resorption while promoting potassium and hydrogen ion excretion in the kidney's collecting duct (principal and intercalated cells)
## Glucocorticoids (cortisol)
- Synthesis = controlled by the pituitary-adrenal axis (hypothalamus → CRH; anterior pituitary → ACTH; adrenal → cortisol)
- Circadian rhythm = serum levels are highest in the early morning (~6 am) and lowest shortly after sleep onset
- Transport = over 90% is bound to cortisol binding globulin (CBG) in the plasma
- Physiological effects = maintains blood pressure by increasing vascular sensitivity to catecholamines, suppresses the immune system (inactivates NF-KB, reduces t and b cell levels), increases serum glucose through gluconeogenesis, and promotes lipolysis and fat deposition
- Adrenal androgens = primarily dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) and androstenedione they contribute significantly to androgen production in females (~50%) but only a small amount in males
