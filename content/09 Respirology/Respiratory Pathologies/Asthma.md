# Definition
- **Inflammation** and increased **responsiveness** of the airways to stimuli. Widespread narrowing of the airways that change in severity, spontaneously or due to treatment. 
## Sub-types of severe asthma based on cell-counts
- Eosinophilic (eosinophils ≥ 3% and total sputum cell count < 15 × 10<sup>6</sup> cells/ml)
	- More responsive to corticosteroids than neutrophilic
- Neutrophilic (eosinophils < 2%, neutrophils ≥ 61%, TCC ≥ 15 × 10<sup>6</sup> cells/ml)
- Mixed granulocytic (eosinophils ≥ 2%, neutrophils ≥ 61%, TCC ≥ 15 × 10<sup>6</sup> cells/ml)
- Pauci-granulocytic  (eosinophils < 3%, neutrophils < 61%, TCC < 10 × 10<sup>6</sup> cells/ml
- 10% of all asthma cases can be uncontrolled despite corticosteroid use
# Etiology
- Hypertrophied smooth muscle of the airways
- Hypertrophied mucus glands, bronchial wall edema, lots of infiltration by eosinophils and lymphocytes
- Inflammation primarily occurs in the terminal bronchioles
- Some severe obstruction by mucus plugs, that could be coughed up in the sputum
- Inflammation could be responsible for everything, but it could also be smooth muscle abnormalities or regulation or airway tone
- Single cell is not responsible → eosinophils, basophils, macrophages, neutrophils, lymphocytes 
	- Some noninflammatory cells, like airway epithelial cells and neural cells also involved in inflammation
- Cytokines, favouring a Th2 response (normally used against things like parasitic infections or helminths) → IL3, IL4, IL5, IL13
- There's some kind of antigen, that goes and cross-links with the antigen on IgE that is on the mast cells, triggers degranulation
	- That causes the release of mediators including histamine, leukotrienes, chemotactic factors, bradykinin, etc
	- That causes increased capillary permeability, inflammatory changes, decrease in cAMP in muscles which is associated with contraction 
- During periods of worsening disease, all expiratory flow is reduced → FEV1, FEV1/FVC, FEF, Vmax50, Vmax74
- FVC is also usually reduced because the airways close prematurely towards the end of a full expiration (already quite small, there may also be destruction of elastic tissue, so the positive pressure on exhalation causes them to collapse)
- Static lung volumes, like for FRC and TLC during asthma attacks are increased
	- Increased RV due to premature airway closure 
- Uneven ventilation, could cause hypoxemia due to low V/Q
- Short-acting bronchodilator, albuterol, usually resolves all indices 
- DLCO could be higher than normal, because of the large lung volume → hyperinflation increases area of blood-gas interface, increasing diffusing capacity
- With exacerbations, pCO2 may decrease, usually because of hyperventilation (stimulation of intrapulmonary receptors)
	- In status asthmaticus, pCO2 can rise while pH falls → respiratory failure
# Epidemiology
- Usually begins in childhood, and usually doesn't get worse over time
- Frequent exposure to childhood infections → lower incidence of asthma (hygiene hypothesis)
- Environmental factors, atmosphere pollutants, some genetic factors

# Investigations
- Have to demonstrate reversible airway obstruction → bronchodilator response on spirometry
- Increased inhaled doses of methacholine (non-selective muscarinic receptor agonist, parasympathetic nervous system) → measure FEV1 → PD20 (provocative dose 20) is the dose that results in a 20% fall in FEV1
# Clinical presentations
- Previous history of atopy, allergic rhinitis, eczema, urticaria
- Could be associated with a specific allergen, especially ragweed and cats (allergic asthma)
- No general history of allergy, no external allergen → non-allergic asthma
- Asthma attack → periods of exacerbation
	- Changes in air quality, viral infections, but also occur without obvious triggers 
	- Dyspnea, orthopneic, anxious, chest tightness, accessory muscles of respiration, wheezes, rapid pulse, pulsus paradoxus (marked fall in systolic and pulse pressure during inspiration), scant and viscid sputum 
- Status asthmaticus → attack that continues for hours, days without remission even with bronchodilator and corticosteroid therapy
	- Exhaustion, dehydration, marked tachycardia
	- Chest may become silent, vigorous treatment needed!
	- Respiratory failure, cardiovascular collapse due to severe air trapping (subsequent effects on venous return and cardiac preload)

Charcot-Leyden crystal, a histopathologic finding in patients with eosinophilic inflammation and/or proliferation → microscopic crystals composed of eosinophil protein galectin-10. Only really associated with **extrinsic asthma** (allergic asthma), an IgE-mediated type 1 hypersensitivity reaction to specific allergens.

## Late-phage response
The asthma late-phase response is a prolonged asthma reaction that occurs hours after an initial allergen exposure, leading to increased airway inflammation, airway hyperresponsiveness, and worsening symptoms like coughing and shortness of breath. It's caused by the continued release of inflammatory mediators from cells like mast cells, which recruit other inflammatory cells such as eosinophils and neutrophils. This process results in mucus production, airway narrowing, and can contribute to long-term airway remodeling and structural changes
# Treatment

| Treatment option            | Usage                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Inhaled corticosteroids     | Routine daily usage for asthma<br><br>Steroids demonstrate a permissive drug interaction with adrenergic receptor agonists, increasing the amplitude of the maximal response to adrenergic agonists. Steroids accomplish this by increasing adrenergic receptor activation and the concentration of signaling intermediates. |
| Beta-adrenergic agonists    | Stimulation of beta-2 receptors relaxes smooth muscle in the bronchi, blood vessels, and uterus <br>All of these bind to beta-2 receptors, increase activity of adenyl cyclase, more cAMP, less contraction of muscles<br><br>Short-acting (SABA): salbutamol, terbutaline<br>Long-acting (LABA): salmeterol, formoterol     |

## Management plans (stepwise asthma treatment for individuals ≥ 12 years of age)

| Steps | GINA 2025                                                                                                                                                         | NAEPP 2020                                                                                                                                              |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Symptoms < 4–5 days/week<br>PRN low-dose budesonide (steroid), formoterol (LABA)                                                                                  | Intermittent asthma <br>PRN SABA                                                                                                                        |
| 2     | Symptoms < 4–5 days/week<br>PRN low-dose budesonide (steroid), formoterol (LABA)                                                                                  | Mild, persistent asthma<br>PRN SABA, scheduled low-dose ICS                                                                                             |
| 3     | Symptoms most days of the week or waking with symptoms ≥ 1 ×/week:<br>Scheduled and PRN low-dose ICS, LABA                                                        | Moderate persistent asthma<br>Preferred: scheduled and PRN low-dose ICS, LABA<br>Alternative: PRN SABA + scheduled medium-dose ICS or low-dose ICS-LABA |
| 4     | Daily symptoms or waking with symptoms ≥ 1 ×/week and low lung function<br>Scheduled medium-dose ICS/LABA + PRN low-dose ICS/LABA                                 | Moderate persistent asthma not controlled with step 3<br>Scheduled and PRN medium-dose ICS/LABA                                                         |
| 5     | Inadequate symptom relief with step 4 treatment<br>LAMA, trial of high-dose ICS/LABA for 3-6 months<br>Add-on therapies like biologics and low-dose oral steroids | Severe persistent asthma<br>Scheduled medium to high dose of ICS/LABA + scheduled LAMA + PRN SABA                                                       |
| 6     | N/A                                                                                                                                                               | Severe persistent, not controlled by step 5<br>Daily high-dose ICS/LABA + scheduled LAMA, PRN SABA                                                      |
