# Definition
Incomplete development of the interventricular septum. Blood flows from the left ventricle into the right ventricle. 
# Etiology
![200](00%20media/{A7745D21-BCA5-48EC-B9E9-561617DC0C1A}.png)
- Mostly in the membranous and muscular portions, rarely in the aortic valve or beside the AV valves 
- If the defect is small, blood isn't going to go through anyway
- Larger defect shunting depends on pressures
	- Before birth, pulmonary pressure = systemic pressure, so little flow through the defect
	- But after birth = left to right shunting
- RV, pulmonary circulation, LA, LV → all volume overload → ↑ SV → chamber dilatation → systolic dysfunction → heart failure 
- If pulmonary resistance is high, shunt is reversed, [Eisenmenger Syndrome](Eisenmenger%20Syndrome)
# Epidemiology
- 1.5 to 3.5 per 1000 live births

# Investigations
## Physical exam
- Holosystolic murmurs at left sternal border with systolic thrill 
- Smaller = more turbulence = louder
- Mid-diastolic rumbling because ↑ flow on mitral valve 
- Loud pulmonic closure, cyanosis in RV heave might happen 
## CXR
- Cardiac silhouette normal in small defects
- In larger
	- Cardiomegaly
	- Pulmonary vascular markings
	- Enlarged pulmonary arteries
## EKG
- Left atrial enlargement and LV hypertrophy, RV hypertrophy
## Echo
- Visualize EKG, estimate pressures
## Cardiac catheterization

# Clinical features
- Asymptomatic
- Tachypnea
- Poor feeding
- Lower respiratory tract infections 
- Bacterial endocarditis 
# Treatment 
- By age 2, 50% of small and moderate VSDs undergo partial or complete closure by themselves
- Surgical correction for heart failure or pulmonary htn
