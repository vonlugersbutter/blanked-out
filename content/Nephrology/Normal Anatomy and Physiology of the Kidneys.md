# Nephrons
- Renal corpuscle = glomerulus (capillary network) + glomerular capsule or Bowman's capsule
- Renal tubule = proximal convoluted tubule, nephron loop or loop of Henle (descending and ascending), and distal convoluted tubule 
- Empties into the collecting duct 
- Most nephrons are **cortical nephrons** → their corpuscles lie mainly in cortex, they have short loops that penetrate only to the outer medulla
	- Receive supply from peritubular capillaries from efferent arterioles
- Some are **juxtamedullary nephrons** → renal corpuscles lie close to the medulla, and they have a long loop that goes down into the deepest regions 
	- Receive supply from peritubular capillaries and vasa recta from efferent arterioles 
	- Their loops have a thin and then thick ascending limb 
- All of it is one single layer of epithelial cells 
- Glomerular filtration = where water and most solutes move into the glomerular capsule and into the renal tubules
- Tubular reabsorption = reabsorption of most water and many useful solutes
- Tubular secretion = removes substances from the blood 
## Glomerulus
![](00%20media/{B7343363-4F30-4438-AC9E-5622DFC09296}.png)
- Bowman's capsule is visceral and parietal layers
	- Visceral = simple squamous epithelial cells called podocytes with foot-like projections (pedicles)
- Juxtaglomerular apparatus = juxtaglomerular cells + macula densa 
	- Regulates blood pressure in the kidneys 
- Fluid enters the space = glomerular filtrate, usually a filtration fraction of 16-20%
- Mesangial cells are contractile and help regulate blood flow → when relaxed, filtration is high
- Efferent arteriole is smaller than afferent, so resistance to outflow of blood from the glomerulus is high → pressure in the glomerular capillaries is higher than other capillaries
- Filtration membrane
	- Fenestrations of glomerular endothelial cells
	- Basement membrane of glomerulus
	- Slit membrane between pedicles of podocytes 
- Filtration pressures
	- Moving things into the glomerulus
		- Glomerular blood hydrostatic pressure 
	- Moving things out of the glomerulus
		- Capsular hydrostatic pressure
		- Blood colloid osmotic pressure 
- NFP = GBHP - CHP - BCOP
- Glomerular filtration rate (GFR) = amount of filtrate formed in all renal corpuscles per minute  
#### Things that regulate GFR
- Adjusting blood flow in and out of glomerulus
- Altering glomerular capillary surface available 
- Myogenic mechanism = stretching causes contraction of smooth muscle cells in afferent arterioles
	- BP ↑ → GFR ↑ → afferent arteriole constriction → GFR ↓ 
- Tubuloglomerular feedback 
	- GFR ↑ → less time for tubular reabsorption → macula densa cells detect Na+, Cl-, water → inhibit release of NO from cells in JGA → afferent arterioles constrict  
- Neural regulation of GFR
	- SNS → NE → vasoconstriction through $\alpha_1$ receptors (lots in afferent arterioles) → constriction and ↓ GFR 
- Hormonal regulation
	- Angiotensin II = potent vasoconstrictor, depresses GFR
	- Atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) = caused by stretching of the atria, relaxes glomerular mesangial cells, ↑ GFR 
## Renal tubule
- Simple cuboidal epithelial cells
- Reabsorbed substances: glucose, AAs, urea, Na+, K+, Ca++, Cl-, HCO3-, HPO4 2-
- Secreted: H+, K+, NH4+, creatinine, drugs 
- Types of movement
	- Paracellular reabsorption
	- Transcellular reabsorption
- Obligatory water reabsorption = water follows the solutes
- Facultative water reabsorption = regulated by ADH in the collecting ducts 
### Proximal convoluted tubule 
- Most solute reabsorption involves Na+ here 
- Note the sodium glucose symporter

![400](00%20media/{91AE9FE6-096C-4CFE-A1C8-33C5D490EE9A}.png)

- And the Na+/H+ antiporter, which also allows for reabsorption of HCO3-

![400](00%20media/{295844E7-9280-4ED1-88A3-44B63DBD741C}%201.png)

- Water moves into the peritubular capillaries to restore osmotic balance → and the cells are permeable because they have aquaporin-1
- When water leaves, in the second half of the PCT, Cl-, K+, Ca++, Mg++, and urea also diffuse into the capillaries 
	- Cl- leaves the most, so it also creates an electrical gradient for the cations to follow 
- Ammonia is a poisonous waste product derived from the deamination of AAs, a reaction that occurs mainly in hepatocytes → convert most of this ammonia to urea, a less-toxic compound
	- Urea and ammonia both filtered at the glomerulus, secreted by PCT into tubular fluid 
	- PCT cells can produce more NH3 by deaminating glutamine, also generating HCO3-
	- Na+/H+ antiporters can also use ammonium 
- About 65% of the water is reabsorbed here 
### Loop of Henle
- Reabsorbs 15% of water and a whole bunch of ions
- Part of the loop is impermeable to water
#### Thick ascending limb
- Apical membrane cells have Na+/K+/2Cl- symporters 

![400](00%20media/{9435AFDC-9B1E-4156-A6D9-0E54D8FE19EC}.png)

### Distal convoluted tubule
- 80% of the water has already been reabsorbed
- Reabsorption of Na+ and Cl- through Na+/Cl- symporters 
## Ducts
- Two types of cells in the last part of the DCT and the collecting duct, both simple cuboidal 
	- Principle cells = receptors for ADH and aldosterone
		- Reabsorb Na+ and secrete K+ 
	- Intercalated cells = homeostasis of blood pH 
		- Reabsorb HCO3- and secrete H+ 

![300](00%20media/{4B1EC43D-48D0-42A4-A89A-F04424892D17}.png)

# Hormonal regulation of tubules
- RAAS system
	- Blood pressure ↓ → less stretch of arterioles → juxtaglomerular cells secrete renin → coverts angiotensinogen from liver into angiotensin I → ACE from lungs converts it to angiotensin II 
	- Angiotensin II
		- GFR ↓ through vasoconstriction of afferent arterioles
		- Enhances reabsorption of Na+ and water in PCT through more activity of Na+/H+ antiporters
		- Makes adrenal cortex make aldosterone → stimulates principal cells → reabsorb more Na+, secrete K+ 

![400](00%20media/{CC0F4552-083F-427D-9D71-46E9EC953664}.png)

- ADH or vasopressin
	- Made in the posterior pituitary 
	- Increases water permeability of principal cells in DCT 
	- Principal cells have aquaporin-2, and ADH makes them go into the apical membranes 
	- When water concentration decreases → osmoreceptors in hypothalamus detect this → more ADH → more water reabsorbed 
	- Depends on hydration level 

![](00%20media/{2D3B06C0-FD5E-42AD-96CE-CE81CD743518}.png)

- Atrial natriuretic peptide → ↓ aldosterone, ADH 

![200](00%20media/{8AAC25AA-0958-4B14-803E-508F38C16B90}.png)

- Ca++ ↓ → parathyroid hormone → stimulate reabsorption from DCT of Ca++ and promotes phosphate secretion
# Dilute versus concentrated urine 

![400](00%20media/{8A5E0113-348D-48F0-8990-200D5149C990}.png)

![](00%20media/{1D111C73-F964-4232-BA6F-2DA14A882F8D}.png)

- Ability of ADH to cause excretion of concentrated urine depends on osmotic gradient of solutes in the interstitial fluid of the renal medulla 
- Two factors towards osmotic gradient
	- Differences in solute and water permeability and reabsorption
	- Countercurrent flow of fluid through structures in renal medulla 
		- Countercurrent multiplication 
			- The long loop of Henle are the countercurrent multiplier 
			- Symporters in thick ascending limb cause buildup of Na+ and Cl- in the renal medulla
			- Countercurrent flow through the descending and ascending limbs of the loop establishes osmotic gradient in the renal medulla
			- Cells in the collecting ducts reabsorb more water and urea
			- Urea recycling causes a buildup of urea in the renal medulla 
		- Countercurrent exchange
			- Vasa recta is the countercurrent exchanger
