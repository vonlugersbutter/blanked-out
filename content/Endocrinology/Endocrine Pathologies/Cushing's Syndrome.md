# Definition

# Etiology
- ACTH independent (ACTH ↓)
	- Most commonly due to corticosteroid medication
	- Adrenal adenoma
- ACTH dependent (ACTH ↑)
	- Pituitary tumors (Cushing’s disease)
	- Ectopic ACTH production (eg, small cell lung cancer)
# Epidemiology

# Clinical features
- Hypertension, hyperglycemia, central obesity ("moon face," "buffalo hump"), immune suppression, thin skin with purple striae, and easy bruising
- Immune suppression, risk of opportunistic infections 
- Proximal muscle weakness = cortisol-induced myopathy (atrophy of type II fast-twitch fibers)
- Altered GnRH release → ↓ FSH, LH → menstrual irregularities 
- Hirsutism
- Erectile dysfunction
- In Cushing's syndrome → ↑ ACTH → ↑ MSH → skin hyperpigmentation 
# Investigations
- 24-hour urine free cortisol, salivary cortisol, and dexamethasone suppression tests
	- Cortisol is higher in the morning, lower in the evenings 
- Low dose dexamethasone suppression test
	- Basically administer 1 mg, low dose, at bedtime → should suppress cortisol level
	- In Cushing's syndrome, the adenoma will not get suppressed and cortisol will be high
	- Check the ACTH level

- Once you have that Cushing's syndrome established, you can do a high dose 8 mg dexamethasone test, which will differentiate causes of high ACTH Cushing's syndrome 
	- In pituitary adenomas, cortisol will be suppressed (higher setpoints)
	- In ACTH tumors, its not going to be suppressed

- Could also use late-night salivary cortisol 
- CT scans are used to evaluate adrenal masses (incidentalomas) and to look for tumors in cases of ectopic ACTH production
# Prognosis

# Treatment 
- Surgery or medical management with ketoconazole (inhibits desmolase and 17 alpha hydroxylase)
