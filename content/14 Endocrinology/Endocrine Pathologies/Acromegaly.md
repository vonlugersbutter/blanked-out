# Clinical features
- Somatic/soft tissue overgrowth
- Widening of dental gaps (diastema), macrognathia, and the increase in shoe/ring size are pathognomonic
- Oily skin and sweating (hyperhidrosis) are due to GH-induced hypertrophy of sweat and sebaceous glands
- Pituitary adenoma is usual cause → bitemporal hemianopsia, headaches because increased sellar pressure or dural stretch
- Acromegalic cardiomyopathy = S3 gallop, crackles, and peripheral edema 
	- GH causes concentric left ventricular hypertrophy and interstitial fibrosis, see [Cardiomyopathies](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Cardiomyopathies.md)
	- Could eventually lead to [Heart Failure](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Heart%20Failure.md)
- GH is a potent insulin antagonist, stimulating gluconeogenesis and lipolysis → pituitary diabetes 
- OSA = direct result of macroglossia and soft tissue hypertrophy of the pharyngeal airway
- Low libido, ED, and reduced shaving frequency in men = tumor is either compressing the gonadotrophs (causing secondary hypogonadism) or co-secreting prolactin
# Investigations
- Serum IGF-1 = unlike GH, which is pulsatile and has a short half-life, IGF-1 levels are stable throughout the day and reflect the integrated GH secretion over the preceding 24 hours
- Pituitary MRI, see [Pituitary Gland](14%20Endocrinology/Pituitary%20Gland.md) for pituitary adenoma discussion
- Goldman visual field test = formally map the extent of chiasmal compression and serve as a baseline for post-surgical recovery

# Treatment 
- Somatostatin analogs (eg, octreotide) = to inhibit GH secretion
- GH receptor antagonists (eg, Pegvisomant) = block peripheral IGF-1 production
- Dopamine agonists (eg, Cabergoline) = especially if the tumor co-secretes prolactin
- Acromegaly is associated with an increased risk of colonic polyps and adenocarcinoma due to the chronic proliferative effects of IGF-1 → colonoscopy to screen for [Colon Cancer](10%20Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Colon%20Cancer.md)
