# Dating
- Embryonic age = age dated to fertilization
- Gestational age = age dated to last menstrual period, embryonic age plus two weeks
- Fertilization = occurs within 1 day of ovulation, usually occurs in the ampulla of fallopian tube
- Implantation = occurs about 6 days after ovulation
- Naegele Rule = estimated date of confinement (EDC) can be calculated by adding seven days to the first day of the LMP and subtracting three months.
- Trimesters = gestation is divided into three units of approximately 14 weeks each.
- In the first trimester, the crown-rump length is highly accurate for dating, typically within ± 5 to 7 days
- Age Definitions = Gestational age (or menstrual age) is measured from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP), while ovulation age (or postconceptional age) reflects the time since actual conception
# hCG and HPL
- Syncytiotrophoblast (see [Embryogenesis](Reproduction/Embryogenesis.md) and [Placenta](Reproduction/Placenta.md)) secretes hCG = human chorionic gonadotropin
	- Similar structure to luteinizing hormone (LH)
	- Two glycoprotein subunits (“heterodimeric glycoprotein”), α and β subunits
	- LH and hCG: same α subunit, also same α subunit in FSH and TSH
	- Progesterone production maintains pregnancy for first 10 weeks, and then placenta maintains pregnancy moving forward
	- Used to detect pregnancy with beta subunit, usually antibody based tests (ELISA variants)
- Human placental lactogen or chorionic somatomammotropin
	- Protein hormone, produced by syncytiotrophoblast
	- Higher levels as placenta grows during pregnancy
	- Blocks effects of insulin → [Diabetes](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Diabetes/Diabetes.md)
		- Raises blood glucose level (good for baby)
		- Promotes breakdown of fatty acids by mother for fuel
		- Promotes breakdown of proteins for fuel
# Other hormones
- Corticotropin-Releasing Hormone (CRH) = Synthesized in the placenta and decidua; it may play a role in relaxing smooth muscle or initiating parturition
- Progesterone = Synthesis depends on maternal LDL cholesterol Production rates reach 250 mg/day in singleton pregnancies and over 600 mg/day in multifetal pregnancies Unlike estrogen, placental progesterone production can persist for weeks even after fetal demise
- Estrogens (Estradiol and Estriol) = The placenta cannot synthesize estrogens from cholesterol because it lacks the enzyme 17α-hydroxylase Instead, it relies on C19 steroid precursors, primarily DHEA-S, from the fetal and maternal adrenal glands
- Estriol (E3) = About 90% of placental estriol is derived from fetal 16OH-DHEA-S
- Directional Secretion = Over 90% of estradiol and estriol and approximately 85% of progesterone produced by the placenta enters the maternal circulation rather than the fetal circulation
# Fetal membranes
- Amnion = First identifiable around day 7 or 8 of development, it eventually engulfs the growing embryo The amnion provides significant tensile strength to the fetal membranes, residing mostly in its compact layer of cross-linked collagens I and III It is metabolically active and involved in solute and water transport
- Umbilical Cord = Contains two umbilical arteries, which carry deoxygenated blood from the fetus to the placenta, and one umbilical vein, which carries oxygenated blood back to the fetus
- Microchimerism occurs when fetal cells (lymphocytes, stem cells) engraft in the mother and remain for decades This phenomenon is implicated in the pathogenesis of certain autoimmune disorders, such as systemic lupus erythematosus and scleroderma
# Decidua
The decidua is the specialized endometrium of pregnancy. It is categorized into three main portions based on its anatomical relationship to the embryo:
- Decidua basalis = directly beneath the site of blastocyst implantation, maternal part of the [Placenta](Reproduction/Placenta.md)
- Decidua capsularis = covering the expanding blastocyst and separating it from the rest of the uterine cavity
- Decidua parietalis (or vera) = remainder of the decidua lining the uterine cavity

Histological Layers of the Decidua → both the decidua parietalis and basalis consist of three zones:
- Zona compacta (surface)
- Zona spongiosa (middle) = remnants of uterine glands and numerous small blood vessels → together, the compacta and spongiosa form the zona functionalis
- Zona basalis (deepest) = remains after delivery and gives rise to new endometrium for future cycles
# Diabetes in pregnancy
- Pregnancy is an insulin-resistant state
- Decreased maternal response to insulin
- Diabetes mellitus = worsened by pregnancy
- Gestational diabetes = onset of diabetes during pregnancy
- Screening with serum glucose testing, glycosuria occurs in normal pregnancy
# Physiologic changes
- Total body volume expands
- Blood fills placenta
- Diverted from maternal circulation
- ↑ renin → salt/water retention
- Red cell mass expands, increased maternal EPO
- Dilutional anemia, rise in volume > rise in red cells, result: ↓ Hct
- Cardiac output rises, preload increased by rise in blood volume, afterload reduced due to fall in systemic vascular resistance
- Maternal heart rate rises slightly, peripheral resistance falls (placenta is in parallel)
- Increased tidal volume 
## Supine hypotension
- Occurs in later stages of pregnancy, large baby compresses IVC when lying flat
- Decreased venous return (preload), fall in cardiac output
- Reflex tachycardia may produce symptoms
## Coagulation
- Pregnancy is a hypercoagulable state
	- Probably evolved to protect against blood loss at delivery
	- Many clotting factor levels change
	- Increased fibrinogen
	- Decreased protein S
- Fetus also obstructs venous return → DVTs common
# Labor
- Regular uterine contractions
- Progressive dilation of cervix
- Descent and expulsion of fetus
- Normally occurs at 40 weeks
- Preterm labor <37 weeks
- Inhibit contractions
	- Terbutaline/Ritodrine
	- β-2 agonists → ↑ cAMP
	- Relax uterine (smooth) muscle
- Apgar score = used to access newborn immediately after birth
	- 10 point score at 1 and 5 minutes after birth
	- Value of 0, 1, or 2 for five categories:
		- Heart rate
		- Respiratory effort
		- Muscle tone
		- Reflex irritability
		- Skin color (pink, blue)
	- 5-min score ≤3 associated with neurologic damage, like in cerebral palsy
- Pregnancy termination
	- Mifepristone = anti-progesterone
		- Blocks progesterone effects on uterus
		- Prevents implantation
	- Misoprostol
		- Synthetic prostaglandin E1 analog
		- Induces uterine contractions
- Combination: Medical abortion in >90% women
- Methotrexate used only in [Ectopic Pregnancy](Reproduction/Reproductive%20Pathologies/Ectopic%20Pregnancy.md)
# Twins
- Often one twin dies in utero, resorption of fetus/embryo, delivery of single baby
- More fetuses = shorter pregnancy, usually 37 weeks for twins, 33 for triplets
- Increased risk of maternal/fetal complications
	- Growth restriction, congenital anomalies, preterm delivery
	- Gestational hypertension/preeclampsia
## Dizygotic twins
- Two zygotes
- Two separate ova fertilized by two separate sperm
- Two siblings born from single pregnancy, separate placentas, each baby has own amnion and chorion = dichorionic diamniotic
- “Fraternal twins”
- Common in IVF
## Monozygotic twins
- One zygote divides in two
- One ova fertilized by one sperm, can be shared placenta, variable number of chorion and amnion, depends on when the zygote splits 
- “Identical twins”

## Hypertension
- Pre-existing/chronic hypertension = elevated BP prior to pregnancy or 20 weeks
- Gestational hypertension = elevated BP that develops after 20 weeks
	- Safe drugs to use: α-methyldopa, labetalol (β1β2α1 blocker), nifedipine (calcium channel blocker)
- [[Preeclampsia-eclampsia]] = hypertension in pregnancy, proteinuria, end-organ damage
## Amniotic fluid issues
- Primary sources: fetal urine and lung secretions, major source for removal: fetal swallowing
- Oligohydramnios = decreased amniotic fluid, often a fetal kidney problem
	- Fetal renal abnormalities like bilateral renal agenesis or posterior urethral valves (males) = obstructing urination 
	- Placental insufficiency like preeclampsia or maternal vascular diseases
	- Premature rupture of membranes, membranes not intact 
	- Can lead to [Potter's Syndrome](Medical%20Genetics/Potter's%20Syndrome.md)
- Polyhydramnios = excessive amniotic fluid, often a swallowing/GI problems
	- [Esophageal Atresia](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Esophageal%20Atresia.md) or anencephaly
	- Maternal [Diabetes](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Diabetes/Diabetes.md), polyuria
	- Fetal anemia = leads to high fetal cardiac output, increased urine production
		- Can occur in parvovirus infection
	- Multiple gestations = more fetal urine
## Low Birth Weight
- Less than 2500 grams (5.5lbs)
- Caused by premature delivery, intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR)
	- Risk factors = preeclampsia, abruptio placenta, [Teratogens](Reproduction/Teratogens.md)
- Increased risk of neonatal mortality, newborn complications
- Problems include hypothermia, hypoglycemia, hyperbilirubinemia and jaundice, neonatal RDS due to ↓ surfactant
- Transient tachypnea of the newborn = inadequate lung fluid clearance
- Pneumonia, respiratory failure, need for ventilator support
- Persistent fetal circulation
	- Persistent high PVR → shunting → hypoxemia
	- Abnormal development of pulmonary vasculature = small vessels, thickened walls, excessive vasoconstriction
- Cellular immunity impaired
- Polycythemia of the newborn
	- Excessively elevated hematocrit at birth (>65)
	- Newborns normally have increased red cell mass
		- Fetus in a relatively hypoxic environment in utero
		- Increased hemoglobin production
		- Placental blood may transfer to baby at birth
	- Usually asymptomatic
	- Rarely may cause symptoms
		- Hypoglycemia (excessive RBC glucose utilization)
			- Hyperbilirubinemia
- Necrotizing enterocolitis
	- Intestinal necrosis and obstruction
	- Usually terminal ileum or colon
	- Can lead to perforation
	- Major risk factor is prematurity, low birth weight
- Intraventricular hemorrhage = hemorrhage into lateral ventricle
	- Hypotonia
	- Loss of spontaneous movements
	- Seizures, coma
	- Germinal matrix problem = highly vascular area near ventricles
		- Premature infants: poor autoregulation of blood flow here
		- In full term infants, this area has decreased vascularity
- SIDS = sudden infant death syndrome, leading cause infant mortality 1 month to 1 year in US
	- Increased risk with preterm birth or low birth weight
	- Increased risk of neurocognitive problems
	- Very young maternal age (<20)
	- Bed sharing (infant/parent)
	- Prematurity/low birth weight
