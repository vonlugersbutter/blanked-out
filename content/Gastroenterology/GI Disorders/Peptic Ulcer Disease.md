# Etiology
- Duodenal ulcers = comprise ~90% of PUD and are nearly always related to h pylori
	- Pain typically improves with meals because food stimulates bicarbonate secretion
	- Brunner’s glands in the duodenum hypertrophy to provide additional alkaline fluid 
	- Complications include bleeding from the gastroduodenal artery or perforation
- Gastric ulcers = usually occur on the lesser curvature and cause pain that is worse with meals, leading to weight loss bleeding
	- Typically involves the left gastric artery

# Clinical features
- Gastric ulcers of the lesser curvature may cause bleeding from the left gastric artery
- Duodenal ulcers of the posterior wall may cause bleeding from the gastroduodenal artery
# Treatment 
- Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) = (eg, omeprazole) block the H+/K+ ATPase
- H2 blockers = (eg, cimetidine, famotidine) block histamine receptors
- Misoprostol = a PGE1 analog that blunts acid secretion
