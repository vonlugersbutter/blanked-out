There are three types: dilated, hypertrophic, and restrictive. We will go through all three (lucky you).

![](00%20media/{82C0E22D-7CCE-499D-89E9-24F7F6B97F3E}.png)

- Dilated cardiomyopathy = ventricular chamber enlargement, impaired systolic contraction
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy = abnormally thickened ventricular wall, abnormal diastolic relaxation, normal systole
- Restrictive cardiomyopathy = abnormally stiffened myocardium bc fibrosis or infiltration, impaired diastolic relaxation, systolic normal 
- Newer classification = MOGE(S) → morphology, organ involvement, genetic inheritance, etiologic annotation, and functional status 
![400](00%20media/{973DB2EA-673F-48DF-8D2F-355AEF32EFED}.png)

We start with dilated cardiomyopathy.

# Definition
Ventricular chamber enlargement, impaired systolic contraction.
# Etiology
- Idiopathic
- Specific genetic mutations
	- Abnormalities in contractile force generation, transmission, energy production
	- Familial cardiomyopathy (in cytoskeletal, sarcomeric, nuclear membrane genes)
		- Especially titin 
	- Muscular/myotonic dystrophy (sarcolemmal membrane genes)
- Inflammatory myocarditis
	- Viral myocarditis
	- Noninfective myocarditis
		- Granulomatous inflammatory disease
			- Sarcoidosis = immunologic response to an unclear trigger and is characterized by noncaseating granulomas 
			- Giant cell myocarditis = t cell inflammation 
		- Connective tissue disease like SLE 
		- Immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy for cancers 
- Too much alcohol
- Cocaine = ↑ myocardial demand, oxygen stress, more platelet formation 
- Hyperthyroidism
- Chronic hypocalcaemia or hypophosphatemia 
- Hemochromatosis 
- Peripartum, with a genetic predisposition 
- Antineoplastic agents 
- Takotsubo cardiomyopathy/apical ballooning syndrome = severe emotional and physical stress 

## General pathophysiology

![](00%20media/{78F5FE1D-8047-4D11-B64A-9A6E29F2FE69}.png)
- It's usually LV dysfunction and enlargement 
- Irregular hypertrophy, atrophy of myofibers, lots of fibrosis 
- RV might also collab 
- SV and CO ↓ → Frank-Starling → ↑ EDV → try to increase the SV and CO 
- Neurohormonal activation → ↑ SNS → more CO
- But these don't last forever, and could lead to [Heart Failure](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Heart%20Failure.md) 
- RAAS activation is going to happen → ↑ afterload → more difficult 
- Too much volume and pressure loads on the atria → dilation → atrial fibrillation
- Regurgitation of blood into LA, ↓ SV into aorta → even greater volume load into the LV

1/3 get better on their own. Persistent DCM has 50% mortality in 5 years.

- L/RBBB
- Myocardial fibrosis w Q waves that look like MI 
## Echo
## Cath
- Check pressures and coronary arteries
## MRI 
- Especially for inflammation 
# Clinical features
Pretty similar to HF
- Dyspnea
- Fatigue
- Light-headedness
- Exertional dyspnea
- ND
- Ascites
- Peripheral edema
- Cool extremities because vasoconstriction
- Arterial pressure ↓ 
- Tachycardia
- Crackles, basilar dullness
- Enlarged heart, leftward PMI
- S3

- HF symptoms
	- Na+ ↓ , ACE inhibitors, beta blockers, maybe spironolactone 
	- Amiodarone for arrythmias 
	- A fib/thromboembolic event/thrombus found on echo → anticoagulant

Next, we go to hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.
# Definition
Hypertrophy not caused by chronic pressure overload. Aka hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy or idiopathic hypertrophic subaortic stenosis. 
# Etiology
- SV contraction ↑ , stiff thick muscle, ↓ ventricular relaxation, ↑ diastolic pressure
- Autosomal dominant, usually issues with beta myosin, cardiac troponins, myosin binding protein C
- Asymmetric hypertrophy of the septum is common
- Disarrayed histology = cardiac fibroblasts, ECM → this leads to stiffness and arrythmias
- Compliance ↓ , hence the ↑ diastolic pressure
- No outflow obstruction
	- ↑ LA, PV pressure → dyspnea
- With outflow obstruction
	- Thickened septum → forceful ejection through aorta → Venturi effect → draw mitral valve anterior leaflet to IVS → blocks aorta
		- Might also cause [Mitral Regurgitation](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Valvular%20Diseases/Mitral%20Regurgitation.md) and contribute to [Atrial Fibrillation (AF)](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Arrhythmias/Atrial%20Fibrillation%20(AF).md)

![500](00%20media/{B197621F-7FC9-456B-8859-529CE8984525}.png)
- LVSP ↑ → wall stress → ↑ angina
- Decrease LV size with ↓ venous return or positive inotropy → promote obstruction → harsher murmur
# Epidemiology
Young athletes, sudden death. 1/500 of the population. Usually mid 20s.
# Prognosis
<1% per year sudden deaths. 5% of patients will develop adverse LV remodeling, decline in systolic dysfunction. Early diagnosis usually means worse clinical course 
# Investigations
## EKG
- LV hypertrophy
- T wave inversion
- Prominent septal forces → deep, narrow Q waves
- Atrial fibrillation
- Ventricular tachycardia 
## Echo
- LV hypertrophy 
- Check for mitral regurgitation with Doppler
## Cardiac catheterization
- Needed when you need to check if it's something else like [Aortic Stenosis](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Valvular%20Diseases/Aortic%20Stenosis.md) 
## Genetic testing 
# Clinical features
- Could be none
- Dyspnea = ↑ diastolic and pulmonary pressures, mitral regurgitation 
- Angina without coronary artery issues 
	- Myocardial ischemia because ↑ O2 demand and narrowed coronary arteries (less coronary perfusion pressure)
- Syncope = cardiac arrythmias
- Atrial fibrillation → further impairs diastolic filling 
- Could result in [Ventricular Fibrillation (VF or V-fib)](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Arrhythmias/Ventricular%20Fibrillation%20(VF%20or%20V-fib).md), which then leads to sudden death 
	- History of syncope, family history of sudden death, prior [Ventricular Tachycardia (VT or V-tach)](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Arrhythmias/Ventricular%20Tachycardia%20(VT%20or%20V-tach).md), extreme hypertrophy
- S4 sound = stiffened LV 
- Mitral regurgitation murmur 
![](00%20media/{4E43BE54-11ED-409E-A06F-7F9C788EE633}.png)
# Treatment 

| Treatment                                           | Reason                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Beta blockers                                       | Lower myocardial O2 demand, slow heart rate, ↓ force of contraction, ↑ diastolic filling time                                                                             |
| L-type Ca++ channel blockers (verapamil, diltiazem) | Beneficial for ventricular relaxation. Contraindicated if there is LV systolic dysfunction.                                                                               |
| Diuretics                                           | Avoid volume depletion with these, but can be used to ↓ congestion<br><br>Do not use vasodilators because they would reduce LV size, exacerbate outflow tract obstruction |
| Antiarrhythmic drugs (amiodarone, disopyramide)     | Treat A fib                                                                                                                                                               |
| Surgery like septal myectomy                        | When pharmaceuticals don't work<br><br>Could also try percutaneous septal ablation, inject dehydrated ethanol to cause a small MI, reduce septal thickness                |

Lastly (but certainly not least :3), restrictive cardiomyopathy.

# Definition
Abnormally rigid, but not necessarily thickened. Systolic function normal, diastolic filling impaired
# Etiology
- Infiltrative
	- Amyloidosis = misfolded amyloid fibrils deposited in the tissues 
		- Could be primary from Ig light-chain AL fragments, sent by plasma cell tumor like multiple myeloma 
		- Could also be transthyretin-related (TTR) amyloidosis, amyloid fibrils form from transthyretin, a transport protein produced by the liver, with infiltration typically restricted to the heart and peripheral nerves
	- Sarcoidosis (more commonly causes DCM)
- Storage diseases
	- Hemochromatosis
	- Inherited metabolic diseases
- Fibrosis
	- Radiation therapy
	- Scleroderma
- Endomyocardial disease
	- Tropical endomyocardial fibrosis
	- Hyper-eosinophilic syndrome 
- Idiopathic (might be familial)

- Compliance ↓ , ↑ diastolic pressures → ↑ systemic and pulmonary venous pressures → congestion → ↓ ventricular cavity size w ↓ filling, ↓ SV, ↓ CO 
![](00%20media/{D4334E25-30C8-4DB3-BAF2-449FE06612BF}.png)
# Epidemiology
# Prognosis
# Investigations
## CXR
- Normal heart, pulmonary congestion
## EKG
- ST and T wave abnormalities
- AV block or BBB
- Voltage reduced in amyloidosis 
- diminished QRS amplitude is a feature that helps distinguish increased echocardiographic LV wall thickness in amyloidosis from HCM (which causes augmented QRS amplitude on the ECG

- Pretty similar to constrictive pericarditis, so you could use transvenous endomyocardial biopsy, CT, and MRI to check between the two and look for constrictive pericarditis 
# Clinical features
- Fatigue
- Reduced exercise tolerance
- Systemic congestion
	- JVP
	- Peripheral edema
	- Ascites
	- Large tender liver
- Arrythmias like atrial fibrillation
- Etiologies like sarcoidosis could also cause conduction blocks 
- Pulmonary rales
- JVP can worsen with inspiration → Kussmaul sign = paradoxical rise in jugular venous pressure, or a failure of the normal fall in pressure, that occurs with inspiration. It is a clinical sign of restricted right ventricular filling, often indicating conditions such as constrictive pericarditis, cardiac tamponade, or right ventricular infarction
# Treatment 
- Na+ restriction
- Cautious use of diuretics
- Vasodilators not beneficial
- Maintain sinus rhythm, use some kind of anticoagulant
