# Definition
- Consists of two chronic autoimmune bowel diseases = Crohn’s disease and Ulcerative Colitis
- Both follow a relapsing, remitting course characterized by "flares" and increased medication requirements
- Common symptoms include recurrent episodes of abdominal pain and bloody diarrhea

# Etiology
- Theoretical triggers
	- Molecular mimicry = similarity between foreign pathogens and self-antigens leads to cross-reactive immune attacks
	- Hygiene hypothesis = lack of early exposure to parasites (which drive Th2 responses) may leave the immune system "idle," predisposing it to Th1/Th17-mediated autoimmunity
	- Gut barrier failure = in IBD, the immune system loses tolerance to the 100 trillion resident microbes (symbionts) across a single-layer epithelial wall
- Western diet is a stimulus for both, pre-packaged foods, low fiber, decreased microbial-accessible carbs, decreased microbial diversity 
- HLA-DRB mutations 
## Crohn's disease
- Th1 cells
- NOD2 mutations, makes hyperactive Th1 cells that makes the body more susceptible in general, HLA-B27 is associated with it
- Tobacco smoke is a modifiable risk factor here → think "<u>c</u>igarettes <u>c</u>ause <u>C</u>rohn's"
- IL23/Th17 disorder signaling → inflammation → local tissue damage, fibrosis, scarring, stricture, strangulation of the bowel 
- Transmural fissures and inflammation → adherence to other tissues → penetration → microperforation and abcess → fistula formation 
## Ulcerative colitis
- Th2 cells 
- HLA-B27 is associated with it
- OCP and NSAID use may exacerbate
- Pro-inflammatory cytokines → recruitment of immune cells (same old, same old) → CD4+ cells become Th2 effectors
- Colonic epithelium gets dysregulated 
### Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)
- Autoimmune disorder involves inflammation, fibrosis, and strictures in both the intra- and extra-hepatic bile ducts it is strongly associated with ulcerative colitis (UC); approximately 90% of PSC patients have inflammatory bowel disease
- Clinical features = strictures obstruct bile flow, causing jaundice, fatigue, and right upper quadrant pain
- Lab and histopathology = labs show cholestasis (elevated alkaline phosphatase and conjugated bilirubin) and positive p-ANCA in up to 80% of patients histopathology shows characteristic "onion skin" periductal fibrosis
- Diagnosis and treatment = diagnosis is confirmed via cholangiography (ERCP or MRCP), which shows biliary "beading" (strictures and dilations) treatment involves stenting or dilation of strictures and liver transplant patients require annual screening for cholangiocarcinoma
- Major risk factor for [Biliary Cancer](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Biliary%20Cancer.md)
# Epidemiology
- Basically same for both
- Same in males and females, some suggest slight female predominance 
- Bimodal distribution, at 20s and at 50s most common among whites and Jewish populations
- Most common among whites and Jewish populations; a classic presentation is a white woman of Jewish descent in her 30s
# Clinical features
- IBD Phenotypes:
	- Ulcerative Colitis (UC) = contiguous mucosal inflammation starting at the rectum and limited to the colon.
	- Crohn’s Disease = patchy, transmural inflammation that can occur anywhere in the GI tract, often leading to fistulas and strictures.
## Crohn's disease
- Dysphagia = chronic, transmural inflammation that leads to structural changes in the esophagus, such as strictures (narrowing), ulcers, and fistula formation
- Symptoms = low grade fever, weight loss, fatigue
	- Chronic diarrhea, RLQ pain or masses, malabsorption!
	- Fistulas or abcess formation 
- Characterized by granulomatous inflammation (Th1!) that is transmural (entire wall affected) it can affect any portion of the GI tract ("mouth to anus", could think <u>C</u>rohn's is <u>c</u>omplete), including oral ulcers
	- Think <u>C</u>rohn's causes non-<u>c</u>aseating granulomas
- Location = the terminal ileum is a common location, leading to malabsorption of bile salts, vitamin B12 deficiency, and right lower quadrant pain it often spares the rectum and exhibits "skip" sections
	- If someone gets complications here, then it can lead to ileocecal resection, and then they really can't reabsorb those bile acids 
	- Decreased bile acid to cholesterol ratio causes cholesterol supersaturation and predisposes to cholesterol gallstone formation, which can lead to [Cholelithiasis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Cholelithiasis.md)
- Microscopy = features non-caseating granulomas
- Gross morphology = includes "cobblestone" mucosa, fistulas (peri-anal, abdominal, or enterovesical to the bladder), "creeping fat" wrapped around the bowel, and strictures (the "string sign")
- Adenocarcinoma = risk exists only when the colon is involved
### Extra-intestinal features
- Migratory polyarthritis (knees, hips) is the most common
- Erythema nodosum (fat tissue inflammation)
- Kidney stones (calcium oxalate stones due to fat malabsorption)
- Ankylosing spondylitis
- Uveitis
## Ulcerative colitis
- Symptoms fever, skeletal issues like arthritis, PSC, erythema nodosum 
- Pathologic features = ulcers form in the intestinal tract due to inflammation of the mucosa and sometimes the submucosa; importantly, it is not full-thickness inflammation
- Location = it always starts in the rectum and works upward rectal involvement is constant, and left lower quadrant pain is common it never involves the small intestine
- Morphology = grossly, it presents with pseudopolyps (healing ulcers) and a loss of haustra, which creates a "lead pipe appearance" on x-ray or CT
- Microscopy = features crypt abscesses with PMN (polymorphonuclear leukocyte) infiltration
- Extra-intestinal features = includes pyoderma gangrenosum (deep, necrotic skin ulcers), primary sclerosing cholangitis, ankylosing spondylitis, and uveitis
# Investigations
## Differentials
- Bloody diarrhea has many causes, particularly infection diagnostic
	- Studies often include stool cultures for salmonella, shigella, campylobacter, yersinia, e coli o157 =h7, c diff, and ova and parasites, see [Acute GI Infections](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Acute%20GI%20Infections.md)
## Crohn's disease
- Patient comes in, seen for chronic diarrhea
- Rule out infectious causes using blood and stool studies, see [Acute GI Infections](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Acute%20GI%20Infections.md)
- Use endoscopy with histological examination, cross-sectional studies (CT, MRI), and the lab studies to make the diagnosis
### Histological exam
- Use ileocolonoscopy 
	- Skip lesions = segmental and/or discontinuous pattern of involvement
	- Cobblestone (<u>C</u>rohn's is for <u>c</u>obblestone, ha! ok i'll stop)
- Remember the non-caseating granulomas!
- Will also see lymphoid aggregates, hypertrophic lymph nodes
### Cross-sectional studies
- Ingestion of osmotic contrast, MR enterography, CT enterography
- Edematous thickening of intestinal wall
- Creeping fat = excessive mesenteric fat around segments of bowel → transmural inflammation → bacteria to move from the gut lumen to the mesenteric fat, causing it to undergo hypertrophy and "creep" around the affected intestinal segments
	- Fat becomes a source of inflammation through adipokines and cytokines 
	- <u>C</u>rohn's is for <u>c</u>reeping fat mwahaha
- Strictures, fistulas 
### Lab studies 
- Inflammatory markers, CRP, ESR
- Iron studies, B12, folate to evaluate for [Anemia](Hematology/Anemia.md)
- Consider genetic testing 
- Fecal calprotectin or fecal lactoferrin → markers of intestinal inflammation → fecal calprotectin is released by neutrophils, and lactoferrin indicates active bacterial infection 
- Antibodies = elevated anti-saccharomyces cerevisiae antibodies (ASCA) are seen
## Ulcerative colitis
- Antibodies = p-ANCA is often seen in UC, though it is not reliable for routine clinical use
- Lab studies to rule out [Acute GI Infections](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Acute%20GI%20Infections.md), ileocolonoscopy with biopsy, CT or MRI if can't do endoscopy 
- Inflammatory markers, CRP, ESR
- Again, fecal calprotectin 
- Will see a friable mucosa with bleeding on contact with endoscope, and small mucosal ulcerations
- Loss of haustra and folds, will see pseudopolyps
- Will see the lead-pipe appearance 
- Crypt abscesses on histology → infiltration of neutrophils into the lumen of intestinal crypts
### Classifications
- Montreal classification
	- Ulcerative proctitis = limited to rectum
	- Left-sided UC = limited to colon distal to splenic flexure 
	- Extensive UC = proximal to splenic flexure 
- Can also use American College of Gastroenterology ulcerative colitis activity index, based on stools per day, frequency of blood in stool, urgency, hemoglobin, ESR, CRP, fecal calprotectin
	- Classified based on mild, moderate-to-severe, fulminant 
# Treatment 
- Immunology = UC is a th2-mediated disorder, while Crohn’s is th1-mediated 
- Smoking improves outcomes in UC but worsens outcomes in Crohn’s
- Use Montreal and Vienna classifications to determine severity and treatment outcomes 
- Generally, glucocorticoids, biologics, immunomodulators, 5-ASA derivatives 
- Pain management, loperamide in some cases, smoking cessation, avoid NSAIDs, identify and treat deficiencies 
- Surgery
	- If complications like abscesses or bowel obstructions
- Screen for intestinal cancers
## Specific treatments
- Treatments = include corticosteroids, azathioprine, methotrexate, 6-mp, and biologics like infliximab/adalimumab
- Sulfasalazine = a combination of sulfapyridine and 5-asa activated by colonic bacteria; it is ideal for UC because it is not active until it reaches the colon
	- Side effects include GI upset, sulfonamide hypersensitivity, and reversible oligospermia in men
- 5-asa (mesalamine) = pure 5-asa has fewer side effects but is absorbed too early in the jejunum; therefore, it is used in delayed-release capsules (eg, asacol, pentasa) to reach the colon
- Combination therapy = adding thiopurines (6-MP/Azathioprine) to biologics reduces B-cell counts, which helps prevent the formation of anti-drug antibodies that would otherwise neutralize the biologic
- Fecal microbiota transplant (FMT) = an experimental therapy for IBD, particularly ulcerative colitis (UC), involving transferring healthy donor stool to restore gut microbial balance
	- Highly effective for recurrent _C. difficile_ in IBD patients, its use for inducing remission in UC shows promise but has conflicting results
# Prognosis
## Crohn's disease
- Fistulizing CDs, especially to the bladder or vagina
- Impaired bile acid reabsorption
- Weight loss
- Failure to thrive in children
- [Anemia](Hematology/Anemia.md)
## Ulcerative colitis
- Toxic megacolon = a rare complication where colonic contractions cease, leading to rapid intestinal dilation, wall thinning, and potential perforation
	- Presentation includes pain, distention, fever, diarrhea, shock, and shock
- Adenocarcinoma = there is a significant risk based on the duration (usually >10 years) and extent of the disease (eg, pancolitis)
	- Screening colonoscopy with multiple biopsies is recommended
