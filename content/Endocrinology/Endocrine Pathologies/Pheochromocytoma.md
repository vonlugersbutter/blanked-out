# Definition
A catecholamine-secreting tumor of the adrenal medulla symptoms include episodic hypertension, headaches, and palpitations. 
# Etiology
- Approximately 40% of these tumors are familial, involving germline mutations in roughly 20 different genes
- Key syndromes include [Multiple Endocrine Neoplasias](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Multiple%20Endocrine%20Neoplasias.md) specifically MEN2, von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) disease, and neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1)
- Mutations are categorized into three clusters = cluster 1 (hypoxia response), cluster 2 (kinase signaling), and cluster 3 (Wnt signaling)
# Epidemiology

# Clinical features
- Classical symptoms include forceful heartbeat (palpitations), headache, tremor, and diaphoresis (sweating)
- Hypertension may be sustained or paroxysmal; some patients remain normotensive between spells
- Spells typically last 15–20 minutes and can be precipitated by exercise, anxiety, or certain medications
# Investigations
- Requires measuring increased concentrations of fractionated metanephrines and catecholamines in urine or plasma
- Once confirmed biochemically, tumors are localized using CT or MRI of the abdomen and pelvis
- $^{68}$ Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT and $^{123}$ I-MIBG scintigraphy are used for negative standard imaging or suspected metastatic disease
- Confounding issues
	- Foods like bananas, coffee, peppers, and pineapples can artificially elevate certain catecholamine metabolites
	- Labetalol and sotalol interfere with assays → both beta blockers
	- Tricyclic antidepressants and amphetamines increase catecholamine excretion
	- Acetaminophen can cause confounding peaks in HPLC chromatograms
	- Anxiety, vigorous exercise, pain, and acute illness like MI
# Prognosis

# Treatment 
- Pre op management
	- Phenoxybenzamine (irreversible α blocker) for 7 to 10 days
	- Non-selective beta blockers (propranolol) 
- Surgery → but be careful as there is risk of ↑ catecholamines 
	- Usually performed laparoscopically for solitary adrenal tumors
