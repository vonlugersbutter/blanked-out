# Definition
Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone (SIADH) occurs when vasopressin is secreted inappropriately relative to osmolality and volume, leading to hyponatremia
# Etiology
- Chronic water retention causes volume expansion, which triggers natriuresis (sodium excretion) to normalize volume, worsening the hyponatremia
- Kidney eventually adapts to chronic excess vasopressin by reducing aquaporin-2 density, allowing for some water excretion despite high hormone levels
- Common causes include ectopic production by tumors (eg, small cell lung cancer), CNS disorders, pulmonary diseases, and various drugs
# Epidemiology

# Clinical features
- Rapid-onset hyponatremia is dangerous and can cause cerebral edema, seizures, or coma 
- Chronic hyponatremia is often better tolerated but still requires careful management
# Investigations
- Clinical criteria
	- (1) Decreased effective osmolality (<275 mOsm/kg)
	- (2) inappropriate urinary concentration (>100 mOsm/kg)
	- (3) clinical euvolemia
	- (4) elevated urinary sodium excretion
# Prognosis

# Treatment 
- fluid restriction
- Acute, symptomatic cases may require hypertonic saline
- Vaptans (eg, tolvaptan, conivaptan) are V2 receptor antagonists that specifically promote water excretion without sodium loss
- Correction of chronic hyponatremia must be slow (typically <10–12 mEq/L in 24 hours) to avoid osmotic demyelination (central pontine myelinolysis), a severe neurologic condition caused by rapid brain cell shrinkage
