# Definition
- BMI = the standard surrogate measure for body fatness, calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared ($kg/m^2$)
- Adult classifications
	- Underweight = bmi < 18.5
	- Healthy weight = bmi 18.5–24.9
	- Overweight = bmi 25.0–29.9
	- Obese = bmi ≥ 30
- Ethnic variations = standard cut-points may not reflect metabolic risk across all races for south and east asian adults, the recommended cut-points are lower = ≥ 23 for overweight and ≥ 25 for obesity
- Pediatrics = for children and adolescents, bmi between the 85th and 95th percentile for age and sex is "at risk" of overweight, while bmi at or above the 95th percentile is considered "overweight or obese"

# Etiology
![](00%20media/{4AD61B3F-CD12-49DF-B073-316CC2926DD1}.png)

- biological bias = the physiological system controlling intake is more strongly biased toward preventing starvation and energy deficiency rather than preventing excess storage
- afferent hormonal signals
	- Leptin = a cytokine-like protein released by adipocytes in proportion to fat mass it informs the brain of energy store levels; a decrease in leptin increases food consumption and minimizes energy expenditure
	- Ghrelin = secreted by the stomach, its levels increase before meals and stimulate food intake (orexigenic)
	- Cholecystokinin (cck) = secreted by the proximal intestine in response to fat and protein; it promotes meal termination and reduces meal size
	- Glp-1 = secreted by the distal gut; it inhibits food intake, decreases gastric emptying, and increases insulin secretion
	- Pyy = released after food ingestion to reduce appetite obese individuals often show lower pyy levels after meals compared to non-obese controls
- arcuate nucleus (arc) = the primary hypothalamic site for receiving energy status signals
	- Agrp/npy neurons = stimulate appetite (orexigenic) and are inhibited by leptin
	- Pomc neurons = suppress appetite (anorexigenic) and are activated by leptin pomc is processed into $\alpha$ -msh, which signals satiety
	- Paraventricular nucleus (pvn) = contains the mc4r (melanocortin-4 receptor)
	- $\alpha$ -msh activates mc4r to inhibit appetite
	- Agrp acts as an antagonist to mc4r, blocking satiety and increasing food intake
- heritability = genetic factors account for 40% to 70% of the population variation in bmi
- common variants = the fto gene is the most prominent common variant associated with an increased risk of obesity
- monogenic obesity = mutations in the mc4r gene represent the most common genetic cause of severe obesity, accounting for 25% of cases rare recessive syndromes involve mutations in leptin, the leptin receptor, or pomc
# Epidemiology
- united states = the prevalence of obesity more than doubled from 150% in 1980 to 329% by 2004 by 2030, it is projected that 863% of us adults will be overweight or obese
- global impact = in 2005, an estimated 937 million adults were overweight and 396 million were obese; projections suggest these numbers could reach 216 billion and 112 billion, respectively, by 2030
- causal complexity = beyond simple imbalances in diet and exercise, obesity is driven by a multifaceted "obesity epidemic" involving social norms, governmental and media influence, behavioral settings, genetics, and the microbiome
# Clinical features
- endocrine dysfunction = adipose tissue is an active endocrine organ secreting adipokines
- adiponectin = improves insulin sensitivity but decreases as fat mass increases
- inflammation = obesity is a state of chronic low-grade inflammation infiltrating macrophages release proinflammatory cytokines like tnf-$\alpha$, which directly impair insulin signaling
- metabolic & cardiovascular = over 80% of type 2 diabetes (t2dm) patients are obese obesity is a major risk factor for dyslipidemia (high triglycerides, low hdl), hypertension, heart failure, and atrial fibrillation
- other systems
	- Pulmonary = obstructive sleep apnea (osa) caused by mechanical effects and airway inflammation
	- Gastrointestinal = nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (nafld), with a 50%–85% prevalence in obese individuals
	- Cancer = increased risk for breast, endometrial, colorectal, and prostate cancers

# Treatment 
- lifestyle = diet and exercise together are most effective success is often defined as maintaining a 10% weight loss for at least one year through high physical activity (~1 hour/day) and low-calorie diets
- pharmacotherapy = fda-approved options include phentermine (appetite suppressant), orlistat (lipase inhibitor), and liraglutide (glp-1 receptor agonist)
- bariatric surgery = recommended for bmi ≥ 40 or bmi ≥ 35 with comorbidities
- roux-en-y gastric bypass (rygb) = the most common procedure; it sustains weight loss through neuroendocrine changes, such as an exaggerated pyy response
- effectiveness = surgery leads to significant resolution of t2dm (~77%) and osa (~86%)
