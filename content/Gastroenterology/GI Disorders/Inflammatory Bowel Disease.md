# Definition
- Consists of two chronic autoimmune bowel diseases = Crohn’s disease and Ulcerative Colitis
- Both follow a relapsing, remitting course characterized by "flares" and increased medication requirements
- Common symptoms include recurrent episodes of abdominal pain and bloody diarrhea
# Etiology
- Theoretical triggers
	- Molecular mimicry = similarity between foreign pathogens and self-antigens leads to cross-reactive immune attacks
	- Hygiene hypothesis = lack of early exposure to parasites (which drive Th2 responses) may leave the immune system "idle," predisposing it to Th1/Th17-mediated autoimmunity
	- Gut barrier failure = in IBD, the immune system loses tolerance to the 100 trillion resident microbes (symbionts) across a single-layer epithelial wall
- Western diet is a stimulus for both, pre-packaged foods, low fiber, decreased microbial-accessible carbs, decreased microbial diversity 
- HLA-DRB mutations 
## Crohn's disease
- Th1 cells
- NOD2 mutations, makes hyperactive Th1 cells that makes the body more susceptible in general
- Characterized by granulomatous inflammation that is transmural (entire wall affected) it can affect any portion of the GI tract ("mouth to anus"), including oral ulcers
## Ulcerative colitis
- Th2 cells 
### Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)
- Autoimmune disorder involves inflammation, fibrosis, and strictures in both the intra- and extra-hepatic bile ducts it is strongly associated with ulcerative colitis (uc); approximately 90% of psc patients have inflammatory bowel disease
- Clinical features = strictures obstruct bile flow, causing jaundice, fatigue, and right upper quadrant pain
- Lab and histopathology = labs show cholestasis (elevated alkaline phosphatase and conjugated bilirubin) and positive p-ANCA in up to 80% of patients histopathology shows characteristic "onion skin" periductal fibrosis
- Diagnosis and treatment = diagnosis is confirmed via cholangiography (ERCP or MRCP), which shows biliary "beading" (strictures and dilations) treatment involves stenting or dilation of strictures and liver transplant patients require annual screening for cholangiocarcinoma
# Epidemiology
- Slight female predominance, and the age of onset is typically 15 to 40 years, with a possible second spike between ages 50 and 80
- Most common among whites and Jewish populations; a classic presentation is a white woman of Jewish descent in her 30s

## Ulcerative colitis
- Toxic megacolon = a rare complication where colonic contractions cease, leading to rapid intestinal dilation, wall thinning, and potential perforation
	- Presentation includes pain, distention, fever, diarrhea, shock, and shock
- Adenocarcinoma = there is a significant risk based on the duration (usually >10 years) and extent of the disease (eg, pancolitis)
	- Screening colonoscopy with multiple biopsies is recommended
# Investigations
## Differentials
- Bloody diarrhea has many causes, particularly infection diagnostic
	- Studies often include stool cultures for salmonella, shigella, campylobacter, yersinia, e coli o157 =h7, c diff, and ova and parasites
## Crohn's disease
- Antibodies = elevated anti-saccharomyces cerevisiae antibodies (ASCA) are seen
## Ulcerative colitis
- Antibodies = p-ANCA is often seen in UC, though it is not reliable for routine clinical use
# Clinical features
- IBD Phenotypes:
	- Ulcerative Colitis (UC): Contiguous mucosal inflammation starting at the rectum and limited to the colon.
	- Crohn’s Disease: Patchy, transmural inflammation that can occur anywhere in the GI tract, often leading to fistulas and strictures.
## Crohn's disease
- Dysphagia = chronic, transmural inflammation that leads to structural changes in the esophagus, such as strictures (narrowing), ulcers, and fistula formation
- Location = the terminal ileum is a common location, leading to malabsorption of bile salts, vitamin B12 deficiency, and right lower quadrant pain it often spares the rectum and exhibits "skip" sections
- Microscopy = features non-caseating granulomas
- Gross morphology = includes "cobblestone" mucosa, fistulas (peri-anal, abdominal, or enterovesical to the bladder), "creeping fat" wrapped around the bowel, and strictures (the "string sign")
- Adenocarcinoma = risk exists only when the colon is involved
- Extra-intestinal features = migratory polyarthritis (knees, hips) is the most common
	- Others include erythema nodosum (fat tissue inflammation), kidney stones (calcium oxalate stones due to fat malabsorption), ankylosing spondylitis, and uveitis
## Ulcerative colitis
- Pathologic features = ulcers form in the intestinal tract due to inflammation of the mucosa and sometimes the submucosa; importantly, it is not full-thickness inflammation
- Location = it always starts in the rectum and works upward rectal involvement is constant, and left lower quadrant pain is common it never involves the small intestine
- Morphology = grossly, it presents with pseudopolyps (healing ulcers) and a loss of haustra, which creates a "lead pipe appearance" on x-ray or CT
- Microscopy = features crypt abscesses with PMN (polymorphonuclear leukocyte) infiltration
- Extra-intestinal features = includes pyoderma gangrenosum (deep, necrotic skin ulcers), primary sclerosing cholangitis, ankylosing spondylitis, and uveitis
# Treatment 
- Immunology = UC is a th2-mediated disorder, while Crohn’s is th1-mediated 
- Smoking improves outcomes in UC but worsens outcomes in Crohn’s
- Treatments = include corticosteroids, azathioprine, methotrexate, 6-mp, and biologics like infliximab/adalimumab
- Sulfasalazine = a combination of sulfapyridine and 5-asa activated by colonic bacteria; it is ideal for UC because it is not active until it reaches the colon
	- Side effects include GI upset, sulfonamide hypersensitivity, and reversible oligospermia in men
- 5-asa (mesalamine) = pure 5-asa has fewer side effects but is absorbed too early in the jejunum; therefore, it is used in delayed-release capsules (eg, asacol, pentasa) to reach the colon
- Combination therapy = adding thiopurines (6-MP/Azathioprine) to biologics reduces B-cell counts, which helps prevent the formation of anti-drug antibodies that would otherwise neutralize the biologic.
