- Preconceptional health encompasses the overall health of nonpregnant women during their reproductive years
- Critical Timing = Because embryos begin to form before most women realize they are pregnant (often 1 to 2 weeks after a missed period), many preventive steps, such as initiating folic acid, must happen before conception to be effective
- The "Fourth Trimester" = This postpartum period is an optimal time for preconceptional counseling, as it sets the stage for future pregnancies and long-term health

Also see [[Prenatal Care and Screening]].
## Medical history 
### [Diabetes](Diabetes.md)
- Hyperglycemia is linked to major congenital malformations
- Goal = Maintain a preconceptional hemoglobin A1c level below 7 percent
- Higher glycosylated hemoglobin values in the first trimester directly correlate with increased risks for major malformations
### Epilepsy
- Women with seizure disorders have an increased risk of neonates with structural anomalies
- Treatment goals include using monotherapy and the least teratogenic medications possible
- Valproic acid should be avoided if possible due to its high association with malformations, see [Teratogens](Teratogens.md)
- Women are advised to take a daily 4-mg oral folic acid supplement
### Immunizations 
- Check which vaccines need to be up to date, as some have contraindications described in [Prenatal Care and Screening](Prenatal%20Care%20and%20Screening.md)
### Genetic screening
- Constructing a pedigree
- Neural-Tube Defects (NTDs) = Universal supplementation with 400 to 800 μg of folic acid daily is recommended for all women who may become pregnant to reduce the risk of NTDs
- Phenylketonuria (PKU) = high maternal blood phenylalanine levels can cross the placenta and damage fetal organs
- Specific Populations = Individuals of Ashkenazi Jewish descent should be offered screening for various autosomal recessive disorders, such as Tay-Sachs and Gaucher disease
	- Those of high-risk ancestry for thalassemias (Mediterranean, Southeast Asian) should be offered carrier screening
## Parental age
- Maternal Age
	- Adolescents (<19) = Higher risks for anemia, preeclampsia, and preterm delivery
	- Advanced Age (>35) = Higher risks for chronic conditions (hypertension, diabetes), fetal aneuploidy, and pregnancy-related death Complication rates rise significantly after age 35 (Figure 9-3)
- Paternal Age = Advanced paternal age is linked to a higher risk of preterm birth and new autosomal-dominant mutations, including complex neuropsychiatric conditions
- Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART) = ART is a major cause of multifetal gestations, which carry higher risks of preterm delivery and placental complications There is also data linking ART to slightly higher rates of major congenital malformations
## Social history
- Recreational Substances = Nonjudgmental screening for alcohol (using tools like the TACE questions), tobacco, and other drugs is vital
	- Smoking cessation before pregnancy mitigates many adverse perinatal risks
- Environmental Exposures = Excess lead exposure (>5 µg/dL) requires counseling; levels >45 µg/dL may require chelation Electromagnetic fields (microwaves, cell phones) are not linked to adverse outcomes
- Diet and Nutrition 
	- Pica = Craving nonfood items should be discouraged as it replaces healthful nutrition
	- Eating Disorders = Anorexia and bulimia increase the risk of low birthweight and microcephaly
	- Obesity = Linked to hypertension, gestational diabetes, and structural fetal anomalies
- Exercise = Conditioned women can usually continue exercising, but they should avoid exhaustion, overheating, and lying flat on their back (supine position) as pregnancy progresses
- Intimate-Partner Violence (IPV) = Because pregnancy can escalate abuse, the preconceptional period is a critical time for screening and intervention
- LGBTQ+ Individuals = Providers should offer quality care regardless of sexual orientation, noting that lesbian and bisexual women may have higher incidences of certain risks like obesity and tobacco use
