# Definition
- Obstetrical hemorrhage is one of the "infamous triad" of maternal death causes, alongside infection and hypertension
# Etiology
- Uterine Atony = Failure of the uterus to contract after delivery
- Abnormal Placentation = Includes placenta previa, placental abruption, and placenta accreta spectrum (PAS)
- Trauma = Injuries to the birth canal such as episiotomies, lacerations, and uterine rupture
- Coagulopathy = Disseminated intravascular coagulopathy (DIC)
- Obstetrical Factors = High parity, obstructed labor, tachysystole, and intrauterine manipulation (such as midforceps rotation or breech extraction)
# Epidemiology
- Single most important cause of maternal death worldwide
# Clinical features
The volume of blood lost varies significantly based on the mode of delivery
- Vaginal Delivery = Roughly 62% of cases involve a loss of < 500 mL
- Repeat Cesarean Delivery = The majority of cases (58%) result in a loss of 500–1000 mL
- Cesarean and Hysterectomy = This procedure carries the highest risk, with 47% of cases losing more than 2500 mL
- Volume Estimation Formulas
	- Blood Volume (mL) = $\frac{[Height (inches) \times 50] + [Weight (pounds) \times 25]}{2}$
- Hematocrit Correlation = Blood loss can be estimated as the sum of the calculated pregnancy-added volume plus 500 mL for every 3-volume-percent decline in the hematocrit
# Investigations
## Risk assessment
- Scoring Tools = Tools developed by the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) provide stratification but are only modest predictors of actual hemorrhage
- AWHONN Tool = The Association of Women’s Health, Obstetric and Neonatal Nurses (AWHONN) assessment tool has been shown to be predictive for identifying high-risk patients
# Prognosis
## Complications
- Uterine Inversion = A rare but critical emergency that can lead to rapid exsanguination Immediate recognition is vital for a good outcome; management may include the use of intrauterine tamponade balloons
- Puerperal Hematomas = Often associated with operative vaginal deliveries (forceps or vacuum) or episiotomies, these can also occur due to vessel rupture without a visible laceration
- Uterine Rupture = Risks include grandmultiparity, advanced maternal age, and previous uterine surgeries like laparoscopy or hysteroscopy
# Treatment 
The provided management flow outlines a step-by-step response to postpartum hemorrhage (defined as an estimated blood loss $\geq$ 1000 mL) =
## Immediate Stabilization
- Determine the cause and massage the uterus
- Establish large-bore IV access (two 16-gauge needles) and infuse 1000 mL of crystalloid fluids
- Administer oxytocin rapidly and place a Foley catheter
- Send laboratory tests, including a CBC and Type and Cross
## Management of Refractory Hemorrhage 
- Mobilize help and move the patient to the operating room if feasible
- Call for blood products and consider a massive transfusion protocol
- Monitor coagulation status (PT/INR, PTT, fibrinogen level)
## Specific Interventions by Cause
- For Uterine Atony = Administer uterotonics such as methylergonovine, 15-methyl $PGF_{2\alpha}$ (Hemabate), or misoprostol If bleeding continues, utilize balloon tamponade or compression sutures before proceeding to angiographic embolization or hysterectomy
- For Lacerations = Surgical repair, evaluation of uterine defects or hematomas, and potential vaginal packing
- For Retained Placenta = Manual removal is indicated if heavy bleeding persists while the placenta remains attached
