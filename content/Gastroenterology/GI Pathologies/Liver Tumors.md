# Definition
# Etiology
- Common precursors include hepatitis b and c, alcoholic cirrhosis, [Wilson's Disease](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Wilson's%20Disease.md), [Hemochromatosis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Hemochromatosis.md), and $\alpha$ -1 antitrypsin deficiency
- Aspergillus and aflatoxin = the fungus aspergillus produces aflatoxin, which can contaminate crops like corn, soybeans, and peanuts high dietary intake of aflatoxin is associated with HCC, though industrialized countries typically screen for this toxin
- Hepatic adenoma = benign epithelial tumors, usually solitary and located in the right lobe they are most common in young women (20s to 40s) and are strongly associated with contraceptive use and anabolic steroids there are rare case reports of these tumors rupturing during pregnancy
- Hepatic hemangioma (cavernous hemangioma) = most common benign liver tumor, consisting of vascular spaces often filled with thrombus they are usually discovered incidentally, but biopsy carries a risk of fatal hemorrhage
- Hepatic angiosarcoma = a rare, high-grade malignant vascular tumor associated with toxic exposures such as arsenic and vinyl chloride (used in PVC plastic)
- Metastasis to the liver = metastatic nodules are the most common malignancies found in the liver, occurring much more frequently than primary HCC
	- Common primary sites include the GI tract (colon, stomach, pancreas), breast, and lung
# Epidemiology
- Hepatocellular carcinoma is the most common primary liver tumor, usually occurring as a consequence of chronic liver disease
# Prognosis
# Investigations
- Diagnosis of HCC involves monitoring alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels, imaging (CT, MRI, ultrasound), and biopsy
# Clinical features
## Hepatocellular carcinoma
- Patients are often asymptomatic, leading to the need for regular screening in high-risk populations physical findings may include hepatomegaly and signs of liver failure such as obstructive jaundice and ascites
- Paraneoplastic syndromes = large tumors may cause hypoglycemia due to high metabolic rates or the production of insulin-like growth factor-ii
	- HCC can also secrete EPO, leading to erythrocytosis additionally, it can cause Budd-Chiari syndrome, characterized by the classic triad of abdominal pain, ascites, and hepatomegaly due to the occlusion of hepatic veins
# Treatment 
- HCC is poorly responsive to chemotherapy or radiation treatments include surgical excision, liver transplantation, radiofrequency ablation, and chemoembolization, but the overall prognosis is poor with a median survival of 6 to 20 months
