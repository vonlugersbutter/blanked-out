- Follicular phase = characterized by increased GnRH pulse frequency and FSH, leading to estradiol production and the selection of a dominant follicle
- Ovulation = a mid-cycle surge where estradiol triggers a massive LH surge, releasing the oocyte roughly 36 hours later
	- Often associated with mittelschmerz = mild, unilateral mid-cycle pain
- Luteal phase = follicle becomes the corpus luteum, producing high levels of progesterone
	- If fertilization does not occur, it degrades, leading to menstruation
- Uterine cycle = parallels the ovarian cycle with two phases
	- Proliferative = estrogen-stimulated growth of the endometrium
	- Secretory = progesterone-driven preparation for an embryo involving the formation of spiral arteries
		- Menstruation occurs when progesterone levels fall, causing these arteries to vasoconstrict and the endometrium to shed

## Clinical conditions
- Amenorrhea = primary (failure of menses at puberty) or secondary (cessation of normal menses)
- Secondary amenorrhea is most commonly caused by pregnancy, but can also result from thyroid disease, prolactinomas, or low body weight
- Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS) = driven by an elevated LH:FSH ratio, often linked to obesity and hyperinsulinemia
	- Symptoms include hirsutism, acne, infertility, and multiple follicular cysts
	- Increase in LH → ↑ androgen production from theca cells 
- Menopause = permanent cessation of periods (median age 51) due to the depletion of ovarian follicles
	- Loss of estradiol and a significant early increase in FSH, and maybe also LH 
	- Hot flashes, vaginal atrophy, and an increased risk of osteoporosis and cardiovascular disease
