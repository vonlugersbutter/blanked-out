# Definition
Excess mineralocorticoids causing hypertension and hypokalemia diagnosed by a low plasma renin activity (PRA) and high plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC).
# Etiology
- Causes of primary aldosteronism
	- Bilateral idiopathic hyperaldosteronism
	- Aldosterone-producing adenoma (Conn's syndrome) = unilateral tumor
- Characterized by hypertension, suppressed plasma renin, and increased aldosterone
- Familial aldosteronism
	- FH Type I (Glucocorticoid-Remediable Aldosteronism - GRA) = caused by a chimeric gene duplication that places aldosterone synthase under the control of ACTH rather than angiotensin II 
		- Can be treated and suppressed using glucocorticoid therapy
		- Notably associated with a high frequency (20%) of intracranial aneurysms
	- FH Type II = autosomal dominant disorder that presents as either APA or IHA
	- FH Type III = associated with germline mutations in the KCNJ5 potassium channel, same ones as the one that would produce Conn's syndrome 
# Epidemiology
- Estimated at 5%–10% of all hypertensive patients and over 20% of those with resistant hypertension
# Clinical features
- Most patients have normal potassium
- High-normal or slightly elevated serum sodium is a useful clue
# Investigations
- Morning seated measurement of plasma aldosterone + renin
- Oral or intravenous salt loading tests to verify autonomous aldosterone production
- Adrenal CT scan followed by adrenal venous sampling (AVS), which is the "gold standard" for distinguishing unilateral from bilateral disease
	- Check which side is producing the excess aldosterone 
# Prognosis

# Treatment 
- Unilateral disease (APA) = laparoscopic unilateral adrenalectomy can provide a surgical cure or significant improvement
- Bilateral disease (IHA) = medical management with spironolactone or eplerenone, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists
