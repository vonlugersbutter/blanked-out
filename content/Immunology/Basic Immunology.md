- Innate Immunity: Characterized as a "radar" system utilizing pattern recognition of surface molecules consistent across non-human organisms. It lacks specificity and memory
- Adaptive Immunity: Mediated by B and T lymphocytes
- The lymphatic system drains interstitial fluid from the periphery; circulating dendritic cells sample, process, and present antigens in lymph nodes for immune action or inaction

## Adaptive immunity
### T cells
- CD4+ T-Cell Subsets:
	- Th1: Targeted at intracellular pathogens (viruses); utilizes IFN-gamma and TNF-alpha
	- Th2: Targeted at helminths (worms) and parasites; mediates allergic responses via histamine
	- Th17: Targeted at extracellular bacteria; recruits neutrophils via IL-17
- Immune Regulation: Regulatory T-cells (Tregs), characterized by the FoxP3 gene, act as "military police" to suppress the proliferation of self-reactive effector cells and maintain homeostasis.
## Autoimmunity
### Basic mechanisms
- Loss of Central Tolerance: Prototypical disease is Autoimmune Polyendocrinopathy Syndrome Type 1 (APS-1/APECED); mutations in the AIRE gene prevent adequate T-cell education in the thymus.
- Loss of Peripheral Tolerance: Prototypical disease is IPEX Syndrome (X-linked); a lack of FOXP3 protein leads to Treg dysfunction, resulting in polyendocrinopathy and enteropathy.
- Theoretical Triggers for Injury:
	- Molecular Mimicry: Foreign antigens share peptide sequences with self-antigens (e.g., homology between GAD and Coxsackie B virus).
	- Bystander Activation: Local cytokine release from viral infections or activated T-cells triggers an immune response.
	- Abnormal MHC Expression: Induction of HLA Class II molecules on non-immune cells (e.g., thyroid follicular cells) by IFN-gamma
