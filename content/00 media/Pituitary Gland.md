- Pituitary sits in the sella turcica and is connected to the hypothalamus via the pituitary stalk
# Blood supply
- Anterior pituitary supply = the superior hypophyseal arteries form a primary capillary plexus at the median eminence
	- Hypophysiotropic peptides enter this plexus and are transported via long hypophyseal portal veins down the infundibular stalk to a secondary plexus in the anterior pituitary
	- Secondary plexus consists of fenestrated sinusoid capillaries, allowing neuropeptides to diffuse easily to reach their target cells
- Posterior pituitary supply = middle and inferior hypophyseal arteries
	- Short portal vessels provide venous connections between the neural lobe and the anterior lobe, allowing neuropeptides released from the posterior pituitary to influence cells in the anterior pituitary
- Systemic drainage = blood from both lobes drains into the intercavernous sinus, then into the internal jugular vein
# Posterior pituitary
- Posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis) = secretes ADH (vasopressin) and oxytocin, which are produced in the hypothalamus → both nonapeptides
- Precursor includes a signal peptide, the hormone, a neurophysin, and (for avp) a glycopeptide called copeptin following cleavage in the er, the precursor moves through the golgi apparatus and is packaged into neurosecretory vesicles for axonal transport
- Neurophysins = by-products of prohormone processing are essential for the proper transport and targeting of hormones to secretory granules
	- Mutations in neurophysins can lead to the retention of precursors in the er, resulting in neuronal cell death and familial neurogenic diabetes insipidus
- Release mechanism = release is triggered by neuronal depolarization, which opens voltage-gated calcium channels at the axon terminals. resulting calcium influx facilitates the docking and exocytosis of vesicle contents into the extracellular space
## Oxytocin
- Principal stimuli = mechanical stimulation of the uterine cervix by the fetus and the suckling of the nipple
- Receptor and pathway = ot binds to gq/11 protein-coupled receptors, activating phospholipase c (plc) this leads to an increase in inositol trisphosphate (ip3) and cytosolic calcium, which activates myosin light chain kinase (mlck) to produce smooth muscle contraction
- Physiologic effects = stimulates milk ejection in the lactating breast and rhythmic uterine contractions during labor it also promotes uterine involution postpartum
- Sensitivity to ot increases dramatically toward the end of pregnancy due to upregulation of ot receptors (mediated by estrogen) and increased gap-junction formation between smooth muscle cells
- Vasodilators like beta-agonists and NO go against the actions of oxytocin

# Anterior pituitary
- anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis) = contains five cell types secreting hormones = corticotrophs (ACTH), thyrotrophs (TSH), gonadotrophs (LH/FSH), somatotrophs (GH), and lactotrophs (prolactin)
- Prolactin = regulates milk production; under inhibitory control by dopamine from the hypothalamus
	- Excess prolactin (hyperprolactinemia) can cause galactorrhea and amenorrhea in women, and decreased libido or impotence in men
- Growth hormone (GH/somatotropin) = important for linear growth in childhood
	- Regulation = stimulated by GHRH and sleep; inhibited by glucose and somatostatin
	- Effects = GH stimulates the liver to produce IGF-1 (somatomedin), which mediates growth effects on bone and muscle
	- Direct effects of GH include raising blood sugar (diabetogenic effect) and promoting lipolysis
	- Pathology = excess GH causes gigantism in children and acromegaly in adults (enlarged jaw, hands, and feet; visceral organ enlargement; insulin resistance)
# Pituitary disorders
- Hypopituitarism = damage to the anterior pituitary from tumors, ischemia, or hemorrhage
- Sheehan syndrome = pituitary infarction following postpartum hemorrhage
- Pituitary apoplexy = sudden hemorrhage into the pituitary, often a pre-existing adenoma, causing severe headache and vision loss
- Empty sella syndrome = the sella turcica fills with CSF, occasionally compressing the pituitary
