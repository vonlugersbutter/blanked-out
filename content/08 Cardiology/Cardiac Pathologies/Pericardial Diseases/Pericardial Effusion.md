# Definition
Too much fluid in the pericardial space.
# Etiology
- Associated with [Acute Pericarditis](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Pericardial%20Diseases/Acute%20Pericarditis.md)
- Noninflammatory serous effusions
	- Increased capillary permeability in hypothyroidism
	- Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure in [Heart Failure](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Heart%20Failure.md)
	- Decreased plasma oncotic pressure 
	- Chylous effusions = lymphatic obstructions from TB or cancer 
- Pericardium is not great at accommodating lots of fluid, very stiff
- Clinical cardiac compression factors
	- Volume of fluid
	- Rate at which it accumulates
		- Sudden increase = lots of pericardial pressure 
		- Pericardium can stretch a little if it's over time 
	- Compliance of pericardium

# Investigations
## Physical exam findings
- Soft heart sounds, insulation
- Friction rub reduced intensity
- Dullness to percussion over posterior left lung over angle of scapula (Ewart sign)
## CXR
- May be larger, in globular symmetric fashion
## EKG
- Height of QRS complex varies → electrical alternans as heart swings side to side in large pericardial volume 
![](00%20media/{FD851D23-C7F0-4DC9-A663-E9C3787A9CD3}.png)
## Echo 
- Identify quantity of fluid
- See if ventricular filling is compromised 
# Clinical features
- Could be asymptomatic, with left side dull ache
- Compression of adjacent structures
	- Dysphagia
	- Dyspnea
	- Hoarseness (recurrent laryngeal nerve compression)
	- Hiccups (phrenic nerve stimulation)
# Treatment 
- Treat the underlying cause
- Pericardiocentesis → remove pericardial fluid
	- Should also drain and perform analysis of the fluid
