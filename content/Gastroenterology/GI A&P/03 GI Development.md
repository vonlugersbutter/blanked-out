# Basics
![400](00%20media/{47D61F5F-16FE-4220-8F70-718681F42E21}.png)

- Inside of the GI starts out as an outpouching of the yolk sac
- Lining of endoderm forms GI lining, followed by mesoderm (muscular layer), then ectoderm
- Endoderm → GI tract
	- GI tract epithelium, glands
	- Many organs bud off: liver, pancreas, trachea
- Mesoderm → Surrounding structures
	- Stroma (GI tract connective tissue)
	- Muscles
	- Peritoneum
	- Spleen (special case)
- Foregut
	- Supplied by celiac trunk
	- Mouth to Ampulla of Vater
- Midgut 
	- Supplied by SMA
	- Ampulla of Vater to transverse colon
- Hindgut
	- Supplied by IMA
	- Transverse colon to rectum
## Mesentery
- Dorsal mesentery (towards the back)
	- Gut moves away posterior wall in development
	- Dorsal mesentery grows between gut and posterior wall
	- Covers most abdominal structures
- Ventral mesentery (towards the front)
	- Only exists bottom esophagus, stomach, upper duodenum
	- Derived from septum transversum (mesenchyme tissue)
	- Liver grows into this mesentery
	- In adult: lesser omentum (between liver and stomach) and falciform ligament (between liver and abdominal wall)
## Foregut
- Lung “buds” off from foregut → “Respiratory diverticulum”; “lung bud”
- Tracheoesophageal septum divides diverticulum
- Matures into separate trachea and esophagus
	- Abnormal septum development → pathology
	- [Esophageal Atresia](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Esophageal%20Atresia.md) (closed esophagus) = occurs when septum deviates posteriorly
## Midgut
- About 6th week of development, abdomen temporarily becomes too small
- Intestines “herniate” through umbilical cord → “physiologic herniation”
- Reduction of hernia occurs by 12th week
- Persistence of normal herniation causes [Omphalocele](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Omphalocele.md)
- Midgut rotates around SMA, continues after return to abdomen, results in normal positioning of small bowel, colon
	- Cecum in right lower quadrant
- Inappropriate rotation is [Malrotation](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Malrotation.md)
- The vitelline duct (omphalomesenteric duct) connects the midgut to the yolk sac and normally disappears by week 9 → pathology is [Meckel's Diverticulum](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Meckel's%20Diverticulum.md)
# Spleen
- The spleen arises from the dorsal mesodermal tissue of the stomach, not from the endoderm
- Its blood supply is the celiac trunk (via the splenic artery)
- Stomach rotation moves the spleen to the left side, but it retains a connection to the stomach via the gastrosplenic ligament, which carries the short gastric arteries
# Gastric acid
- Birth has very low gastric acid production, pH is 7 or 8, and drops to less than 3 in the first couple of hours
# LES
- Often underdeveloped in infants = common spit-up (reflux) because the muscle isn't fully mature
- Well-developed by around 2 weeks 
# Pancreas
- Exocrine pancreas function isn’t really good until first year
# Intestines
- Babies can absorb intact proteins → including antibodies from breast milk
## Meconium
- Meconium is the earliest stool of an infant resulting from defecation. Unlike later feces, meconium is composed of materials ingested during the time the infant spends in the uterus: intestinal epithelial cells, lanugo, mucus, amniotic fluid, bile, and water
- Unlike later feces, is viscous and sticky like tar – its color usually being a very dark olive green and it is almost odorless
# Select congenital disorders
- Hirschsprung’s disease = a motor disease associated with down syndrome where ganglion cells (Auerbach’s and Meissner’s plexus) fail to migrate it presents as a failure to pass meconium and is diagnosed via a rectal suction biopsy showing an absence of ganglion cells    
	- The problem is in the submucosa and the muscularis externa, where the plexuses are 
- Ogilvie syndrome = acute "pseudo-obstruction" with a dilated colon in the absence of a lesion, often seen in severely ill or hospitalized patients
- Necrotizing enterocolitis = a neonatal disorder, primarily in premature infants, showing pneumatosis intestinalis (air in the bowel wall) on x-ray
