# Definition
Pericardial fluid under high pressure, severely limits filling of the heart.

![](00%20media/{9F7B6F62-80CE-4D4F-BBCB-16F3E30ADD21}.png)
# Etiology
- VSV ↓, CO ↓ , hypotension, shock death 
- Any etiology of [Acute Pericarditis](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Pericardial%20Diseases/Acute%20Pericarditis.md) can progress to cardiac tamponade
	- Usually neoplastic, post-viral, and uremic pericarditis
	- Acute hemorrhage important cause
		- Blunt chest trauma
		- Rupture of LV free wall after MI
		- Complication of dissecting aortic aneurysm 
- Heart gets compressed, diastolic pressure within each chamber becomes elevated and equal to the pericardial pressure 
- Cannot accommodate venous return → systemic and pulmonary venous pressures rise
- Systemic venous pressure → right heart failure → JVP
- Pulmonary congestion
- Inadequate perfusion to vital organs, shock, death

# Investigations
## Echocardiogram
- Compression and collapse of RV during systole (high pressure pericardial fluid)
## Definitive cardiac catheterization
- Measurement of intracardiac and intrapericardial pressures, usually with pericardiocentesis 
# Clinical features
- Suspected in anyone with pericarditis, pericardial effusion, or chest trauma who has
	- JVP distension
	- Systemic hypotension
	- Small, quiet heart on examination
	- Sinus tachycardia
	- Pulsus paradoxus 
		- Decrease of systolic pressure >10 mmHg during inspiration
		- Normally, expansion of thorax → intrathoracic pressure more negative → ↑ systemic venous return → RV filling → IV septum to the left → ↓ LV filling → LV ↓ slightly during inspiration 
		- In cardiac tamponade, this is exaggerated because the ventricles share a reduced, fixed volume because of external compression by tense pericardial fluid 
		- Bulging of IV septum has a proportionally greater effect on limitation of LV filling 
		- Could also happen when inspiration is exaggerated, like in asthma or COPD 
	- Dyspnea
	- Tachypnea 
# Treatment 
- Pericardiocentesis
	- Patient is positioned head up at a 45-degree angle to promote pooling of the effusion, and a needle is inserted into the pericardial space through the skin, usually just below the xiphoid process (to avoid coronary artery)
	- In tamponade, pericardial pressure is elevated and is equal to the diastolic pressures within all of the cardiac chambers, reflecting compressive force
	- Also observe JVP 
![300](00%20media/{6F055FE2-FBA0-4A3A-B73B-6456231529C8}.png)
- High pressure pericardial fluid compresses the heart, impairs RV filling, so y descent is blunted 
- Stained and cultured fluid, check for malignancy with cytologic exam
- Inflammatory markers, cell counts, LDH levels (if it's higher, it's an exudate; lower is transudate)
	- Exudate is a protein-rich fluid that leaks from inflamed blood vessels due to local inflammation, while transudate is a watery, protein-poor fluid that seeps out of vessels due to systemic issues that alter pressure, such as heart failure or low protein levels. Exudates are caused by **increased capillary permeability**, whereas transudates result from **increased hydrostatic pressure or decreased oncotic pressure**.
