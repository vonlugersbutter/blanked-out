## Normal lipid metabolism

![550](00%20media/{683CC6DE-9EC4-44C7-927F-320EA66BD0F3}.png)

- HMG-CoA reductase is the rate-limiting element of cholesterol biosynthesis
- Receptor-mediated endocytosis of circulating LDL takes it in 
- When intracellular cholesterol ↓ → sterol regulatory element-binding protein (SREBP) released from ER, which ↑ transcription of HMG-CoA reductase and LDLR
- When intracellular cholesterol ↑ → ↑ ABCA1 and ABCG1
	- ABCA1 and ABCG1 → transmembrane proteins that effluxes to form HDL particles 
	- HDL esterified by lecithin cholesterol acyltransferase (LCAT)
	- Back to the liver by SRB1 receptor 
## Evolution of the plaque
![](00%20media/{DB197DF3-5D1A-4263-82E8-14E594C1DFA3}.png)
1. Accumulation of LDLs in the intima, oxidation and glycation
	- Endothelial dysfunction, allowing for these things to enter 
		- Branch sites are usually affected. Disturbed flow across branch points causes low shear stress, so atheroprotective functions like KLF2 do not occur
	- Could be high LDLs, could be mutations of LDLR, ApoB, PCSK9 (protease that regulates LDLR)
2. Oxidative stress, local cytokine elaboration
3. Attract leukocytes (MCP-1, monocyte chemoattractant protein 1)
	- V-CAM1, I-CAM1, selectins all help attract monocytes
	- T cells are also around here 
4. Scavenger receptors ↑ due to macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF)
5. Scavenger receptors → update of LDLs, foam cells, produce superoxide and MMPs
6. SMCs migrate from intima to media
	- Foam cells produce ↑ PDGF, IL1, TNF, TGFB → SMC proliferation and ↑ ECM
7. SMCs divide, more ECM. Fatty streak → fibrofatty lesion
8. Calcification, fibrosis, SMC death → acellular fibrosis capsule surrounding lipid-rich (necrotic) core with dead or dying cells
	- Inflammation in plaque microenvironment (things like CD40L)

## Summary of how plaques form
Lipoproteins are produced by the liver. These are used to transport cholesterol around the blood. LDLs transport it from the liver to the body cells, while HDLs remove extra cholesterols from the body. 

When excess LDLs start to accumulate in the inner layer of an artery wall, lipids and proteins undergo oxidation and glycation. Macrophages are released, leading to formation of foam cells. T cells follow, amplify the inflammatory response. Those foam cells and T cells form a fatty streak. 

Smooth muscle migrates from the middle artery layer to cover the plaque with a cap.

## Complications of atherosclerosis
- Calcification
- Rupture
- Hemorrhage
- Embolization 
- White (anemic) infarcts are caused by arterial occlusion in solid organs like the heart, kidney, and spleen, resulting in a pale area due to limited blood flow. Red (hemorrhagic) infarcts occur in loose tissues, organs with dual blood supply (like the lungs), or those with venous occlusion, causing blood to flood the area and creating a dark, reddish appearance

# Prognosis
## Biomarkers for atherosclerosis
- Homocysteine = oxidation, inflammation → elevated levels of the amino acid homocysteine can promote the development of atherosclerosis by damaging the lining of arteries, contributing to inflammation, and increasing the risk of blood clots 
- Lipoprotein A = LDL variant. Competes w normal plasminogen activity because it looks similar. ↓ fibrinolysis of clots
- CRP = marker of inflammation
- Genetics = chromosome 9 has some stuff with TGFB inhibition, other factors
- Infection w herpesvirus or chlamydia
