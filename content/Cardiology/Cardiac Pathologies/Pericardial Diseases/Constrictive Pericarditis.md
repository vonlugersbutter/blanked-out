# Definition
Not super common, but important to understand because it looks like other disorders. Chronic condition where the heart's outer lining, the pericardium, becomes stiff and scarred, preventing the heart from filling properly and leading to heart failure symptoms like leg swelling and shortness of breath.
# Etiology
- TB used to be a major cause
- Now it's idiopathic, or viral acute
- Could be any etiology of [Acute Pericarditis](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Pericardial%20Diseases/Acute%20Pericarditis.md)
- Any pericardial effusion will undergo rapid resorption
- Fluid undergoes organization, fusion of the pericardial layers, fibrous scar formation
- Calcification of adherent layers ensues → very stiff 
- Issues occur during diastole, inhibits normal filling of cardiac chambers 
- Systemic venous pressure rises, right sided heart failure 
- Lower SV, CO, blood pressure

# Investigations
## Physical exam
- Early diastolic knock with severe calcific constriction → sudden ventricular diastolic filling stop
- Not usually pulsus paradoxus 
	- In constrictive pericarditis, the negative intrathoracic pressure not as easily transmitted through rigid pericardial shell to right side
- Instead, when they inhale, blood drawn to the thorax, but it can't go anywhere, so we see distention of the JVP → Kussmaul sign, opposite to normal 
	- Kussmaul's sign is absent in cardiac tamponade because the entire heart is compressed equally by the fluid-filled pericardium, unlike in constrictive pericarditis where the right ventricle is more constrained. In tamponade, the inspiratory decrease in intrathoracic pressure is transmitted to all cardiac chambers, so the right ventricle can still accommodate increased venous return
## CXR
- Enlarged cardiac silhouette, maybe some calcification
## EKG
- Nonspecific ST and T wave abnormalities
- Arrhythmias
## Echo
- Pericardium will be thickened
- Small ventricles, contract vigorously 
- Ventricular filling terminates abruptly in early diastole 
- Aberrant diastolic motion of IV septum 
## CT or MRI
- Really easy to see thickened pericarditis
## Cardiac catheterization
- Elevation and dip of diastolic pressures in cardiac chambers
- Early diastolic dip and plateau in RV and LV tracings 
- Normal on left, constrictive pericarditis on right
- Plateau is quick halt imposed by stiff pericardium

# Clinical features
- Develop over months to years! From elevated systemic venous pressure and reduced CO 
- Development of hepatomegaly and ascites 
# Treatment 
- Surgical removal of pericardium
