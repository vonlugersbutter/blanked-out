# Definition
Excessive PTH.
# Etiology
- Primary hyperparathyroidism = usually caused by a parathyroid adenoma, this involves inappropriate PTH secretion not triggered by low Ca++
	- Traditionally described as "stones, bones, groans, and psychiatric overtones," including kidney stones, bone pain, constipation, and anxiety
	- Osteitis fibrosa cystica = classic bone disease associated with this condition, characterized by subperiosteal bone resorption and "brown tumors"
		- Brown tumors = rare, non-neoplastic, benign bone lesions caused by severe, long-standing primary or secondary hyperparathyroidism
- Secondary hyperparathyroidism = occurs in renal failure as a response to chronically low calcium
- Tertiary hyperparathyroidism = occurs when the parathyroid becomes autonomous after chronic renal failure, leading to very high PTH and elevated calcium

# Clinical features
- PTH ↑ → Ca++ serum ↑ → but then there's less going in the bone, so the risk of osteoporosis is high 
	- See [Metabolic Bone Disease](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Metabolic%20Bone%20Disease.md)
- Hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria (kidney stones, [Nephrolithiasis](11%20Nephrology/Renal%20Pathologies/Nephrolithiasis.md)), and low phosphate
# Investigations
- DEXA scan = compares bone density to a peak young adult mean (t-score)
- Alkaline phosphatase = marker of osteoblastic activity/bone formation → also used in [09 Gallbladder, Bile, Bilirubin](10%20Gastroenterology/GI%20A&P/09%20Gallbladder,%20Bile,%20Bilirubin.md) blockage testing
- N-telopeptide (NTX) = marker of collagen breakdown/bone resorption

# Treatment 
- Bisphosphonates (inhibit osteoclasts), SERMs → selective estrogen receptor modulators (eg, raloxifene), intermittent PTH administration, and calcitonin
