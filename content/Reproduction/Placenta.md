- Placental disc, extraplacental membranes, and a three-vessel umbilical cord
- Surfaces
	- Basal Plate = The maternal surface lying against the uterine wall, divided into segments called cotyledons
	- Chorionic Plate = The fetal surface where the umbilical cord typically inserts in the center Large fetal vessels branch across this plate, with arteries usually crossing over veins
- Sonographic Appearance = A normal placenta is homogenous, 2 to 4 cm thick, and separated from the myometrium by a hypoechoic retroplacental space measuring less than 1 to 2 cm

	- Decidua basalis
		- Uterus at site of implantation
		- Interacts with trophoblast
	- Decidua capsularis = surrounds fetus

- Trophoblast = outer layer of blastocyst, develops into placenta, proliferates into two cell layers
	- Syncytiotrophoblast: outer layer
		- Invades endometrium
		- Finger-like projections: villi
		- Form lacunae (spaces) for maternal blood
	- Cytotrophoblast: inner layer
		- Proliferates → cells migrate into syncytiotrophoblast
		- Secretes proteolytic enzymes to aid invasion
	- Chorionic villi: projections of both layers, GI and gas exchange for fetus
		- Contact with maternal blood
		- Nutrient/gas exchange

# Placental Circulation
- Maternal side = endometrial (spiral) art → villous space → endometrial vein
- Fetal side = umbilical arteries (deoxygenated blood), umbilical arteries → chorionic arteries → capillaries → umbilical vein (oxygenated blood)
- No mixing of maternal/fetal blood
- Oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse, facilitated transport of glucose, active transport of amino acids
- IgG antibodies cross (not IgM)
- Some other nutrients, drugs, infectious agents
- Human placenta is hemochorial, meaning maternal blood directly bathes the fetal syncytiotrophoblast. This arrangement allows for the exchange of gases and nutrients without the mixing of maternal and fetal blood.
- Villi Formation: Around 12 days post-conception, intercommunicating channels called trophoblastic lacunae form within the syncytiotrophoblast and fill with maternal blood. Solid columns of cytotrophoblasts protrude into these channels to form primary villi.
- Arborization: As the placenta matures, villi undergo increasing arborization (branching), and fetal capillaries move closer to the villous surface to improve exchange efficiency.
- Functional Units: The functional unit of the placenta is the lobule (or cotyledon), which is the structure supplied by each main stem (truncal) villus
- Growth: In the first trimester, placental growth is more rapid than fetal growth. By 17 weeks, their weights are equal, and by term, the placenta is roughly one-sixth the weight of the fetus.
# Umbilical Cord
- Connection between embryo and placenta, derives from fetus, from the yolk sac and allantois
- Contains umbilical arteries and veins
- Yolk sac = cavity (sac) formed in early embryogenesis
- Allantois = outpouching of hindgut, walls form umbilical blood vessels
	- Lumen occludes in development
	- Becomes urachus = fibrous remnant of allantois, connects bladder to umbilicus
		- In adult: median umbilical ligament, may cause adenocarcinoma of bladder
		- Urachus anomalies
			- Patent urachus = urine discharge from umbilicus
			- Vesicourachal diverticulum = diverticulum of bladder
			- Urachal cyst = partial obliteration, fluid-filled cavity, may become infected
- Umbilical cord
	- Two umbilical arteries = deoxygenated fetal blood to placenta
	- One umbilical vein = oxygenated fetal blood from placenta
	- Single umbilical artery = abnormal variant, often identified on prenatal ultrasound, associated with fetal anomalies
	- Wharton jelly = contains mucopolysaccharides, similar to vitreous humor
	- Allantoic duct
# Immunology of pregnancy
- Fetus: foreign antigens
- Protected from maternal immunity by placenta
- Several mechanisms
	- Trophoblast cells do not express many MHC class I antigens
	- Placenta secretions block immune response
# Complications
## Size and shape variation
- Multilobate Variants
	- Bilobate (Bipartite/Duplex) = Two nearly equally sized discs with the cord inserting between them
	- Succenturiate Lobe = One or more smaller accessory lobes develop in the membranes away from the main placenta These are clinically significant because their connecting vessels can lead to vasa previa or can be retained after delivery, causing hemorrhage or endometritis
- Surface Area Variants
	- Placenta Membranacea = Villi cover the entire uterine cavity, potentially causing serious hemorrhage due to associated previa or accreta
	- Placenta Fenestrata = A "hole" in the placenta where the central villous tissue is missing, though the chorionic plate usually remains intact
- Placentomegaly = Defined as a placenta thicker than 40 mm
	- Homogeneous thickening is often linked to maternal diabetes, severe anemia, or fetal infections (syphilis, CMV, toxoplasmosis)
	- Inhomogeneous thickening may be seen with partial molar pregnancies (containing anechoic cysts) or mesenchymal dysplasia (enlarged stem villi)
## Extrachorial placentation 
- When the chorionic plate fails to extend to the periphery of the placental disc
- Circummarginate = Fibrin and old hemorrhage lie between the disc and the amniochorion
- Circumvallate = The periphery of the chorionic plate is a thickened, circular ridge made of a double fold of chorion and amnion While it can be associated with abruption and preterm birth in postpartum studies, sonographic evidence suggests many cases are transient and benign
## Vascular issues
- Placental reserve allows for the loss of up to 30 percent of villi without adverse effects, but extensive lesions can limit fetal growth
### Maternal Blood Flow Disruption
- Fibrin Deposition = Subchorionic fibrin appears as white/yellow plaques beneath the chorionic plate Perivillous fibrin deposition reflects normal aging, but if it affects >25% of villi, it is linked to growth restriction
- Maternal Floor Infarction = An extreme variant where a dense fibrinoid layer in the basal plate impedes blood flow It is associated with miscarriage, stillbirth, and recurrence in future pregnancies
- Intervillous Thrombus = A common collection of coagulated maternal and fetal blood, often benign but sometimes a cause of elevated maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein (MSAFP)
- Infarction = Caused by obstructed maternal blood supply to the intervillous space Numerous central infarctions may be associated with preeclampsia
### Hematomas
- Retroplacental = Formed between the placenta and decidua (essentially a placental abruption)
- Marginal (Subchorionic Hemorrhage) = Formed between the chorion and decidua at the periphery
- Subamnionic = Of fetal origin, located between the amnion and chorionic plate
- Higher risk for [First-Trimester Abortion](Reproduction/Reproductive%20Pathologies/First-Trimester%20Abortion.md) = large size, persistent bleeding, retroplacental location 
## Calcification and tumors
- Calcification = Deposits of calcium salts (most common on the basal plate) increase with gestation, smoking, and high maternal calcium levels
	- Sonographic grading (0–3) reflects this progression, but grade 3 calcification prior to 32 weeks is the only significant predictor of adverse outcomes like stillbirth
- Chorioangioma = The most common benign placental tumor (approx 1% incidence)
	- Small tumors are usually asymptomatic
	- Large tumors (>4 cm) can cause arteriovenous shunting, leading to high-output heart failure, hydrops, and fetal death
	- Management = May involve endoscopic laser ablation of feeder vessels at specialized centers
- Metastatic Tumors = Maternal melanomas, leukemias, and lymphomas rarely metastasize to the placenta, and even more rarely to the fetus
## Membrane issues
- Chorioamnionitis = Bacterial infection of the membranes, often ascending after prolonged rupture It is a common cause of unexplained preterm labor
- Amnionic Band Sequence = Amnion bands can tether or amputate fetal parts, leading to limb defects or facial clefts Targeted sonography is required if limb defects or encephalocele are noted
- Amnionic Sheets = Formed when membranes drape over preexisting uterine synechiae; generally low risk
## Umbilical cord variation
- Length = Normal range is 40 to 70 cm Short cords are linked to malformations and intrapartum distress; excessively long cords risk entanglement or prolapse
- Coiling = spiraling usually occurs in a left-twisting direction Extremes of the Umbilical Coiling Index (UCI)—hypocoiled or hypercoiled—are controversially linked to growth restriction and heart rate abnormalities
- Vessel Number = The standard is one vein and two arteries A single umbilical artery (SUA) occurs in roughly 06% of births and requires targeted sonography to rule out cardiovascular or genitourinary malformations Isolated SUA carries a slightly higher risk for growth restriction
## Cord insertion and vasa previa
- Marginal Insertion (Battledore) = Cord anchors at the placental margin; usually benign
- Velamentous Insertion = Umbilical vessels travel within the membranes before reaching the placenta These vessels are vulnerable to compression and rupture
- Vasa Previa = Fetal vessels overlie the cervical os
	- Type 1 = Associated with velamentous insertion
	- Type 2 = Vessels span between lobes of a bilobate or succenturiate placenta
	- Risk = Rapid fetal exsanguination if vessels tear during membrane rupture
	- Management = Ideally diagnosed antenatally via color Doppler Standard practice includes cesarean delivery at 34 to 36 weeks
## Mechanical issues
- Knots = True knots (1% of births) increase stillbirth risk four- to ten-fold False knots are merely vessel redundancies with no clinical significance
- Nuchal Cords = Loops around the neck are found in 20–34% of deliveries and are generally not associated with adverse outcomes, though they may cause variable heart rate decelerations
- Aneurysms and Varices = Rare umbilical artery aneurysms are associated with trisomy 18 and stillbirth Umbilical vein varices require surveillance for thrombosis or rupture
