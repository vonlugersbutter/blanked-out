# Definition

| Condition              | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ischemic heart disease | Condition in which imbalance between myocardial oxygen supply and demand results in myocardial hypoxia and accumulation of waste metabolites, most often caused by [atherosclerotic](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Ischemic%20Heart%20Diseases/Atherosclerosis.md) disease of the coronary arteries                  |
| Angina pectoris        | Uncomfortable sensation in the chest and neighboring anatomic structures produced by myocardial ischemia                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Stable angina          | Chronic pattern of transient angina pectoris, precipitated by physical activity or emotional upset, relieved by rest within a few minutes; episodes often associated with temporary depression of the ST segment, but permanent myocardial damage does not result                           |
| Variant angina         | Typical anginal discomfort, usually at rest, which develops because of coronary<br>artery spasm rather than an increase of myocardial oxygen demand; episodes often associated with transient shifts of the ST segment, usually ST elevation (also termed Prinzmetal angina)                |
| Silent ischemia        | Asymptomatic episodes of myocardial ischemia; can be detected by electrocardiogram and other laboratory techniques                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Unstable angina        | Pattern of increased frequency and duration of angina episodes produced by less exertion or at rest; high frequency of progression to myocardial infarction if untreated                                                                                                                    |
| Myocardial infarction  | Region of myocardial necrosis usually caused by prolonged cessation of blood supply; most often results from acute thrombus at the site of coronary atherosclerotic stenosis; may be a first clinical manifestation of ischemic heart disease, or there may be a history of angina pectoris |

# Etiology
## Normal myocardial oxygen supply

![400](00%20media/{33017517-88CF-4A0D-A73C-605C40D250B9}.png)
### Myocardial oxygen supply
- Coronary perfusion pressure = diastolic pressure 
- External compression = especially compresses the subendocardium
- Intrinsic regulation
	- Local metabolites
		- Hypoxemia → ↓ oxidative metabolism → ↑ ADP, AMP, adenosine → adenosine is a vasodilator (binds to SMC receptors to ↓ Ca++ entry into cells)
		- Lactate, acetate, H+, CO2
	- Endothelial factors
	- Neural innervation
		- Not much PNS control
		- SNS control: $\alpha$ -adrenergic = vasoconstrict; $\beta$ -adrenergic = vasodilate 
![400](00%20media/{E0CEBFAC-CE6F-4B98-AB18-7A8EB76BABD9}.png)

### Myocardial oxygen demand
- L ventricular wall stress
	- Laplace's law $\sigma = \frac{P \times r}{2h}$ where $\sigma$ is the wall stress, P is pressure, r is radius, and h is thickness 
- Heart rate
- Contractility 

Again, Fick's principle!! 
$$
MVO_2=CBF \times (CaO_2-CVO_2)
$$

## Pathophysiology of ischemia
- Fixed vessel narrowing (so a plaque is there, recall Poiseuille's Law)
- Endothelial cell dysfunction (mostly mediated by NO and prostacyclin)
	- Inappropriate vasoconstriction
	- Loss of antithrombosis
### Other causes of ischemia
- Perfusion pressure ↓ because hypotension in shock
- Blood O2 content ↓ 
- Myocardial O2 demand ↑ because of rapid tachycardia, acute hypertension, severe aortic stenosis 
## Consequences of ischemia
- ATP ↓ → ↓ proper contraction → ↑ LV diastolic P → pulmonary caps → congestion → SOB
- Lactate, serotonin, adenosine ↑ → pain receptors C7-T4 → angina
- Transient ion abnormalities → arrythmias 
- Stunned myocardium = a **acute** reversible condition where the heart muscle is temporarily unable to contract properly after an episode of severe ischemia (lack of blood flow) and subsequent reperfusion (blood flow is restored)
	- As opposed to hibernating myocardium = **chronically** depressed function due to persistent, severe coronary artery stenosis
	- Neither of these are truly necrotic, infarcted tissue 
## Ischemic syndromes
![400](00%20media/{3A2987AB-09A0-463B-B854-2E2424D5F37C}.png)

- The inappropriate vasoconstriction is probably happening due to endothelial dysfunction
- Variable-threshold angina = type of angina where the amount of physical exertion that triggers chest pain varies inconsistently from one time to the next
	- Fixed-threshold angina is the opposite
- Unstable angina is when we start to get into [Acute Coronary Syndrome](Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Ischemic%20Heart%20Diseases/Acute%20Coronary%20Syndrome.md)
- Variant or Prinzmetal angina = caused by vasospasm, a narrowing of the coronary arteries due to contraction of the heart's smooth muscle tissue in the vessel walls 
- Silent ischemia is asymptomatic → often in diabetics (impaired pain sensation), elderly, and women
- Syndrome X = a heart condition causing chest pain (angina) and signs of myocardial ischemia despite normal coronary arteries on diagnostic imaging

# Clinical features
- Chest pain that is pressure, discomfort, tightness, burning, heaviness
- Levine sign = a patient holding a clenched fist over the center of their chest to describe chest pain
- Could be diffuse, is usually retrosternal 
- Tachycardia, dyspnea, nausea, fatigue, weakness

# Treatment
- Lifestyle changes are huge 
![](00%20media/{39707556-2366-4FA7-927A-76C71A0C1CD0}.png)

- Venodilation → ↓ LV volume (preload) → ↓ myocardial O2 demand
- Ranolazine → ↓ late phase Na+ → bad tissues thought to have too much Ca++ overload, so this reduces that 
- Beta-blockers can have some mortality benefit for patients w past MI
- None of these show ↑ longevity in patients w chronic stable angina w preserved LV function 
## Preventing acute cardiac events
- Reduce platelet aggregation and thrombosis
	- Aspirin, antithrombosis
	- Platelet P2Y12 ADP receptor antagonists (eg, clopidogrel, prasugrel, and ticagrelor)
	- Low-dose anticoagulant (rivaroxaban)
- Lipid lowering therapy w statins 
- Ezetimibe is a selective inhibitor of cholesterol uptake at brush border of small intestines
- Glucose-lowering therapies like GLP-1 agonists in diabetes
- Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors reducing the heart's workload, improving blood flow, and preventing long-term damage to the heart muscle
	- Work by blocking the production of angiotensin II, a hormone that constricts blood vessels and increases blood pressure
## Revascularization
- Percutaneous transluminal coronary angioplasty (PTCA)
	- Under fluoroscopy in which a balloon-tipped catheter is inserted through a peripheral artery (usually a radial, femoral, or brachial artery) and maneuvered into the stenotic segment of a coronary vessel. The balloon at the end of the catheter is then inflated under high pressure to dilate the stenosis, after which the balloon is deflated and the catheter is removed from the body
- Coronary stents 
- Drug-eluting stents
	- Sirolimus (an immunosuppressive agent that inhibits T-cell activation), everolimus (an immunosuppressive similar to sirolimus), or paclitaxel (which interferes with cellular microtubule function)
- CABG (coronary artery bypass graft) surgery entails grafting portions of a patient’s native blood vessels to bypass obstructed coronary arteries
