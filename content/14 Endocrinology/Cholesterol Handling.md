# Lipoproteins
- Lipoproteins are spherical assemblies of lipids and proteins designed to transport hydrophobic molecules (cholesterol, triglycerides) through aqueous fluids like blood and lymph
- Surface monolayer = composed of amphipathic molecules including phospholipids, free cholesterol, and apolipoproteins
- Hydrophobic core = contains neutral lipids, specifically triglycerides and cholesteryl esters
- Types of lipids 
	- Simple lipids = FFAs and cholesterol
	- Complex lipids = triglycerides and phospholipids
	- Fatty acids = saturated, monounsaturated, or polyunsaturated
	- Essential fatty acids = omega-3 and omega-6 species that cannot be synthesized
## Classes
- Chylomicrons = largest and least dense; they originate in the intestine and carry primarily dietary triglycerides
- Very low-density lipoproteins (VLDL) = produced by the liver to transport endogenous triglycerides
- Intermediate-density lipoproteins (IDL) = remnants formed during the conversion of VLDL to LDL
- Low-density lipoproteins (LDL) = carriers of plasma cholesterol; they are highly atherogenic
- High-density lipoproteins (HDL) = involved in reverse cholesterol transport, retrieving cholesterol from peripheral tissues for delivery to the liver
- Lipoprotein(a) = an LDL-like particle covalently bound to apoprotein(a); it is an independent risk factor for coronary disease and has structural homology with plasminogen
# Apolipoproteins
- Apo b-100 = found in VLDL, IDL, and LDL; it is the ligand for LDLR
- Apo b-48 = truncated version of b-100 found only in chylomicrons and their remnants
- Apo a-i = the major structural protein of HDL; it activates lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase
- Apo c-ii = required cofactor for lipoprotein lipase, the enzyme responsible for TG hydrolysis
- Apo c-iii = inhibitor of LPL and VLDL clearance
- Apo e = exists in three isoforms (e-2, e-3, e-4); it mediates the hepatic uptake of chylomicron and VLDL remnants apo e-4 is associated with an increased risk of early-onset Alzheimer's disease
# Lipoprotein pathways
- Exogenous pathway = dietary fat is absorbed, assembled into chylomicrons, and enters the blood LPL at the capillary endothelium hydrolyzes the TG into FFAs for tissue use, leaving chylomicron remnants that are cleared by the liver via Apo E
- Endogenous pathway = liver secretes VLDL, which is similarly hydrolyzed by LPL to form IDL and then LDL
	- Half of VLDL remnants are cleared by the liver; the rest become LDL
- LDLR pathway = cells take up LDL via receptor-mediated endocytosis
	- Intracellular cholesterol levels regulate the production of these receptors through SREBP transcription factors
	- PCSK9 is a protein that promotes the lysosomal degradation of the LDL receptor → ↑ LDL levels
- Reverse cholesterol transport = HDL acquires free cholesterol from tissues, which is esterified by LCAT
	- Mature HDL then delivers this cholesterol to the liver or transfers CE to TG-rich lipoproteins via CETP
# Clinical disorders
## Primary hyperlipidemias
- [Familial Hypercholesterolemia](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Familial%20Hypercholesterolemia.md)
- Familial combined hyperlipidemia = variable elevations of LDL, VLDL, or both; it is a major contributor to premature [Atherosclerosis](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Ischemic%20Heart%20Diseases/Atherosclerosis.md)
- Familial dysbetalipoproteinemia = Apo E-2 homozygosity, resulting in the accumulation of VLDL and chylomicron remnants
- Familial chylomicronemia syndrome = severe hypertriglyceridemia caused by a complete deficiency of LPL activity; patients are at high risk for [Acute Pancreatitis](10%20Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Acute%20Pancreatitis.md)
## Primary hypolipidemias
- Tangier disease = near absence of HDL due to mutations in the ABCA1 transporter, leading to orange tonsils and hepatosplenomegaly
- Chylomicron retention disease = rare neonatal disorder causing fat malabsorption and severe deficiency of fat-soluble vitamins (especially Vitamin E)
## Secondary Causes
- Disordered lipid metabolism often stems from other clinical conditions
	- [Diabetes](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Diabetes/Diabetes.md) and [Obesity](10%20Gastroenterology/GI%20Pathologies/Obesity.md) = hypertriglyceridemia (increased VLDL) and low HDL
	- [Hypothyroidism](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Hypothyroidism.md) = elevated LDL and IDL by decreasing LDL receptor activity
	- Nephrosis = biphasic disorder where LDL increases first, followed by severe lipemia as albumin levels drop
	- Drugs = estrogens, corticosteroids, alcohol, and certain antihypertensives can aggravate dyslipidemia
