- Four glands formed by the 3rd and 4th pharyngeal pouches, located behind the thyroid gland
- Primary function is to secrete parathyroid hormone (PTH), which is essential for maintaining calcium and phosphate homeostasis
- PTH = protein hormone synthesized by the chief cells of the parathyroid gland and acts by binding to cell surface receptors in the bone and kidneys
# Calcium handling
- Exists in three forms = ionized (50%), protein-bound (40%, mostly to albumin), and complexed (10%, with citrate or phosphate)
- Acidosis decreases protein binding, increasing ionized calcium
- Alkalosis (eg, from hyperventilation) increases binding, decreasing ionized calcium and leading to neuromuscular hyperexcitability (tetany)
- See [Thyroid](Endocrinology/Thyroid.md) for calcitonin discussion 
# PTH secretion
- PTH secreted by chief cells, synthesized as a prepropeptide, cleaved
- Cleaved by the liver into amino-terminal fragments (biologically active, short half-life) and carboxy-terminal fragments (inactive, longer half-life)
- Intact PTH measurement is the only reliable index of status
- Response to ↓ Ca++ in blood, ↑ plasma phosphate, ↓ active vitamin D
- CaSRs on glands inhibit PTH release when Ca++ ↑ 
- Also activated by Mg++
	- Mg++ ↓ → ↑ PTH release mildly
	- If Mg++ very low → ↓ PTH release because some of the Mg is required for normal CaSR function
# PTH effects
- Effects of parathyroid hormone the net effects of PTH include ↑ plasma calcium, ↓ plasma phosphate, ↑ urinary phosphate
	- Through both direct action and the indirect activation of vitamin D
- Kidneys
	- In DCT, PTH ↑ Ca++ resorption
	- In PCT, ↓ PO4 resorption
	- Stimulates production of active vitamin D
- GI tract
	- Ca++ and PO4 absorption ↑ through activated vitamin D
- Bone
	- Stimulates both bone resorption and formation
	- Continuous = resorption, ↑ serum Ca++
	- Low dose, once daily (like through teriparatide) = increase bone mass
# Vitamin D (calcitriol)
- Synthesis and activation
	- Produced in skin via UV light (D₃) or obtained through diet (D₂ or D₃)
	- Liver = hydroxylation at c-25 to form 25-hydroxyvitamin d (major circulating and storage form)
	- Kidney = final activation by 1α-hydroxylase (stimulated by pth) to form 1,25(oh)₂d (calcitriol)
- Effects
	- Intestine = significantly increases absorption of dietary ca²⁺ and phosphate
	- Kidney = facilitates renal reabsorption of filtered ca²⁺
	- Bone = increases bone turnover and stimulates bone resorption by increasing osteoclast numbers
	- Feedback = high calcitriol inhibits its own synthesis by suppressing 1α-hydroxylase and directly reduces PTH synthesis
# Bone remodeling
- Osteoblasts = mediate bone formation; express PTH receptors
- Osteoclasts = mediate bone resorption by acidifying a microenvironment (pH ~4) to dissolve hydroxyapatite
- Osteocytes = terminally differentiated osteoblasts embedded in bone that produce FGF-23
	- Produced by bone in response to high phosphate, promotes renal phosphate excretion and inhibits 1α-hydroxylase to reduce active vitamin D
	- Excessive FGF23 can lead to chronic phosphate wasting, resulting in rickets or osteomalacia
## RANK/RANKL/OPG system
- RANKL (on osteoblasts) binds to RANK (on osteoclast precursors) to stimulate resorption
- Osteoprotegerin (OPG) = a decoy receptor secreted by osteoblasts that inhibits RANK → its production is increased by estrogen and decreased by PTH and glucocorticoids
- Estrogen = ↓ bone loss by inhibiting resorption and ↑ OPG
- Growth hormone/igf-1 = stimulate bone  growth
- Glucocorticoids = increase bone resorption and decrease synthesis, raising fracture risk

## Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia (FHH)
- Rare autosomal dominant disorder involving abnormal CaSRs, resulting in a higher-than-normal set point for calcium, leading to high serum calcium but low urinary calcium
- Pseudohypoparathyroidism = a group of disorders where kidneys and bones are unresponsive to PTH, often due to impaired g-protein signaling
	- Albright's hereditary osteodystrophy (AHO) is a form of this, presenting with short stature and shortened 4th and 5th metacarpals
## Ca++ ↑ but PTH ↓ in malignancy
- Paraneoplastic production of PTHrP (e.g., in squamous cell carcinomas of the lung, head, and neck; renal, bladder, breast, and ovarian cancer; lymphoma and leukemia)
