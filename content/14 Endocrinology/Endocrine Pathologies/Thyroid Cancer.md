- Follicular carcinoma = characterized by the invasion of the fibrous capsule unlike adenomas, it has the potential for hematogenous metastasis
- Medullary carcinoma = arises from c-cells and produces calcitonin it is associated with amyloid deposits and genetic men syndromes (ret oncogene mutations)
- Anaplastic carcinoma = a highly malignant, undifferentiated cancer seen in the elderly with a very poor prognosis
	- Undifferentiated cells lose their ability to produce thyroglobulin → the cells will be negative for this on staining 
	- Not to be confused with Riedel's which had the rock hard thyroid in a young patient 
- See [Multiple Endocrine Neoplasias](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Multiple%20Endocrine%20Neoplasias.md)

# Investigations
- Most thyroid cancers present as a nodule without systemic symptoms and are diagnosed via fine-needle aspiration (FNA)
	- Follicular adenomas are completely surrounded, cannot distinguish between adenomas and cancer, because you can't tell from the FNA if the capsule is surrounding the cancer (meaning it is benign)
	- Just follow over time, if new findings = surgery 
- Ultrasonography (US) = noninvasive technique that has become an integral part of thyroid clinical evaluation
- TIRADS (Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System) = scoring system used to estimate malignancy risk based on five US features, improving interobserver agreement
- Elastography = assesses tissue stiffness in solid nodules; higher stiffness often indicates a higher risk of malignancy
- Nuclear medicine imaging (scintigraphy) = uses isotopes (technetium-99m, I123, I131) to identify functional (hot) or nonfunctional (cold) tissue
- CT and MRI = define the anatomic extent of large or intrathoracic goiters and to stage aggressive cancers like Anaplastic Thyroid Carcinoma (ATC)
- PET CT = visualize regional glucose metabolism, which is enhanced in poorly differentiated cancer cells (the Warburg effect)
	- Really good for detecting medullary thyroid carcinoma, I wonder if this would be useful in [Multiple Endocrine Neoplasias](14%20Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Multiple%20Endocrine%20Neoplasias.md)

# Treatment 
- Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology (TBSRTC) is a standardized, six-tiered framework used to classify thyroid fine-needle aspiration (FNA) results to estimate cancer risk and guide management
	- Category I: Non-diagnostic or Unsatisfactory (1–4% risk): Sample lacks enough cells
	- Category II: Benign (0–3% risk): Includes follicular lesions
	- Category III: Atypia of Undetermined Significance (AUS) or Follicular Lesion of Undetermined Significance (FLUS) (5–15% risk)
	- Category IV: Follicular Neoplasm or Suspicious for a Follicular Neoplasm (15–30% risk) 
	- Category V: Suspicious for Malignancy (60–75% risk)
	- Category VI: Malignant (97–99% risk)
