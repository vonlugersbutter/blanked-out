- Vital endocrine organ composed of two lobes connected by a thin band of tissue called the isthmus in some individuals, a pyramidal lobe may also be present above the isthmus
- Blood supply is derived from the superior thyroid artery (the first branch of the external carotid) and the inferior thyroid artery (originating from the thyrocervical trunk off the subclavian artery)
- Embryology and development = the thyroid develops from the floor of the pharynx and descends into the neck during gestation
	- Maintains a connection to the tongue via the thyroglossal duct
	- Can leave remnants like the foramen cecum at the base of the tongue or a thyroglossal duct cyst, typically presenting as a painless midline neck mass in children that moves with swallowing if the thyroid fails to descend properly
	- Ectopic thyroid tissue may remain at the base of the tongue, which can become the individual's only source of thyroid hormone
- Histology and hormone synthesis = the gland is made of follicles filled with colloid and lined by follicular cells these cells synthesize thyroid hormones (T3 and T4) using tyrosine (from thyroglobulin) and iodine the synthesis process involves
	- Uptake = iodide is transported into cells by the Na-iodine symporter (NIS)
	- Oxidation and organification = the enzyme thyroid peroxidase (tpo) oxidizes iodide to iodine and adds it to tyrosine to form monoiodotyrosine (mit) and diiodotyrosine (dit)
	- Coupling = tpo then couples these molecules to create t3 (mit+dit) or t4 (dit+dit)
- T4 is produced in much larger quantities (>90%), T3 is the more potent hormone
- Most t3 is created in peripheral tissues when the enzyme 5’-deiodinase converts t4 (the prohormone) into t3
# Physiologic effects
- Metabolic effects = they increase the basal metabolic rate by upregulating na/k atpase pumps, which consumes more atp and oxygen, thereby increasing body temperature and respiratory rate they also stimulate carbohydrate metabolism (glycogenolysis/gluconeogenesis) and fat metabolism (lipolysis), which leads to lower cholesterol levels
- Cardiac effects = hormones increase cardiac output, heart rate, and contractility by increasing the number of beta-1 receptors in the heart
    
- cns and bone development = they are essential for normal bone growth and brain maturation; a deficiency in childhood results in cretinism, characterized by stunted growth and mental retardation
    

regulation = the system is controlled by the hypothalamus-pituitary-thyroid axis the hypothalamus releases thyrotropin-releasing hormone (trh), stimulating the anterior pituitary to release thyroid-stimulating hormone (tsh), which then binds to follicular cells to trigger the release of t3 and t4

iii thyroid disorders

disorders are broadly categorized by whether the gland is overactive (hyperthyroidism) or underactive (hypothyroidism)

hypothyroidism =

- symptoms = metabolism slows down, leading to lethargy, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, and bradycardia a classic feature is hyperlipidemia because t3 normally upregulates ldl receptors
    
- physical findings = myxedema (non-pitting edema caused by hyaluronic acid deposits) and hypothyroid myopathy (muscle weakness/cramps with elevated creatine kinase) are common
    
- hashimoto’s thyroiditis = this is the most common non-dietary cause of hypothyroidism, characterized by an autoimmune lymphocytic infiltration and the presence of anti-tpo antibodies
    

hyperthyroidism =

- symptoms = metabolism speeds up, causing hyperactivity, heat intolerance, weight loss despite increased appetite, and tachycardia
    
- grave’s disease = the leading cause of hyperthyroidism, where antibodies stimulate the tsh receptor unique features include exophthalmos (bulging eyes) and pretibial myxedema
    
- thyroid storm = a life-threatening massive catecholamine surge, often triggered by surgery or infection in patients with existing hyperthyroidism
    

laboratory interpretation =

- primary disease = the tsh level is usually opposite to the thyroid hormone level (eg, high tsh and low t4 in primary hypothyroidism)
    
- amiodarone = this drug is complex; it can cause hypothyroidism via the wolff-chaikoff effect (excess iodine inhibits synthesis) or hyperthyroidism either through iodine load (type i) or direct toxic thyroiditis (type ii)
