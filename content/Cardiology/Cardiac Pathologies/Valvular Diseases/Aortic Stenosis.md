# Definition
Narrowing of the aortic semilunar valve. 

![](00%20media/{BA7A0F37-D828-4724-A8EB-333A819F1AF9}.png)
# Etiology
![200](00%20media/{3D90137A-1228-44BB-BDBE-65CEAB17840C}.png)
##  Degenerative calcification of normal tricuspid aortic valve
- Similar to atherosclerosis
- Similar to atherogenesis → myofibroblasts turn into osteoblasts, deposit calcium hydroxyapatite crystals
- Valve thickening and stiffening 
- Risk factors are dyslipidemia, smoking, hypertension
## Calcification of congenitally bicuspid
- 1-2% population, men more often
- This can possibly be attributed to bicuspid valves being subject to greater levels of mechanical stress because there is less surface area to share the impact of the valves closing against each other. In fact, calcified BAV comprises roughly 50% of aortic stenosis found in adults.
## Rheumatic aortic valve disease
- Usually accompanied by rheumatic involvement of mitral valve 
- Fibrosis of valve, fusion of commissures, calcified masses within aortic cusps 
## General pathophysiology
- Afterload ↑ 
- Obstruction develops gradually, LV can compensate with concentric hypertrophy, concentric remodeling, and eccentric hypertrophy (according to Hurst. According to Lilly, it's just concentric)
	- Reduces LV wall stress, but also reduces compliance of ventricle
	- Diastolic LV pressure ↑ , LA hypertrophies to fill stiffened LV 
- Diastolic dysfunction occurs early, but progresses to a restrictive filling pattern = a condition where the heart's main pumping chamber (the left ventricle) becomes stiff and unable to relax properly to fill with enough blood
- LVEF could ↓ → could also be associated with common comorbidity like CAD 
- Advanced AS
	- Angina 
		- Muscle mass of LV is increased → ↑ myocardial O2 demand 
		- Wall stress is increased because ↑ systolic ventricular pressure 
		- LV diastolic pressure ↑ means coronary perfusion gradient is reduced (shown below)
	- Exertional syncope
		- Cannot maintain CO during exercise because fixed orifice
		- Exercise leads to vasodilation, too
	- Heart failure
		- Progresses because of myocyte cell death and myocardial fibrosis 
			- Types of fibrosis: diffuse interstitial (occurs early, reversible), replacement fibrosis (occurs later, irreversible)
		- LV can develop contractile dysfunction, very high afterload, ↑ LVEDV and LVEDP
$$
coronary \; perfusion \; pressure = P_{aortic \; diastole} - P_{LVEDP}
$$
## Steps of pathophysiology
### Initiation phase
- Thickened, progressive calcification and increased stiffness and ↓ mobility 
- Some kind of mechanical stress and injury to endothelium
- Bicuspid is less efficient at dissipating mechanical forces 
- But statins do not work for treatment → apparently self-perpetuating after initiation 
### Propagation phase 
- Almost like bone formation, osteoblast type cells → more and more calcium deposition

## How to differentiate between hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and aortic stenosis
- Valsalva maneuver → decreased preload.
- In aortic stenosis + decreased preload → less blood being pushed across the stenosed valve. Murmur will become **softer**.
- In hypertrophic cardiomyopathy + decreased preload → less blood causes the thickened heart muscle and an abnormally moving mitral valve to come closer together, leading to a more severe obstruction of blood leaving the heart. Murmur becomes **harsher**.

# Clinical features
- Angina
- Syncope = narrowed valve prevents the heart from pumping enough blood to the body, especially during exertion
- Heart failure
- Physical examination
	- Coarse late-peaking systolic ejection murmur
	- Weakened and delayed upstroke of the carotid artery (pulsus parvus et tardus)
		- Narrowed valve restricts blood flow from the left ventricle to the aorta, leading to a pulse that is both weak (pulsus parvus) and delayed (pulsus tardus). This is often referred to as a "pulsus parvus et tardus" and is a key physical finding that can be felt on the carotid artery
		- The heart has to work harder to force blood through the constricted valve, resulting in a slower, weaker pulse wave that reaches its peak later than normal.
	- Carotid thrills = A "thrill" felt at the carotid artery is a palpable, vibrating sensation that can be caused by severe aortic stenosis. This vibration occurs because the turbulent blood flow through the narrowed aortic valve is transmitted up to the carotid arteries
	- Paradoxical splitting, which indicates severe obstruction and happens when the delayed closure of the aortic valve (A2) occurs after the pulmonic valve (P2) closes
	- Could have S4 = An S4 heart sound in aortic stenosis is caused by the left atrium forcefully contracting against a stiffened, hypertrophied left ventricle, forcing blood into a non-compliant chamber
	- Diminished S2 = A diminished or absent S2 heart sound indicates severe disease, as the leaflets of the aortic valve become immobile and cannot close properly
	- 
- Could have JVP elevation = sign of right-sided heart failure, which can develop as a consequence of the left-sided heart strain caused by the aortic stenosis. Aortic stenosis makes it harder for the left ventricle to pump blood, which can lead to left-sided heart failure and, eventually, increased pressure in the lungs and the right side of the heart, resulting in a high JVP
# Treatment 
- Percutaneous balloon valvuloplasty kind of useless in calcific AS 
- Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) = a minimally invasive heart procedure to replace a narrowed aortic valve (aortic stenosis) with a new one, delivered via a catheter
