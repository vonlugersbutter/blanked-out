# Definition
- Carcinoid tumors are neuroendocrine tumors found in various organs, most commonly the small intestine they are named for their slow growth
# Etiology
- Secrete serotonin (5-ht), which causes diarrhea (increased GI motility), flushing, and valvular lesions via fibroblast growth
# Epidemiology

# Clinical features
- Abdominal pain, flushing, diarrhea, and right-sided heart valve disease
- Symptoms only occur if the tumor has metastasized to the liver, because the liver and lungs normally inactivate serotonin
- Heart disease = deposits form on tricuspid and pulmonic valves (stenosis/regurgitation) left-sided lesions are rare because serotonin is inactivated in the lungs
	- See [Pulmonic Stenosis](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Valvular%20Diseases/Pulmonic%20Stenosis.md), [Pulmonic Regurgitation](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Valvular%20Diseases/Pulmonic%20Regurgitation.md), [Tricuspid Stenosis](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Valvular%20Diseases/Tricuspid%20Stenosis.md), [Tricuspid Regurgitation](08%20Cardiology/Cardiac%20Pathologies/Valvular%20Diseases/Tricuspid%20Regurgitation.md)
- Metabolic effects = carcinoid tumors consume up to 70% of the body's tryptophan (normal is ~1%) to produce serotonin
	- Can lead to a tryptophan deficiency, causing niacin (b3) deficiency and symptoms of pellagra
# Investigations
- Serotonin is metabolized into 5-HIAA, which is measured in a 24-hour urine sample
# Prognosis

# Treatment 
- Surgical excision, hepatic resection, and octreotide
- Octreotide = a somatostatin analog that binds to receptors on carcinoid tumors to inhibit the release of serotonin, catecholamines, and histamine, significantly improving flushing and diarrhea
