Once the [Pregnancy](Pregnancy.md) is identified, ideally following [Preconception Counseling](Preconception%20Counseling.md), a number of appointments are set up for prenatal care. Genetic screening could also be conducted. 
# Visits
- Maternal and fetal health status, estimate gestational age, and initiate a care plan
- Routine components include a complete history, physical examination (including pelvic exam), and various laboratory tests
- Psychosocial Screening = all women should be screened at least once per trimester for barriers to care, depression, substance use, and intimate-partner violence (IPV)
	- IPV prevalence is estimated between 4 and 8 percent and is associated with risks like preterm delivery and fetal-growth restriction
- Laboratory Tests = Standard initial tests include a CBC, blood type and Rh status, antibody screen, and screening for HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B, and rubella immunity
- Subsequent Visits = Routine visits occur every 4 weeks until 28 weeks, every 2 weeks until 36 weeks, and weekly thereafter
	- Fundal height (measured in cm from the symphysis pubis) correlates closely with gestational age between 20 and 34 weeks
	- Fetal heart sounds are typically detectable by 10 weeks using Doppler ultrasound and by 20 weeks with a standard stethoscope
# Nutrition and behavior
- Weight Gain = Recommendations are based on prepregnancy BMI
	- For example, women with a normal BMI (185–249) should gain 25–35 lb, while obese women (BMI ≥30) are limited to 11–20 lb
	- Excessive gain is linked to gestational hypertension and cesarean delivery
- Increased caloric intake
- Supplementation
	- Daily folic acid throughout the periconceptional period can prevent over half of neural-tube defects
	- Iron supplementation daily recommendation
- Substance Avoidance = Smoking increases risks for [Placenta](Placenta.md) previa, placental abruption, and preterm birth
- Alcohol is a potent [Teratogens](Teratogens.md) and can cause fetal alcohol spectrum disorders; total abstinence is recommended
- Most women can work until labor onset, though heavy lifting or prolonged standing may increase preterm birth risks
- Air travel is generally safe up to 36 weeks’ gestation in the absence of complications
- Pregnant women should consume fish weekly to obtain omega-3 fatty acids, but must avoid high-mercury species like shark, swordfish, and king mackerel
- Influenza and Tdap (preferably between 27–36 weeks) vaccines are recommended for all pregnant women; live vaccines, such as MMR (measles, mumps, rubella), are contraindicated during pregnancy
- Nausea and vomiting affect 75% of women and can often be managed with small meals, ginger, or vitamin B6 with doxylamine; backache is reported by nearly 70% of gravidas and is managed with analgesics, heat, and proper posture
# Maternal-fetal genetics   
## Diagnostics and screening
- Cytogenetic Testing
	- Karyotyping = Traditional method for assessing chromosome number and large structural changes
	- FISH = Rapidly identifies specific abnormalities using fluorescent probes on metaphase or interphase cells
	- CMA = 100 times more sensitive than karyotyping; uses CGH or SNP platforms to detect submicroscopic variations
- Molecular and sequencing tools
	- PCR = Used for rapid amplification and diagnosis of known genetic basis diseases
	- Whole Exome Sequencing (WES) = Analyzes only the coding regions (15% of the genome) to evaluate complex syndromes
	- Cell-free DNA (NIPS) = A screening test analyzing DNA fragments from apoptotic placental cells in maternal blood It is cleared within minutes and can be used for aneuploidy screening or Rh D genotyping
## Aneuploidy screening
- Cell-free DNA (cfDNA) Screening
	- Mechanism = Identifies DNA fragments derived primarily from apoptotic trophoblasts (placental cells), not the fetus itself
	- Timing = Can be performed anytime after 9 to 10 weeks’ gestation
	- Performance = Offers the highest sensitivity and specificity For Trisomy 21, the pooled sensitivity is 997%, with a combined false-positive rate of less than 02%
	- Limitations = "No-call" or indeterminate results occur in 2 to 4% of cases, often due to low fetal fraction (the proportion of placental cfDNA in maternal blood) Low fetal fraction is associated with early testing (<10 weeks), maternal obesity, and certain aneuploidies (T18 and T13)
    

3. Analyte-based Screening =
    

- First-Trimester Screen = Includes nuchal translucency (NT) measurement, human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), and pregnancy-associated plasma protein A (PAPP-A) between 11 and 14 weeks
    
- Second-Trimester (Quad) Screen = Measures maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein (MSAFP), hCG, unconjugated estriol, and dimeric inhibin alpha between 15 and 21 weeks
    
- Benefits = Can identify genetic abnormalities beyond the common trisomies (eg, Turner syndrome, T13)
    

B Ultrasound Markers

- Major Anomalies = Identification of structural defects (eg, cystic hygroma, cardiac defects) usually warrants diagnostic testing over screening due to high aneuploidy risk
    
- Minor Markers ("Soft Signs") = These are normal variants that increase the likelihood of aneuploidy (particularly T21) when present Examples include =
    

- Nuchal skinfold thickening (≥6 mm)
    
- Echogenic intracardiac focus
    
- Mild renal pelvis dilatation (pyelectasis)
    
- Echogenic bowel
    
- Absent or hypoplastic nasal bone
    

III Carrier Screening for Genetic Disorders

Ideally performed preconceptually to guide pregnancy planning

- Approaches = Ethnicity-based, panethnic (offered regardless of ethnicity), or expanded carrier screening (testing for dozens to hundreds of conditions)
    
- Key Conditions Screened =
    

- Cystic Fibrosis (CF) = Caused by mutations in the CFTR gene; carrier frequency is highest in Ashkenazi Jews and non-Hispanic whites (1 =25)
    
- Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA) = Recommended for all women considering pregnancy; carrier detection rates are 90–95% for most ethnicities
    
- Hemoglobinopathies = Includes sickle-cell anemia and thalassemias; screening via hemoglobin electrophoresis is offered to high-risk ethnicities (African, Mediterranean, Southeast Asian)
    
- Ashkenazi Jewish Conditions = Screenings for Tay-Sachs, Canavan disease, and familial dysautonomia are standard
    

IV Prenatal Diagnostic Tests

These procedures provide a definitive diagnosis rather than a risk estimate

A Invasive Procedures

1. Amniocentesis =
    

- Procedure = Transabdominal withdrawal of amnionic fluid under ultrasound guidance
    
- Timing = Typically 15 to 20 weeks’ gestation
    
- Risk = Procedure-related loss rate is estimated at 01 to 03% (1 in 500)
    

3. Chorionic Villus Sampling (CVS) =
    

- Procedure = Biopsy of placental villi performed transcervically or transabdominally
    
- Timing = 10 to 13 weeks’ gestation
    
- Benefit = Provides earlier results than amniocentesis
    
- Risk = Procedure-related loss rate is comparable to amniocentesis (01 to 03%)
    
- Limitation = Up to 2% of specimens show chromosomal mosaicism, which usually represents confined placental mosaicism rather than fetal aneuploidy
    

5. Fetal Blood Sampling (Cordocentesis) =
    

- Procedure = Withdrawal of blood from the umbilical vein
    
- Indication = Primarily used for assessing/treating fetal anemia; rarely used for genetic diagnosis today
    
- Risk = Higher procedure-related loss rate of approximately 1%
    

B Laboratory Analysis

- Chromosomal Microarray Analysis (CMA) = Recommended when structural abnormalities are found; it can detect microdeletions/microduplications that karyotyping misses
    
- Karyotyping = Traditional method to identify aneuploidy, polyploidy, and balanced chromosomal rearrangements
    
- Fluorescence in situ Hybridization (FISH) = Used for rapid identification of specific chromosomes (13, 18, 21, X, Y)
    

C Preimplantation Genetic Tests (PGT)

Performed on embryos during IVF prior to transfer

- PGT-A = Screens for aneuploidy
    
- PGT-M = Tests for specific monogenic (single-gene) disorders
    
- PGT-SR = Tests for known parental structural chromosomal rearrangements
