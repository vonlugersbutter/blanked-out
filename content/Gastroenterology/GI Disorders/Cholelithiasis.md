# Definition
- Bile in the gallbladder consists of bilirubin, bile salts, and cholesterol an upset in the balance of these components causes precipitation and stone formation
# Etiology
- Cholesterol gallstones = these are typically radiolucent (not visible on x-ray) and are most common in 40-year-old females
	- Risk factors include estrogen (which increases cholesterol synthesis), obesity, rapid weight loss, and pregnancy
- Bile salt risk factors = a loss of bile salts—which are normally 95% recycled via the terminal ileum—leads to stones this can be caused by cirrhosis (decreased synthesis), Crohn’s disease (impaired resorption in the ileum, see [Inflammatory Bowel Disease](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Inflammatory%20Bowel%20Disease.md)), cystic fibrosis, or medications like clofibrate and bile acid resins
- Pigment stones (bilirubin stones) = composed of calcium bilirubinate, these are radiopaque (visible on x-ray) and can be black or brown they result from a rise in unconjugated bilirubin, often due to extravascular hemolysis, cirrhosis, or recurrent biliary tree infections where bacteria produce glucuronidases

# Clinical features
- Biliary colic = episodic RUQ pain, often after fatty meals, caused by the gallbladder contracting against a stone in the outlet
- Acute cholecystitis = gallbladder inflammation caused by a stone obstructing the cystic duct features include RUQ pain, fever, and a positive murphy’s sign
- Choledocolithiasis = a stone in the common bile duct causing biliary obstruction, jaundice, and elevated alkaline phosphatase
- Ascending cholangitis = a medical emergency where a stone blocks bile flow, allowing GI bacteria to ascend the biliary tree
	- Presents as Charcot’s triad (fever, pain, jaundice) or Reynolds pentad (adding confusion and hypotension, indicating sepsis) treatment involves antibiotics and biliary drainage, often via ERCP
- Gallstone ileus = a massive stone erodes through the gallbladder wall into the small intestine, creating a fistula and causing a bowel obstruction at the ileocecal valve a key imaging finding is air in the biliary tree
- Gallbladder carcinoma = a rare adenocarcinoma resulting from chronic inflammation (eg, porcelain gallbladder) or chronic salmonella typhi infection (carrier state)
