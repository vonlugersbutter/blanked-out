# Definition
- Insufficient cortisol production 
![](00%20media/{937094BE-570B-4521-8275-2CCFC4902A1E}.png)
# Etiology
![](00%20media/{620C6DA5-6B34-487C-9FFB-884A34ECB530}.png)
- Primary (Addison’s disease) = failure of the adrenal gland itself low cortisol and aldosterone with high ACTH, leading to skin hyperpigmentation (via MSH)
	- Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome = acute adrenal insufficiency caused by hemorrhage, often associated with meningococcemia
		- Rapid onset of septic shock, widespread purpura (purple skin rash), and disseminated intravascular coagulation
	- Common causes include autoimmune adrenalitis (loss of cortex), infections, and tumor metastases from the lung 
- Secondary = failure of pituitary ACTH release only cortisol is low; aldosterone is usually unaffected
	- Most commonly caused by glucocorticoid therapy, where the sudden discontinuation causes hypoadrenalism 
	- This is why people wean off of steroids 
	- Patients on chronic steroids, when they have infection, trauma, or surgery, are given a high dose → reduce risk of adrenal crisis 
	- No skin findings, no hyperkalemia 
- ADH is also affected, so we will see some forms of central forms of [Diabetes](Endocrinology/Endocrine%20Pathologies/Diabetes/Diabetes.md)
	- Cortisol normally inhibits ADH → ↑ ADH 
# Epidemiology
- Addison's, autoimmune is main cause in the west, while TB is the main cause worldwide 
# Clinical features
- Fatigue, weight loss, hypotension, hypoglycemia, and hyperkalemia (in primary only)
- ACTH is high in primary adrenal insufficiency → skin hyperpigmentation 
- Secondary (central) = ACTH deficiency (pituitary disease or exogenous steroid suppression) → no hyperpigmentation or mineralocorticoid deficiency occurs
# Investigations
![400](00%20media/{0ACC3DD5-AB0B-4CBB-84B0-94923F577984}.png)
- Low cortisol, morning ACTH high would mean primary 
![](00%20media/{C7EDA512-989D-48D9-B469-49E7071318F2}.png)
- Using cosyntropin stim test → exogenous ACTH administered → should see ↑ cortisol
	- Failure to rise  = primary disorder
	- Normal rise = secondary disorder 
# Prognosis
- Adrenal crisis = an acute, life-threatening loss of function resulting in shock
	- Sudden discontinuation of corticosteroids
	- Or undiagnosed, and person experiences some kind of huge stress 
	- Can also mimic an acute abdomen 
	- Cortisol → ↓ ADH → loss of cortisol will ↑ ADH → free water retention and dilutional **hyponatremia**
	- 5S's → Salt (0.9% saline), Sugar (50% dextrose), Steroids (100 mg hydrocortisone IV once, then 200 mg over 24 hours), Support (normal saline to correct hypotension and electrolyte abnormalities), and Search (for the underlying disorder)
- In secondary and tertiary, if they don't get stimulated, the adrenal gland will eventually become atrophied and it will become primary 
# Treatment 
- Replacement involves daily hydrocortisone and, in primary failure, fludrocortisone
