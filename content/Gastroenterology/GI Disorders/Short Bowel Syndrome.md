# Definition
- Usually from intestinal resection to treat things like Crohn's disease in [Inflammatory Bowel Disease](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Inflammatory%20Bowel%20Disease.md), herniations, ischemias, [Malrotation](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Malrotation.md) 
- <200 cm of remaining small bowel
# Etiology
- Multiple surgical resections 
# Epidemiology

# Clinical features
- Diarrhea, weight loss, malabsorption of fat, protein, carbs 
- See crypt cell proliferation, epithelial hyperplasia 
- Increased gastric secretion, followed by enhanced function
- Specific nutrient deficiencies like B12 and bile acid reabsorption cannot get back 
- Resection of the colon will also result in water and electrolyte loss 
- Choleretic diarrhea
	- Liver will produce more bile if you can't absorb it, but it will end up in the colon and cause secretory diarrhea
	- You use cholestyramine or bile acid sequestrants, which are resins that avoid irritating the colon 
	- In a large resection, though, if there is not enough bile left to emulsify fats, you get steatorrhea and malabsorption of ADEK vitamins 
		- In that case, don't give sequestrants 
- In the case where he has had a end-jejunostomy (stoma, mouth to outside of body)
	- Dehydration, sodium depletion
	- No fermentation

| Phase       | Duration  | Key Features                                                                                               | Management Focus                                                  |
| ----------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Acute       | 3-4 weeks | Gastric hypersecretion, massive losses (dehydration, AKI, electrolytes) due to absent ileal brake hormones | Hospital monitoring, IV fluids/electrolytes                       |
| Adaptation  | 1-2 years | Villus hypertrophy, slowed transit, hyperphagia; needs luminal nutrients + hormones (ileum/colon)          | Enteral nutrition to drive adaptation                             |
| Maintenance | Lifelong  | Stable residual function                                                                                   | Special diets, supplements, drugs (e.g., loperamide, teduglutide) |
# Investigations

# Treatment 
- Control diarrhea → loperamide
- Acid blocking medications to treat gastric hypersecretion, like PPIs, or histamine 2 antagonists 
- Teduglutide = GLP-2 analog that enhances crypt cell proliferation and villous hyperplasia, increases nutrient, fluid, electrolyte absorption
- Need luminal nutrients, so even with TPN is okay 
- Oral rehydration solution, and do not drink too much plain water because it would cause osmotic diarrhea eek and electrolyte depletion 
# Prognosis
- Vitamin D deficiency because of limited fat absorption, osteoporosis, assess bone density using DEXA scans 
- Renal calcium oxalate stones because fat binds to the calcium → [Nephrolithiasis](Nephrology/Renal%20Disorders/Nephrolithiasis.md)
