# Definition
Acute inflammation of the pancreas, characterized by liquefactive necrosis and hemorrhage.
# Etiology
- Common causes are gallstones and alcohol
- Full list is I GET PP SMASHED (I love the world of educational medical mnemonics)
	- Idiopathic
	- Gallstones
	- Ethanol
	- Trauma
	- Posterior duodenal ulcer rupture
	- Pancreas divisum
	- Steroids
	- Mumps
	- Autoimmune disease (autoimmune pancreatitis = a rare condition involving IgG4 positive plasma cells that responds to steroids)
	- Scorpion sting
	- Hypercalcemia or hypertriglyceridemia/hyperchylomicronemia 
	- ERCP
	- Drugs (sulfa drugs, NRTIs, protease inhibitors,)
- The condition occurs when the flow of enzymes is blocked while synthesis is ongoing
- Large amounts of trypsin are activated, which then triggers a cascade activating other enzymes such as phospholipase, chymotrypsin, and elastase, leading to the "auto-digestion" of the pancreas
	- The positive feedback cycle of trypsin being able to activate itself from trypsinogen is important

# Clinical features
- Epigastric pain that typically radiates to the back, accompanied by nausea and vomiting
- Rare physical examination findings include Cullen’s Sign (periumbilical hemorrhage) and Grey Turner’s Sign (flank hemorrhage), which result from the spread of necrosis and blood due to enzyme-induced damage
# Investigations
- Diagnosis requires at least two of the following three criteria = epigastric pain, elevated amylase or lipase (more than three times the normal limit), and abnormal pancreatic imaging (typically a CT scan)
- Laboratory tests = lipase is more specific for pancreatic damage than amylase liver function tests may show a cholestatic picture (elevated alk phos and conjugated bilirubin) if gallstones are the cause leukocytosis (elevated WBC) is also common
- Imaging = ultrasound may reveal gallstones or bile duct dilatation, while CT scans can show pancreatic edema or necrosis
- Ranson’s criteria, which evaluates factors at admission
	- Age >55, WBC >16,000, glucose >200, LDH >350, AST >250) and at 48 hours (Hct drop >10%, BUN increase >5, calcium <8, po2 <60)
- Could also use APACHE II criteria, or revised Atlanta criteria 
- SIRS (systemic inflammatory response syndrome) in pancreatitis indicates severe disease 
# Prognosis
- Pseudocyst = a walled-off collection of fluid (without epithelium) that takes about 4 weeks to "mature" it can rupture, leading to peritonitis, or become infected
- Pancreatic abscess = an infection of a pseudocyst, typically appearing 10 days into the illness, often caused by intestinal bacteria like e coli
- [ARDS](Respirology/Respiratory%20Pathologies/ARDS.md) = damage to the capillary endothelium allows fluid to pour into the lungs; it resembles pulmonary edema but with a normal PCWP (pulmonary capillary wedge pressure)
- Fat necrosis = enzymes release fatty acids that bind calcium, a process called saponification, low calcium is a poor prognostic indicator
- Multi-organ failure = severe cases may lead to DIC, renal failure, and the need for ICU support
- Repeated episodes causes [Chronic Pancreatitis](Gastroenterology/GI%20Disorders/Chronic%20Pancreatitis.md)
# Treatment 
- Focuses on NPO to rest the pancreas, aggressive IV fluids to maintain blood pressure and renal perfusion, and pain control
